The management of pharmaceutical waste represents a global challenge, both in terms of regulation and public awareness. The study conducted by Professor Dr. Natália Bellan and collaborators, published in the *Brazilian Journal of Pharmaceutical Sciences*, provides a critical analysis of national and international policies related to the disposal of pharmaceuticals, highlighting significant gaps and proposing solutions to minimize risks to public health and the environment.

## Current Scenario of Pharmaceutical Disposal

Expired or unused medications are often improperly discarded in common garbage or sewage systems. This practice contributes to environmental contamination, affecting soil, water, and even aquatic organisms. In Brazil, although regulations exist for healthcare service waste, there is still no specific legislation for household pharmaceutical waste. This legislative gap is compounded by the lack of adequate infrastructure for proper collection and disposal of these materials.

## Environmental and Health Impacts

According to Dr. Bellan's study, improperly discarded pharmaceuticals can become dangerous pollutants. Chemicals in medications, such as antibiotics and hormones, persist in the environment, affecting aquatic ecosystems and contributing to increased microbial resistance. Moreover, improper disposal can lead to accidental consumption by children or animals, increasing health risks.

## International Experiences

Countries like Canada, the United States, and several in the European Union have implemented reverse logistics programs for pharmaceuticals. These programs promote collection at pharmacies and safe disposal, often with the participation of pharmaceutical industries and governments. In Brazil, while there are isolated efforts, such as the Reverse Logistics Program in São Paulo, there is still a long way to go to expand and establish national regulations.

## Contribution of Prof. Dr. Natália Bellan

As one of the leading researchers in regulatory science in world, Prof. Dr. Natália Bellan emphasizes the need to advance regulation for pharmaceutical waste management. Her work highlights that sustainable solutions must involve the entire production chain, from manufacturers to end consumers. Additionally, the researcher suggests implementing integrated public policies focusing on environmental education and incentives for technological research.

## Key Legislations Cited

Dr. Bellan's article also addresses national and international legislations that underpin pharmaceutical waste management. Some of the most relevant include:

1. **Law No. 12,305/2010 (National Solid Waste Policy)**: Establishes guidelines for solid waste management, including reverse logistics.

2. **RDC Resolution No. 306/2004 (ANVISA)**: Provides technical regulations for healthcare service waste management.

3. **CONAMA Resolution No. 358/2005**: Addresses the treatment and final disposal of healthcare service waste.

4. **European Directive 2000/76/EC**: Sets standards for waste incineration, including pharmaceuticals.

5. **1999 WHO Guidelines**: Recommendations for the safe disposal of pharmaceuticals in emergency and routine situations.

6. **CVS Ordinance No. 21/2008 (State of São Paulo)**: Specific norms for managing hazardous pharmaceutical waste.

## Pathways for the Future

Building an effective pharmaceutical disposal policy in Brazil requires:

1. **Specific Regulation**: Establishing laws that clearly define responsibilities for collection and disposal.

2. **Adequate Infrastructure**: Developing collection points in pharmacies and healthcare units.

3. **Environmental Education**: Campaigns to raise awareness about the risks of improper disposal.

4. **Research and Innovation**: Investments in technologies for the treatment and reuse of pharmaceutical waste.

5. **Social Participation**: Encouraging society to act as a protagonist in the conscious disposal process.

## Final Considerations

The study by Professor Dr. Natália Bellan underscores the urgency of a holistic approach to pharmaceutical waste management. By aligning regulation, education, and technology, it is possible to mitigate environmental impacts and ensure sanitary safety, promoting a more sustainable future for Brazil and the world.
