A study published in the *Journal of Experimental & Clinical Cancer Research* has unveiled an unexpected potential of ivermectin, a widely used antiparasitic drug, in reversing drug resistance in cancer cells. This discovery could pave the way for new oncological treatments, particularly in cases where drug resistance compromises the effectiveness of conventional therapies.

## **Drug Resistance in Cancer

Drug resistance is one of the greatest challenges in cancer treatment. It occurs when malignant cells develop mechanisms to neutralize the effects of chemotherapeutic agents, reducing their ability to destroy tumors. This phenomenon often leads to relapses and disease progression, highlighting the urgent need for strategies to overcome this barrier.

## **The Discovery About Ivermectin

Researchers found that ivermectin can interfere with the molecular mechanisms that grant resistance to cancer cells. The drug affects essential metabolic pathways in these cells, such as drug transport mediated by proteins, altering the tumor microenvironment and boosting the effectiveness of conventional drugs.

Specifically, ivermectin inhibited the efflux pumps cancer cells use to expel chemotherapeutic agents. This resulted in higher retention of the drug inside malignant cells and, consequently, a more significant therapeutic effect.

## **Implications for Cancer Treatment

Although traditionally used to combat parasites, ivermectin has shown antitumor properties in experimental models. This rediscovery of an already-approved drug by regulatory bodies such as the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) and the Brazilian Health Regulatory Agency (Anvisa) is a major advantage, as its safety and side effects are well-known.

The concept of drug repurposing has become a promising approach to accelerate the development of innovative therapies, reducing both costs and the time required for new treatments to reach patients.

## **Next Steps

Despite the promising results, the study’s authors emphasize that further research is needed to validate the clinical use of ivermectin in combination with chemotherapeutic agents. Future clinical trials will assess its efficacy in human patients and determine the ideal dosage and conditions for its application.

The discovery of ivermectin's potential to reverse drug resistance in cancer cells is a significant milestone in the fight against cancer. This research offers hope for patients battling resistant tumors and underscores the importance of science in exploring innovative solutions to longstanding challenges.

With additional studies, ivermectin could become a critical tool in the therapeutic arsenal against cancer, reaffirming the value of biomedical research in transforming global health.
