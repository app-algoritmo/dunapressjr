## ANVISA’s Role in Global Pharma: Insights from 20 Years of Regulation

In a recent podcast titled *Regulation and Innovation in the International Sphere*, hosted by Dr. Natalia Bellan from Norway, Roberto dos Reis, a seasoned ANVISA (Brazil’s National Health Surveillance Agency) official with 20 years of experience, sheds light on Brazil’s regulatory framework for pharmaceutical inspections and Good Manufacturing Practices (GMP). Broadcast internationally, the discussion highlights Roberto’s expertise in inspecting domestic and global drug manufacturing facilities, emphasizing ANVISA’s role in safeguarding public health amid a complex global supply chain. This article explores Roberto’s insights into ANVISA’s operations, the harmonization of international standards, and advice for pharmaceutical professionals, underscoring the agency’s critical contribution to Brazil’s healthcare system.

## ANVISA’s Broad Mandate and Roberto’s Journey

Roberto, who joined ANVISA in March 2005 as part of its first cohort, brings a rich background, having worked as a technical manager in a pharmacy and in quality control at a national pharmaceutical company. For over 18 years, he has focused on GMP inspections of synthetic and biological active pharmaceutical ingredients (APIs), with brief stints in port/airport surveillance and administrative roles. His experience underscores ANVISA’s expansive mandate under Law 9782/1999, which grants it exclusive federal authority over international inspections, operational authorizations, and drug registrations. This framework, Roberto explains, ensures rigorous oversight of Brazil’s over 500 drug manufacturers, balancing national and international responsibilities.

## Decentralization and Collaborative Inspections

Since joining the Pharmaceutical Inspection Co-operation Scheme (PIC/S) in January 2021, ANVISA has decentralized routine GMP inspections to seven states—Rio Grande do Sul, Paraná, Santa Catarina, Minas Gerais, São Paulo, Rio de Janeiro, and Goiás—while retaining oversight. Roberto notes that state and municipal inspectors conduct these inspections, often with ANVISA’s support, reflecting the National Health Surveillance System’s collaborative model. For operational authorizations, ANVISA relies on state and municipal inspection reports, issued under Law 6360, to grant licenses, ensuring a unified yet decentralized approach. This structure, he argues, optimizes resource use across Brazil’s vast territory.

## Training and International Harmonization

Roberto highlights the PROG (National Development Program), which trains inspectors across federal, state, and municipal levels in specialized modules—e.g., sterile products, radiopharmaceuticals—aligned with global standards like PIC/S and ICH. This training, updated with international regulatory shifts, ensures a harmonized understanding of GMP expectations. His experience with PIC/S, which includes 56 authorities, has reduced surprises during inspections by aligning Brazil’s norms with global practices. Roberto sees this harmonization as a resource-efficient strategy, though he stresses ANVISA’s autonomy in final decisions based on Brazil’s risk matrix.

## Handling Combination Products and Innovative Medicines

Addressing combination products (e.g., pre-filled syringes combining drugs and medical devices), Roberto explains that ANVISA classifies them as drugs, focusing inspections on GMP for the most complex component. Unlike Europe or the U.S., where agencies like the FDA certify by product, ANVISA certifies by production line, a distinction driven by resource constraints. For innovative medicines, prioritization is triggered by health ministry needs (e.g., PNI or RENAM), with risk assessments guiding inspection decisions. This approach, Roberto notes, balances innovation with safety, leveraging international data to streamline processes.

## International Experiences and Cultural Insights

With 18 years of inspecting facilities in Argentina, Colombia, Cuba, Mexico, the U.S., Canada, China, India, Japan, Turkey, Germany, Spain, Scotland, Ireland, Finland, Sweden, Norway, Denmark, France, Latvia, Croatia, and Australia, Roberto highlights cultural and organizational maturity differences. These global exposures enhance his ability to assess GMP compliance, revealing universal challenges like transport qualification, mitigated by Resolution 430. He emphasizes that Brazil’s market, with nearly 220 million consumers, is no longer a “black box,” thanks to international engagement, fostering mutual respect and growth.

## Post-Inspection Processes and Pharmacovigilance

Post-inspection, companies receive a report with non-conformities, having 10 days to submit an action plan. Roberto outlines outcomes: satisfactory ratings allow certification with follow-up, while critical issues may require re-inspection or product recalls under ANVISA’s authority. The Rapid Alert system, rooted in WHO guidelines, enables global information sharing among PIC/S members, ensuring market safety. Pharmacovigilance, via the Notivisa system, complements inspections by monitoring adverse events, reinforcing a proactive risk management approach.

## Advice for Pharmaceutical Professionals

As a lecturer at recognized institutions (per Brazil’s public servant ethics code), Roberto advises aspiring professionals to pursue relevant fields like pharmacy, chemistry, biology, or engineering, and to never stop learning amid regulatory and technological shifts. For industry veterans, he recommends staying updated, mastering soft skills like communication and leadership, and embracing a holistic view of quality’s societal impact. He views his role as “building medication quality” to preserve lives, urging a partnership between regulators and industry for public health.

## Sanitary Regulation vs. Bureaucracy

Roberto and Bellan distinguish sanitary regulation from bureaucracy, arguing it drives prosperity when well-applied. Missteps, like the Thalidomida case, highlight state and industry accountability under Brazil’s Constitution, where health is a state duty. They advocate for regulatory intelligence to optimize processes, countering perceptions of excessive red tape with a focus on safety and economic benefit.

Roberto’s insights reveal ANVISA’s pivotal role in ensuring pharmaceutical quality through a robust, evolving regulatory framework. From decentralized inspections to international harmonization and innovative product oversight, the agency balances technical rigor with societal impact. His advice to professionals underscores a lifelong commitment to learning and collaboration, positioning Brazil as a key player in global health security. As regulations adapt to new challenges, ANVISA’s mission remains clear: safeguarding lives through quality and trust.
