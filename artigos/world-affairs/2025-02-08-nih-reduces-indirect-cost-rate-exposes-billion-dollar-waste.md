A **historic decision by the National Institutes of Health (NIH)** has just redefined how the U.S. government funds scientific research. After years of criticism regarding the misuse of funds intended for science, the NIH has drastically cut the maximum **indirect cost rate** that institutions can charge the government, **capping it at 15%**. This change, which will **save over $4 billion per year**, exposes a system where some of the world’s wealthiest universities were **diverting over 60% of public research funding for “administrative costs.”**

## **The Great Indirect Cost Scandal

In 2023, the NIH distributed **$35 billion** for medical and scientific research. However, **$9 billion** of that amount was not used directly for research but was instead consumed by institutional expenses labeled as **“indirect costs”**—a term that includes everything from building maintenance to administrative salaries.

While some overhead costs are necessary, they reached **absurd levels** at certain universities, many of which have **billion-dollar endowments** yet still charged the NIH over **60%** in "indirect costs." Among the biggest beneficiaries of this system were:

- **Harvard** – **69% indirect cost rate**, with an endowment of **$53.2 billion**

- **Yale** – **67.5%**, with an endowment of **$41.4 billion**

- **Johns Hopkins** – **63.7%**, with an endowment of **$13.1 billion**

This means that in some cases, if a researcher received **$1 million** from the NIH to conduct a study, **$690,000 stayed with the university for “operational expenses”**, while only **$310,000 actually went toward the science.**

## **The Party Is Over: 15% Cap on Indirect Costs

The new rule establishes that no institution can charge the NIH more than **15%** in indirect costs. This rate is still **higher** than what some major private foundations allow—for example, the **Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation** already caps indirect costs at **10%**—but it represents a **drastic reduction from previous NIH practices**.

The decision has been welcomed by those who have long denounced the **abuse of research funding**. One of the most vocal critics was **Elon Musk**, who commented on X (formerly Twitter):

**"Can you believe that universities with billions in endowments were siphoning off 60% of research award money for ‘overhead’? What a ripoff!"**

## **Impact: Who Wins and Who Loses?

## Winners:

- **Taxpayers**, who will see more money directly invested in science.

- **Independent researchers**, who will have greater access to real funding for their projects.

- **Institutions that already operate with lower indirect costs** and do not rely on the NIH to inflate their administrative budgets.

## Losers:

- **Elite universities**, which previously used NIH grants as an **indirect revenue source** to cover their overhead costs.

- **Bloated bureaucracies within universities**, which will now need to be restructured since they can no longer depend on NIH funds to sustain their high operational costs.

## **A New Era of Transparency and Efficiency in Research Funding

With this move, the NIH is taking a crucial step toward ensuring that taxpayer money is **truly used for advancing science and medicine, rather than sustaining inefficient bureaucracies.** The change also pressures other agencies and institutions to **reassess their own funding models**, ensuring that resources reach **researchers and innovations that genuinely make a difference.**

The question now is: **Will other government agencies follow this example?**
