## Is This Normal in an Open Society? Glenn Diesen’s Critique and Candidacy Announcement

Professor Glenn Diesen, a prominent voice in Norway’s foreign policy debate, has recently announced his candidacy as the top candidate for Akershus in the 2025 parliamentary election for the newly formed Peace and Justice Party (FOR). His message is clear: Norway must return to its role as a peace nation, and freedom of speech must be restored in a time marked by what he describes as war propaganda and censorship. Diesen’s statements and candidacy have already caused a stir, as he sharply criticizes Norway’s foreign policy, NATO’s role in the Ukraine conflict, and the lack of open debate in Norwegian society.

## A Voice for Peace in a Polarized Time

Diesen claims he stands with the majority of Ukrainians, who, according to him, now desire immediate negotiations to end the war in Ukraine. He criticizes what he perceives as a one-sided Norwegian consensus, where politicians and media portray arms deliveries as the path to peace while dismissing diplomacy and negotiations. “It’s not allowed to discuss NATO’s direct involvement in this war or to argue for diplomacy and negotiations without being accused of spreading Russian propaganda,” Diesen says. He believes that criticism of war policy is systematically smeared as treason, which he considers abnormal in an open society.

Diesen highlights examples to support his claim that peace initiatives are being undermined. He points to Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky’s 2019 election victory, where 73% of Ukrainians supported his platform to implement the Minsk Agreement and negotiate with Donbas. Yet, Diesen argues, Zelensky was pressured to abandon this peace plan by nationalist forces and a Norwegian-funded NGO that labeled the peace mandate as “capitulation” to Russia. Diesen asks: How can this be justified in a democracy?

## Criticism of Norwegian Tax Money and NGOs

Another central point in Diesen’s critique is the use of Norwegian tax money to fund organizations that he believes promote war and regime change under the guise of human rights. He specifically points to the Helsinki Committee, which receives tens of millions of kroner from the Norwegian state, and the National Endowment for Democracy (NED), an American organization with documented ties to the CIA. Diesen claims these actors smear and cancel critics of Norway’s war policy, undermining freedom of speech.

He also references events from 2014, when Ukraine’s democratically elected president was ousted in a regime change that Diesen argues was neither supported by the constitution nor the majority of Ukrainians. Nevertheless, this was portrayed as a step toward democracy and NATO membership, despite only 20% of Ukrainians at the time supporting such membership. Diesen asks: “Is it normal that all criticism of NATO’s involvement in the war since 2014 is presented as support for Russia’s invasion?”

## A Threat to Freedom of Speech?

Diesen describes a Norwegian public sphere where consensus is shaped by what he calls “extreme war propaganda and vilification.” He draws a parallel to Pavlov’s dog, where critics of war policy are immediately labeled as “Putinists,” “Putin’s friends,” or “Kremlin mouthpieces.” He points to the media’s reaction to his party’s political advertisement, where speculations about Russian funding were raised without evidence. “Is this normal discourse or an indication of mass psychosis?” he asks rhetorically.

He also questions the lack of opposition in the Norwegian Parliament to NATO’s Ukraine policy and the uniformity in Norwegian media. “Is it normal that all our media say exactly the same thing?” he asks, citing a VG headline as an example of how war criticism is framed as disloyalty.

## A Vision for a New Norwegian Foreign Policy

Through his candidacy for the Peace and Justice Party, Diesen seeks to challenge what he sees as a dangerous development. He argues that Norway’s foreign policy must adapt to a new geopolitical reality, where U.S. dominance is waning, and Europe is deprioritized. “War will come to Norway unless we adapt to this new reality,” he warns. His goal is to restore Norway as a peace nation that prioritizes diplomacy and dialogue over military escalation.

## A Call for Debate

Glenn Diesen’s candidacy and critique of Norway’s foreign policy raise fundamental questions about freedom of speech, democracy, and Norway’s role in the world. Whether one agrees or disagrees with his analyses, his call for open debate is hard to dismiss. In a society where all voices should be heard, there should be room to discuss both war and peace without fear of smearing or censorship. The question Diesen poses—“Is this normal in an open society?”—deserves to be taken seriously.
