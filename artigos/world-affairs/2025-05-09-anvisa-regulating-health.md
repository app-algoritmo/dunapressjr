## Brazil’s Anvisa: Henrique Mansano on Regulating Health and Fostering Innovation

In a recent episode of the podcast *Regulation & Inovation*, hosted by [Prof. Dr. Natalia Bellan](https://nataliabellan.org) from Norway, **Henrique Mansano**, a pharmacist and Anvisa (Brazil’s National Health Surveillance Agency) official, provides an in-depth look at the agency’s role in regulating health products and its impact on Brazil’s pharmaceutical industry and public health. Recorded on May 8, 2025, the interview highlights Anvisa’s rigorous regulatory processes, the pharmacist’s critical role in Brazil’s health system, and the importance of fostering industrial growth to enhance health security. Mansano also discusses future challenges like regulating AI-driven medical devices and the need for ongoing professional development in the field.

## Anvisa’s Role and the Pharmacist’s Impact in Brazil

Mansano, who joined Anvisa in 2014 after a career inspired by an internship in Goiás, emphasizes the agency’s broad mandate under its founding law, Law 9782 of 1999. Anvisa regulates all products and services impacting public health, including medicines, medical devices, cosmetics, sanitizing products, and processed foods, but not in natura foods, which fall under the Ministry of Agriculture. This extensive scope distinguishes Anvisa from many international counterparts, as it oversees a wide range of health-related products, ensuring safety and efficacy across diverse sectors.

The pharmacist’s role in Brazil, Mansano notes, is uniquely versatile due to the country’s generalist training model, a shift from earlier specialized tracks like industrial pharmacy or clinical analysis. Pharmacists operate across settings—from community pharmacies (drogarias) and compounding pharmacies (farmácias de manipulação) to industrial roles—acting as responsible technical officers (RTs) mandated by law for medicine-related activities. This contrasts with some countries where pharmacists are restricted to dispensing roles. At Anvisa, pharmacists are pivotal in evaluating product registrations, conducting inspections, and ensuring compliance, leveraging their technical expertise to protect public health in a country with vast geographical and climatic diversity.

## Anvisa’s Regulatory Framework: Transparency and Rigor

Mansano details Anvisa’s meticulous regulatory process, governed by Resolutions of the Collegiate Directorate (RDCs). These norms are developed through a robust, transparent process involving Regulatory Impact Analysis (RIA) to assess a norm’s potential effects, followed by public consultations allowing societal input from companies, patients, and the Ministry of Health. Technical teams then refine the draft based on contributions, ensuring relevance and scientific grounding. The final RDC is approved by Anvisa’s board—comprising the president and four directors—based on technical arguments, with some norms subject to post-implementation Regulatory Outcome Evaluation to verify their effectiveness.

This process, Mansano underscores, provides legal certainty for investors, as norms are not arbitrarily changed but rooted in scientific and legal rigor. Anvisa’s transparency is notable: all regulatory acts, including directors’ votes, are publicly accessible on its website, fostering trust among stakeholders. This framework supports Brazil’s health security by managing sanitary risks, a core objective of all Anvisa regulations.

## Pharmacovigilance and Post-Market Surveillance

A practical example of Anvisa’s impact, Mansano explains, is its pharmacovigilance program, which monitors medicines post-market to ensure they maintain the benefit-risk balance established during clinical trials. Unlike controlled trials, real-world use involves diverse patient profiles, including those with polypharmacy (e.g., managing hypertension and diabetes alongside new medications). This monitoring extends to medical devices (technovigilance) and cosmetics (cosmetovigilance), ensuring ongoing safety across all regulated products. Such surveillance is critical in a continental country like Brazil, where climate variations—temperatures reaching 40°C—require specific stability studies for imported products to maintain quality.

## Fostering Industrial Growth in Brazil

Mansano highlights why Brazil is an attractive destination for pharmaceutical industries, beyond mere importation. First, Anvisa’s regulatory framework offers legal certainty through its rigorous, transparent norm-setting process, aligned with international standards via forums like the International Council for Harmonisation of Technical Requirements for Pharmaceuticals for Human Use (ICH) and the International Medical Device Regulators Forum (IMDRF). This convergence ensures Brazil adheres to global best practices, reducing uncertainty for investors.

Second, industrial investment enhances health security by reducing reliance on imports, a vulnerability exposed during the COVID-19 pandemic when supply chain disruptions led to shortages of active pharmaceutical ingredients (APIs). The Ministry of Health’s Complexo Econômico Industrial da Saúde (CEIS) policy incentivizes re-industrialization, aiming to bolster Brazil’s domestic production capacity. This not only mitigates risks of desabastecimento (shortages) but also generates significant employment, as the health sector is a major global employer.

## Innovation and Future Challenges

Anvisa supports innovation through norms like RDC 204/2017, which prioritizes expedited analysis for innovative products, such as medicines with APIs not yet manufactured in Brazil. This encourages the introduction of novel therapies, enhancing patient access. Looking ahead, Mansano identifies artificial intelligence (AI) and software as medical devices (SaMD) as emerging regulatory frontiers. AI-driven devices, like those monitoring patients in real-time, generate vast data, raising questions about storage, usage, and privacy. Anvisa is poised to adapt its frameworks to address these challenges, ensuring safety while fostering technological advancement.

## Professional Development and Advice for Aspiring Pharmacists

Reflecting on his career, Mansano credits his growth to continuous learning, including a postgraduate course in regulatory affairs where Bellan was his professor. This education, supported by Anvisa’s programs encouraging advanced degrees, deepened his understanding of regulation’s complexities, enabling roles like teaching postgraduate courses and contributing to Anvisa’s quality regulatory improvement initiatives. He advises aspiring pharmacists to pursue specialized training in regulatory affairs, as it equips them to contextualize technical roles (e.g., quality control) within broader regulatory frameworks, enhancing career opportunities in both public and private sectors.

Mansano’s journey underscores the importance of incremental growth. Entering Anvisa in 2014 via a competitive public exam, he built expertise over years, a path he encourages for others. For those eyeing Anvisa roles, he recommends rigorous preparation and continuous education to navigate the agency’s technical demands.

## Anvisa’s Legacy and Future

Celebrating 26 years since its 1999 founding, Anvisa has built a robust legacy, evolving from the Ministry of Health’s Secretariat of Health Surveillance to an independent regulatory agency under Law 9782. This shift to a state-driven, technically focused model enhances its agility and autonomy, even regulating Ministry-affiliated entities like public medicine producers. Anvisa’s framework, rooted in laws from the 1970s (e.g., Law 6360/1976), demonstrates enduring solidity, with updates ensuring relevance.

Mansano and Bellan emphasize Anvisa’s role in protecting public health while fostering industrial growth, a dual mission critical for Brazil’s future. As new technologies like AI reshape healthcare, Anvisa’s commitment to transparency, convergence, and scientific rigor positions it as a global leader in health regulation, safeguarding Brazilians while inviting investment. Their call to share this knowledge underscores a vision for future generations to build on this foundation, ensuring Brazil’s health system continues to evolve.
