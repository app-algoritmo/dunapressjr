## New Pope Elected on May 8, 2025: White Smoke Signals Habemus Papam

On the afternoon of Thursday, May 8, 2025, the chimney of the Sistine Chapel released the long-awaited white smoke, signaling to the world that the conclave gathered in the Vatican has reached a consensus: a new Pope has been elected. The Catholic Church, following the death of Pope Francis on April 21, now has its new leader, the Roman Pontiff who will guide over 1.3 billion faithful in the years to come. The world awaits with anticipation the official announcement of the new Pontiff’s name, to be proclaimed shortly with the traditional phrase “Habemus Papam” in St. Peter’s Square.

## The 2025 Conclave: A Historic and Global Process

The conclave, which began on May 7, brought together 133 voting cardinals under the age of 80, hailing from 71 countries, reflecting the global diversity of the Catholic Church. This was the most international conclave in history, with strong representation from cardinals of Latin America, Africa, Asia, and Oceania, a result of Pope Francis’ strategy to appoint cardinals from the world’s peripheries. Of the 252 cardinals in the College of Cardinals, 149 were appointed by Francis, with 108 being electors, suggesting a significant influence of his legacy on the choice of the new Pontiff.

The election, held in the Sistine Chapel under absolute secrecy, followed centuries-old rituals. After the *Pro Eligendo Pontifice* Mass on Wednesday morning, the cardinals entered in procession, chanting the hymn *Veni Creator Spiritus*. With the doors closed following the command “Extra Omnes” (“Everyone out”), the voting began. Each cardinal wrote their chosen candidate’s name on a ballot inscribed with *Eligo in Summum Pontificem* (“I elect as Supreme Pontiff”), which was then placed in an urn. To be elected, the new Pope needed at least 89 votes, representing two-thirds of the 133 electors present.

On Wednesday, the first vote ended with black smoke, indicating no candidate had secured the required majority. However, this afternoon, the white smoke announced that the process concluded in just two days, consistent with the average duration of modern conclaves, such as those that elected Benedict XVI (2005) and Francis (2013).

## The Announcement and the Room of Tears

Following the election, the chosen cardinal was asked by the Dean of the College of Cardinals: *“Acceptasne electionem de te canonice factam in Summum Pontificem?”* (“Do you accept your canonical election as Supreme Pontiff?”). Upon acceptance, the second question followed: *“Quo nomine vis vocari?”* (“By what name do you wish to be called?”). The name chosen by the new Pope will soon be revealed by the cardinal protodeacon, Dominique Mamberti, from the Loggia of the Blessings at St. Peter’s Basilica.

Before appearing to the public, the new Pontiff retired to the so-called “Room of Tears,” a small sacristy adjacent to the Sistine Chapel, where he donned the papal vestments prepared in three sizes. This moment, laden with emotion, is known as a time of solitary reflection, where the elected confronts the magnitude of his new responsibility.

## Who is the New Pope?

While the new Pope’s name has not yet been announced, speculation about the frontrunners has dominated discussions in recent days. Among the most mentioned names were:

- **Pietro Parolin** (Italy, 70 years old), the current Vatican Secretary of State, seen as a continuer of Francis’ reformist legacy. An artificial intelligence analysis suggested Parolin had a 27.6% chance of being elected, highlighting his diplomatic skill and global experience.

- **Luis Antonio Tagle** (Philippines, 67 years old), known for his closeness to communities and pastoral charisma, representing the growing Asian influence in the Church.

- **Jean-Marc Aveline** (France, 66 years old), Archbishop of Marseille, whose youth and progressive outlook made him a promising candidate.

- **Péter Erdő** (Hungary, 72 years old), a conservative with strong influence in Europe and Africa, an advocate for the New Evangelization.

The choice reflects a balance between continuity and renewal, considering Francis’ legacy of a more inclusive Church, focused on the poor and open to interreligious dialogue. However, the Catholic adage “He who enters a pope, exits a cardinal” reminds us that favorites are not always elected, as was the case with Jorge Bergoglio in 2013.

## The Future of the Catholic Church

The new Pope assumes leadership of the Church at a time of global challenges: secularization in developed nations, the growth of Catholicism in peripheral regions, debates over celibacy, inclusion of minorities, and climate change. With 80% of the voting cardinals appointed by Francis, the chosen leader is expected to maintain a conciliatory tone, avoiding drastic breaks but with nuances that may moderate or adjust some of the openings of the previous pontificate.

As St. Peter’s Square fills with faithful and onlookers, the world awaits the new Pope’s appearance on the balcony of the Basilica, where he will deliver his first *Urbi et Orbi* blessing (“to the city and the world”). This moment will mark the beginning of a new chapter for the Catholic Church, under the leadership of a Pontiff whose name and vision will be revealed momentarily.
