## Trump Ukraine Peace Plan: Latest Developments and Rejections Since November 2025

In the two weeks since the publication of an in-depth article on the Trump Ukraine peace plan on November 23, 2025, the landscape of proposed peace negotiations between Russia and Ukraine has shifted dramatically. What began as a comprehensive 28-point draft aimed at ending the ongoing conflict has evolved into a contentious battle of revisions, rejections, and accusations.

The original Trump Ukraine peace plan, leaked and verified by U.S. and Ukrainian officials, outlined measures including territorial recognitions, military caps, and economic reintegration efforts. However, recent events have highlighted deep divisions among the key players: Washington, Kyiv, Moscow, and European allies. As of December 9, 2025, the Trump Ukraine peace plan appears on the brink of collapse, with Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky firmly rejecting any cession of territory and U.S. President Donald Trump publicly criticizing Zelensky for allegedly sabotaging the process.

The initial excitement around the Trump Ukraine peace plan stemmed from its ambition to address sovereignty, security, and economic recovery in a single framework. Drafted through U.S.-mediated talks, it proposed confirming Ukraine's sovereignty alongside a non-aggression pact, limiting NATO expansion, capping Ukraine's military at 600,000 personnel, and barring NATO membership. It also included U.S. security guarantees with conditions, EU eligibility for Ukraine, and reconstruction funding from frozen Russian assets.

Russia would benefit from gradual sanction relief, economic reintegration, and recognition of Crimea, Luhansk, and Donetsk as Russian territory, with frozen lines in Kherson and Zaporizhzhia, a demilitarized zone, prisoner exchanges, amnesty, cultural programs, Ukrainian elections within 100 days, and a Trump-led peace council for enforcement, all starting with an immediate ceasefire. This plan, obtained by Axios and described as a real draft, set the stage for what many hoped would be a swift resolution.

Yet, in the days following the article's release, cracks began to emerge. On November 25, 2025, Trump backed away from imposing strict deadlines for Russia and Ukraine to reach a deal, signaling early flexibility amid growing criticism that the plan favored Russian interests. Sources revealed that the U.S. peace proposal had drawn elements from a Russian document, raising concerns about its balance. Analysts from organizations like the Center for Strategic and International Studies (CSIS) noted that Trump was pressuring Ukraine to sign the 28-point agreement, which they deemed pro-Russian and vague on enforcement. By November 26, reports emerged of secret U.S.-Russian talks in Abu Dhabi to discuss the proposal, indicating behind-the-scenes maneuvering.

### Zelensky's Firm Rejection of the Trump Ukraine Peace Plan

One of the most significant developments has been Ukraine's staunch opposition to core elements of the Trump Ukraine peace plan. President Zelensky has repeatedly declared that Ukraine will not surrender any territory, directly rejecting a central demand incorporated into Trump's proposal that would require Kyiv to cede areas occupied by Russian forces. This stance was reiterated in public statements and through diplomatic channels, emphasizing that any peace must be "real" and prevent future aggression. Zelensky's team has been actively working on counter-proposals, coordinating with European partners to refine components that address Ukrainian and European security concerns.

In response to the original 28-point plan, Ukraine proposed a revised 19-point version, cutting provisions that would shrink its military by 70%, abandon NATO aspirations permanently, and hand over the entire Donbas region. This counter-offer includes agreements to limit Ukraine's armed forces to 800,000 troops, with final details to be negotiated directly between Trump and Zelensky.

However, it firmly opposes territorial concessions, NATO exclusion, and vague security guarantees. Zelensky's refusal to back down on Donbas has made an agreement with Moscow unlikely, as Russia insists on recognition of its annexations. On December 9, 2025, Zelensky announced that Ukraine, in coordination with Europe, would present its refined proposal to the U.S., stressing that everything depends on Russia's willingness to halt the bloodshed.

Trump has not taken this lightly. In recent interviews, including one with Politico, he accused Zelensky of sabotaging the peace process, claiming the Ukrainian leader hadn't even read the plan while Putin had. Trump emphasized Russia's stronger position, the need for Ukrainian elections, and alleged mismanagement of U.S. funds by Kyiv. He warned that Zelensky must "come to grips with reality" as Ukraine has "lost" with no path to victory. This rhetoric has escalated tensions, with Trump lashing out at Zelensky and asserting Russia's upper hand.

### Russia's Stance and Potential Rejection

Russia's response to the evolving Trump Ukraine peace plan has been characteristically cautious and strategic. While publicly distancing itself, Moscow has indicated through officials like Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov that any deviation from earlier drafts, such as the Anchorage proposal, is unacceptable. Sources suggest Russia is preparing to reject the revised 19-point plan but will maintain a facade of openness to dialogue to avoid alienating Trump. This approach includes vague statements and strategic leaks, a tactic to prolong negotiations while advancing on the battlefield.

Critics, including former Ukrainian Foreign Minister Dmytro Kuleba, argue that the Trump Ukraine peace plan may already be "dead," but Trump continues to push it, potentially allowing Russia to seize more territory. Project Syndicate analysts have described the plan as heavily skewed toward Russia, meeting many of Putin's demands like territorial gains and military limits on Ukraine. An October conversation between Trump and Putin reportedly convinced the U.S. not to supply Ukraine with Tomahawk missiles, further tilting the balance. Meanwhile, Trump's sanctions on Russian oil giants like Lukoil and Rosneft have cut revenues by 27-35%, adding economic pressure but not yet forcing concessions.

Battlefield developments have bolstered Russia's position. Pokrovsk is encircled and on the verge of falling, a turning point that strengthens Moscow's negotiating leverage. Ukraine's use of ATACMS missiles deep into Russia elicited minimal response, suggesting Russia's focus on consolidation rather than escalation. Dmitry Medvedev has warned that seizing frozen Russian assets could lead to war, with the EU potentially paying reparations.

### European Pushback and Broader Implications

Europe has emerged as a vocal critic of the Trump Ukraine peace plan, frustrated by its expectations for the continent to fund $100 billion in reconstruction while the U.S. claims half the profits without equivalent investment. Leaders like German Chancellor Friedrich Merz have asserted Europe's ability to handle its security independently, rejecting U.S. attempts to dictate terms. European advisors met in London to align on a counter-proposal, demanding real U.S. Article 5-style guarantees and offering to contribute financially. This coordination underscores fears that the plan undermines EU unity and NATO cohesion.

Public sentiment in Ukraine has shifted, with 70% now favoring peace compared to 20% in 2022, reflecting war weariness. However, the Trump Ukraine peace plan's ambiguities—such as vague security guarantees and reliance on paper agreements—echo the failed 1994 Budapest Memorandum, eroding trust. Analysts from The Atlantic describe Trump's approach as pendulum-like, swinging toward Moscow with demands for eastern territory surrender and NATO bans.

The Trump Ukraine peace plan's future remains uncertain. While Trump claims the revised plan is "almost finished" with only a few issues left, envoys like Keith Whitcoff and Mike Driscoll continue shuttle diplomacy. Yet, with Ukraine's counter-proposal set for U.S. review and Russia's likely rejection, the process could drag into 2026. Economic pressures, including increased NATO seizures of Russian shadow fleet ships, may force movement, but territorial disputes persist as the core impasse.

As negotiations falter, the human cost mounts. The Trump Ukraine peace plan, once hailed as bold, now risks inviting further conflict if not balanced equitably. Al Jazeera reports progress on a revised version awarding Ukraine more concessions, but skepticism abounds. Fletcher School experts criticize the plan's inexperience and unrealistic expectations. For now, all eyes are on whether pressure from sanctions and battlefield realities can bridge the gaps.

The past two weeks have transformed the Trump Ukraine peace plan from a promising draft into a flashpoint of international discord. Zelensky's defiance, Trump's impatience, Russia's stalling, and Europe's demands illustrate the challenges of achieving lasting peace. As talks continue, the world watches to see if diplomacy can prevail over division.
