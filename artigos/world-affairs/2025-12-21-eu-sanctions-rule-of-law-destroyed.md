## EU Sanctions Rule of Law Destroyed: Anyone Can Be Next with MEP Insights

The European Union, often hailed as a beacon of democracy and human rights, is facing a profound crisis in its adherence to the rule of law. Through its sanctions regime, the EU has quietly implemented measures that echo medieval practices of outlawry, stripping citizens of their basic rights without transparent judicial oversight. This development raises alarming questions about the erosion of fundamental liberties in an institution built to protect them.

MEP Michael von der Schulenburg, a German parliamentarian from the BSW party, provides a firsthand account of these unchecked power dynamics, highlighting how EU sanctions rule of law violations are becoming normalized. In a recent interview, von der Schulenburg detailed the secretive processes behind these sanctions, emphasizing their incompatibility with EU treaties and international standards.

The issue came to light with the latest EU sanctions package, which targeted several EU and Swiss citizens accused of supporting Russian propaganda or disinformation. Von der Schulenburg learned of these decisions only on the day they were announced, underscoring the opacity of the process. No prior information, no protocols, and no details are shared with parliamentarians or the public. This lack of transparency is not an oversight but a deliberate design, as these measures fall under the EU's Common Foreign and Security Policy (CFSP), governed by the Council of the European Union.

At the heart of the problem is the sanctions list, which now includes 59 individuals, including Germans, Swiss, French, and others. These people are not necessarily linked to Russia; some focus on issues like Gaza or Africa. Yet, they are branded as threats, effectively becoming outlaws in modern Europe. Sanctioned individuals lose access to bank accounts, employment, enterprises, and travel freedoms. Their financial existence is erased, making it nearly impossible to mount a legal defense. Von der Schulenburg compares this to medieval outlawry, where individuals were declared outside the protection of the law, left vulnerable and destitute.

What makes this particularly egregious is the circumvention of judicial safeguards. According to the Lisbon Treaty, decisions by the Council on foreign relations cannot be challenged in the European Court of Justice (ECJ). This creates a scenario where the Council acts as both lawmaker and judge, reminiscent of absolute monarchies like Henry VIII's England. Von der Schulenburg points out that while national laws in member states provide sophisticated protections against unfair reporting or incitement, the EU route is chosen precisely to bypass these. There is no definition of "disinformation" in EU law, rendering accusations arbitrary and unsubstantiated.

### EU Sanctions Rule of Law: A Direct Assault on Democracy

The EU sanctions rule of law crisis extends beyond individual cases to a broader attack on free speech and social media. Von der Schulenburg argues that these measures intimidate dissenting voices, especially those critical of EU war policies. Established media often align with government positions, leaving alternative narratives to social media platforms. By targeting journalists, experts, and commentators, the EU stifles parallel discourse, creating a chilling effect on public debate.

A striking example is Jacques Baud, a Swiss expert on intelligence, who has appeared on national TV multiple times but now faces media silence post-sanctioning. Similarly, individuals like Natali Yam and Huseyin Dogru find their lives upended without recourse. Yam reported being denied boarding on African airlines due to the sanctions list, illustrating how these measures extend globally through corporate compliance fears.

Von der Schulenburg and his colleagues commissioned a legal opinion from a former ECJ judge and international law professors. This report, professionally crafted and apolitical, concludes that the EU's sanctioning process violates the EU Charter of Fundamental Rights and international law. It does not question the existence of sanctions but insists they must adhere to rule of law principles, which they currently do not. The experts found no justification for the current regime under EU law, labeling it a complete denial of due process.

The decision-making process further exemplifies the EU sanctions rule of law breakdown. The Council meets at various levels—ambassadors prepare, foreign ministers decide—but no protocols or voting details are public. Even MEPs like von der Schulenburg are kept in the dark. This secrecy contrasts sharply with court proceedings, where transparency is mandatory. The Parliament's role is limited; it lacks initiative rights and can only react to Commission proposals. Von der Schulenburg notes the absence of separation of powers, with executive, legislative, and judicial functions blurred in the Council and Commission.

Hypocrisy is rampant. While the EU awards the Sakharov Prize to imprisoned journalists in Belarus and Georgia, it simultaneously sanctions dozens for their opinions. Parliament walls project slogans like "freedom of thought," yet few of the 720 MEPs raise concerns. Von der Schulenburg laments the pro-war stance permeating even left-leaning groups, with only a handful supporting efforts to challenge these measures.

### Can the European Parliament Intervene in EU Sanctions Rule of Law Violations?

The Parliament's ability to intervene is uncertain due to its vague statutes. While it is the only democratically elected EU body, it cannot initiate laws or directly reverse Council decisions. Von der Schulenburg suggests that a parliamentary majority could morally challenge the sanctions, but politically, it's unlikely. The EU's structure reveals its limitations: no true separation of powers, leading to medieval-like governance where individuals are outlawed indefinitely without time limits or appeals.

For sanctioned individuals, legal challenges are daunting. Implementation occurs at the national level—banks close accounts on orders from member states—but appeals must target the EU decision, hidden behind bureaucratic walls. Costs are prohibitive, especially for those without income. Von der Schulenburg's team is pushing back: they've published the legal opinion, issued press releases, and plan an open letter to EU leaders like Borrell and von der Leyen, demanding a review based on rule of law.

Public response is crucial. Von der Schulenburg calls for outcry, suggesting citizens write to political parties expressing concerns about EU sanctions rule of law erosion. Media silence exacerbates the issue; without coverage, awareness remains low. He warns that anyone could be next—critics of EU policies might be labeled as supporting adversaries, facing similar fates.

### The Broader Implications for EU Sanctions Rule of Law and European Future

This crisis ties into the Ukraine war, where EU policies have escalated tensions. Von der Schulenburg, drawing from his UN experience in conflict resolution, criticizes the EU's war rhetoric and self-destructive strategies. Sanctions reflect a wartime mentality: controlling public opinion to maintain support, demonizing dissent as internal sabotage. He references historical concepts like the "stab-in-the-back" myth, where failures are blamed on domestic enemies.

In Brussels, the atmosphere is tense. Leaders like von der Leyen and Kallas push aggressive stances, but parliamentary interest wanes—few attend their speeches. Resolutions demand Russian capitulation, removing negotiation flexibility. Von der Schulenburg argues this ignores battlefield realities and Russia's security concerns, dooming Ukraine to further destruction.

The EU's power grab undermines member states' sovereignty, turning the union into a tool against its own citizens. Von der Schulenburg fears Europe is committing suicide: isolated geopolitically, economically strained, and losing moral high ground. Even figures like Trump criticize EU democracy deficits, a damning indictment.

Advice for EU citizens: Organize, demand transparency, and support initiatives like the legal opinion repository on von der Schulenburg's website. Without massive public pressure, these trends will continue, eroding the rule of law that defines Europe.

The EU sanctions rule of law crisis signals a dangerous shift. What began as foreign policy tools now threatens domestic freedoms, reviving outlawry in a continent that prides itself on progress. Von der Schulenburg's insights urge immediate action to reclaim democratic principles before it's too late.
