## Asia’s Response to America of Trump’s Unpredictable Economic and Foreign Policies

The re-emergence of Donald Trump as a dominant force in American politics has sent ripples across the globe, with East Asia at the epicenter of this uncertainty. In a recent interview, Professor Glenn Diesen and Einar Tangen, a senior fellow at the Taihe Institute and chairman of Asia Narratives, dissected the implications of Trump’s “big beautiful bill” and the broader trajectory of U.S. policy, particularly its trade war with China. Their discussion paints a picture of a region grappling with America’s strategic unpredictability, which threatens economic stability and geopolitical cohesion.

### The “Massive Miscalculated Mandate”

Trump’s signature legislative achievement, often referred to as the “big beautiful bill,” is a colossal $5 trillion borrowing package that significantly expands the U.S. national debt. Tangen describes it as a “massive miscalculated mandate” that backloads economic pain for the American public until after the midterms. While offering tax breaks that disproportionately favor the wealthy, the bill’s long-term consequences include skyrocketing debt and potential interest rate hikes. The Congressional Budget Office estimates the national debt could balloon by an additional $8 trillion over the next three and a half years, on top of the $5 trillion borrowing authority. This fiscal recklessness, coupled with a declining U.S. dollar (down 10% recently), erodes global confidence in American treasuries, forcing reliance on private markets with higher risk premiums.

The bill also includes a tariff “slush fund,” projected to generate $300–400 billion, which Trump intends to use to delay economic pain and appease his base. However, Tangen warns that tariffs, such as the proposed 20% across-the-board and 40% on transshipped goods, will fuel inflation. In countries like Vietnam, where 70–80% of exports involve Chinese inputs or transshipping, these tariffs could disrupt trade flows and increase costs, further straining global supply chains.

### Economic and Political Fallout

The economic ramifications extend beyond inflation. Trump’s policies, including mass deportations of migrant workers, threaten to exacerbate labor shortages in sectors like agriculture and hospitality, driving up costs and adding to inflationary pressures. Meanwhile, U.S. exports face challenges due to a global slowdown and uncompetitive pricing—Vietnam, for instance, imported only $13 billion in American goods last year, a fraction of its $150 billion trade volume. The domestic pushback is already evident, as Americans grapple with rising prices and question Trump’s unfulfilled promises to control costs and avoid foreign entanglements.

Politically, Trump’s “strategic unpredictability” is alienating allies and adversaries alike. His “flood the zone” tactics—daily pronouncements and crisis creation—may dominate media cycles but undermine trust in U.S. leadership. The BRICS coalition, representing half the world’s population and 40% of global GDP, has united in criticizing America’s arbitrary actions. In East Asia, nations like Japan and Vietnam are caught between maintaining economic ties with the U.S. and navigating pressure from China, which views compliance with U.S. tariffs as a betrayal.

### East Asia’s Response

East Asia, particularly China, is adopting a cautious yet strategic approach to Trump’s volatility. China, as Tangen notes, is “slow-playing” Washington, engaging in protracted negotiations to buy time while building alternative trade networks. By eliminating visa requirements for numerous countries and reducing tariffs for African nations, China is fostering a broader trade ecosystem that reduces reliance on the U.S. market. This strategy is complemented by efforts to boost domestic consumption and strengthen regional partnerships, such as through ASEAN.

Japan, traditionally a staunch U.S. ally, is showing signs of recalibration. Despite historical tensions with China, Japan is initiating new communications, potentially signaling a shift toward pragmatic economic cooperation. Tangen highlights the possibility of China joining the Comprehensive and Progressive Agreement for Trans-Pacific Partnership (CPTPP), which could bolster regional trade without U.S. involvement. Similarly, resolving disputes in the South China Sea through shared resource development could transform regional dynamics, reducing the need for U.S.-led “freedom of navigation” patrols and reshaping perceptions of China’s role.

Vietnam and India present more complex cases. Vietnam’s agreement to U.S. tariff structures risks antagonizing China, which may retaliate given the heavy reliance on Chinese components in Vietnamese exports. India, meanwhile, balances its historical grievances against colonial powers with strategic partnerships with the U.S., Israel, and France. However, growing frustration with U.S. interference—evident in Modi’s belief that American actions cost him an electoral majority—could push India toward closer ties with China, though it is unlikely to fully abandon either side.

### China’s Opportunity

China’s approach contrasts sharply with Trump’s unpredictability. By leading with pragmatic trade policies and regional cooperation, China positions itself as the “adult in the room.” Tangen suggests a potential “Phase Two” agreement where China commits to purchasing U.S. energy and agricultural products at market prices, creating predictability that could stabilize bilateral relations. Such a deal would force Trump to honor commitments, lest he disrupt American farmers and energy producers.

Moreover, China has a golden opportunity to capitalize on America’s declining soft power. The U.S.’s self-inflicted wounds—evident in its ballooning debt, eroding dollar dominance (60% of world trade now occurs outside the dollar), and inconsistent foreign policy—provide China with leverage to strengthen ties with neighbors like Japan and ASEAN members. Resolving regional disputes, such as those in the South China Sea, could further cement China’s role as a stabilizing force.

### The Global Context

The interview underscores a broader crisis in Western leadership, not limited to the U.S. Tangen points to a lack of independent thinking in democratic systems, where party loyalty and narrative-driven politics stifle innovation. This is evident in Europe, where diplomats and politicians privately acknowledge policy failures but adhere to public scripts. The result is a global order increasingly skeptical of Western reliability, with BRICS and other non-Western blocs gaining traction.

For East Asia, the challenge is to navigate this transition without succumbing to U.S.-instigated divisions. China’s strategy of slow-playing Trump, building alternative trade networks, and fostering regional cooperation offers a path forward. However, success depends on China’s ability to present a compelling alternative to the U.S.-led order—one based on mutual prosperity rather than hegemony.

Trump’s unpredictable policies, from massive debt increases to aggressive tariffs, are reshaping global dynamics, with East Asia at the forefront of this shift. While the U.S. risks alienating allies and undermining its economic credibility, China and its neighbors have an opportunity to redefine regional relationships. By prioritizing predictability, trade, and cooperation, East Asia can mitigate the chaos emanating from an unpredictable America and chart a course toward a more stable and prosperous future.
