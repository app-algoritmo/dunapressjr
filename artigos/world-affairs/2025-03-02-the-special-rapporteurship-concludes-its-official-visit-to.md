Freedom of expression is one of the fundamental pillars of any democracy, and the recent visit of the [Special Rapporteurship for Freedom of Expression (RELE)](https://www.oas.org/pt/CIDH/jsForm/?File=/pt/cidh/expressao/prensa/notas/2025/046.asp&utm_content=country-bra&utm_term=class-inst) to Brazil comes at a crucial moment for the country. In recent years, debates over censorship, media control, regulation of social networks, and the persecution of journalists and public figures have gained prominence, raising questions about the limits of freedom of expression and the role of the state in guaranteeing this right. Given this scenario, the evaluation by the Inter-American Commission on Human Rights (IACHR) is essential to understanding the current state of Brazilian democracy and identifying challenges and progress in respecting this fundamental right. The report resulting from this visit may influence public policies, strengthen international transparency, and even impact Brazil’s global image, making its analysis and interpretation even more relevant.

Washington D.C. – The Special Rapporteurship for Freedom of Expression (RELE) of the Inter-American Commission on Human Rights (IACHR) concluded its official visit to Brazil between February 9 and 14, 2025. The mission was carried out in response to an official invitation from the Brazilian State, aiming to gather information on the state of freedom of expression and its impact on human rights in the country.

The delegation was led by the Special Rapporteur for Freedom of Expression, Pedro Vaca Villarreal, who, along with members of RELE and the IACHR Executive Secretariat, visited Brasília, Rio de Janeiro, and São Paulo, where they engaged with a broad range of voices.

During the visit, the team met with authorities at different levels and branches of government, including representatives from the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, the Ministry of Justice and Public Security, the Ministry of Health, the Ministry of Finance, the Ministry of the Environment and Climate Change, the Ministry of Women, the Ministry of Human Rights and Citizenship, the Secretariat for Social Communication of the Presidency of the Republic, the Attorney General’s Office, the Supreme Federal Court, the Superior Electoral Court, the National Council of Justice, the National Congress, the Federal Prosecutor’s Office for Citizen’s Rights, and other members of the Federal Public Ministry. They also engaged with the National Council of the Public Ministry, the Public Defender’s Office of the State of São Paulo, the Federal Police, the National Telecommunications Agency, the National Council for Human Rights, the Economic and Social Sustainable Development Council, and the Internet Steering Committee.

Likewise, the delegation held listening and dialogue sessions with organizations dedicated to the defense and promotion of human rights, entities monitoring the state of freedom of expression on the Internet, journalists, media outlets, think tanks, individuals who believe their right to freedom of expression has been violated and their families, representatives of digital platforms, as well as academics and experts on freedom of expression and human rights. Additionally, the delegation met with parliamentarians and political leaders from various ideological backgrounds, ensuring a pluralistic and inclusive perspective.

Until February 21, RELE received a broad, detailed, and diverse range of information on the state of freedom of expression and its impact on human rights, which will be thoroughly evaluated alongside all the documentation presented during the working meetings.

The Rapporteurship thanks the Brazilian State for the guarantees and support provided for the development of the mission. It also expresses its gratitude to all individuals who participated in the working sessions, presenting reports and testimonies to the delegation, sharing their perspectives and experiences regarding the state of the right to freedom of expression in Brazil. RELE also highlights the valuable support of civil society organizations in coordinating activities and meetings.

Finally, the Special Rapporteurship recognizes Brazil’s openness to international observation in the area of freedom of expression, which is a sign of its commitment to democratic institutions and human rights.

The Special Rapporteurship for Freedom of Expression was created by the Inter-American Commission on Human Rights (IACHR) to promote the hemispheric defense of the right to freedom of thought and expression, considering its fundamental role in the consolidation and development of a democratic system.
