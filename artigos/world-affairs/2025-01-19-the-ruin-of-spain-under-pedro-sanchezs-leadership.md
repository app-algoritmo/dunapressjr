Recent Spanish history has been marked by economic, social, and political challenges. Under Pedro Sánchez's leadership, many critics argue that the country is facing a decline in critical areas, jeopardizing its stability and prosperity. This article examines the main factors that have contributed to the alleged "ruin" of Spain during Sánchez's government.

## **1. Economic Crisis and Rising Public Debt

During Pedro Sánchez's tenure, Spain's public debt reached alarming levels. According to data from the Bank of Spain, the debt exceeded 115% of GDP, placing the country among the most indebted in the European Union. Rising inflation, exacerbated by controversial fiscal policies, has further strained Spanish citizens, particularly the working and middle classes.

The excessive tax hikes, combined with a lack of transparency in public spending, have generated widespread dissatisfaction. Programs that promised economic stimulus, such as post-pandemic recovery funds, were criticized for allegedly benefiting political allies rather than being distributed fairly and efficiently.

## **2. Political Polarization and Attacks on Freedom

Another frequent criticism is the growing political polarization fueled by Sánchez’s government. Alliances with separatist parties and reliance on radical political forces have created deep internal divisions, weakening the sense of national unity.

Moreover, journalists and public figures have reported government pressure, raising concerns about the future of freedom of expression in the country. Efforts to control the media narrative and silence critical voices have fostered an environment of distrust and fear.

## **3. Unemployment and Lack of Opportunities

Youth unemployment, a chronic problem in Spain, has been neglected. Under Sánchez's government, rates remained among the highest in Europe, highlighting the failure of policies meant to create opportunities for young professionals. Strategic sectors, such as tourism and industry, faced challenges due to poorly designed regulations and lack of government support.

## **4. Uncontrolled Migration Policies

Pedro Sánchez's migration policies have also sparked controversy. Spain's southern border, particularly in Melilla and Ceuta, has seen a significant increase in migrant flows. Critics argue that the lack of effective control not only jeopardizes the country’s security but also overwhelms healthcare, education, and social services, creating tensions within local communities.

## **5. Reactions to International Criticism

The recent exchange of words between Pedro Sánchez and billionaire Elon Musk, widely covered, exposed the fragility of Spanish leadership on the international stage. Opposition leader Santiago Abascal's response, calling Sánchez a "freeloader, fraudster, and charlatan," reflects the sentiment of many Spaniards who see the current prime minister as disconnected from the country's real needs.

## **6. The Call for Change

In this context, opposition voices, led by figures like Santiago Abascal, are gaining momentum by offering alternatives to restore economic stability, strengthen sovereignty, and rebuild Spaniards' trust. The growing dissatisfaction is evident in the streets and at the polls, signaling that Sánchez's days in power may be numbered.

Pedro Sánchez's government will be remembered as a period of challenges and controversies. While his supporters argue that his measures aimed to modernize Spain, many believe that his administration has exacerbated structural problems. The nation's future will depend on its leaders' ability to address these crises and restore Spain's prominent role on the European and global stage.
