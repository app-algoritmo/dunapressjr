## The Maidan Massacre Unveiled: Ivan Katchanovski on Ukraine’s 2014 False Flag Coup

The Maidan Massacre of February 20, 2014, in Kyiv remains a pivotal and controversial event that reshaped Ukraine and triggered a decade-long conflict with global ramifications. In a May 4, 2025, interview with Prof. Glenn Diesen, Ukrainian-Canadian political science professor Ivan Katchanovski, an expert on Ukrainian studies, delves into his extensive research on the massacre. Katchanovski, who has also exposed the celebration of a Ukrainian Nazi collaborator in the Canadian Parliament, reveals overwhelming evidence that the massacre—blamed on President Viktor Yanukovych—was a false flag operation orchestrated by elements of the oligarchic opposition and far-right groups, with Western complicity, to overthrow the Ukrainian government and ignite conflict with Russia.

## The Maidan Massacre: A Turning Point in History

The Maidan protests, which began in late 2013, escalated into violence on February 20, 2014, when snipers killed 49 protesters and several policemen in Kyiv’s central square. The massacre was immediately attributed to Yanukovych’s government, specifically the Berkut police unit, providing the catalyst for his ouster. This narrative, propagated by Western and Ukrainian media, framed the killings as a brutal crackdown on peaceful protesters, justifying the regime change that followed. The event triggered a cascade of conflicts: the annexation of Crimea, the Donbas war, and ultimately Russia’s 2022 invasion of Ukraine, as well as heightened tensions between Russia and the West.

Katchanovski’s research, detailed in his upcoming journal article in *Econ Journal Watch* and his open-access book published by Palgrave Macmillan, challenges this narrative. He has meticulously analyzed publicly available evidence—court hearings, witness testimonies, forensic reports, and video footage—over 11 years. His findings reveal that the massacre was not the work of Yanukovych’s forces but a deliberate act by Maidan-aligned snipers, aimed at creating chaos and blaming the government to legitimize the coup.

## Evidence of a False Flag Operation

Katchanovski’s investigation draws on a wealth of primary sources, including live broadcasts of the Maidan events, trial sessions (2015-2023), and the 2023 Maidan Massacre verdict, which he followed closely. The evidence is damning:

- **Witness Testimonies:** The majority of wounded Maidan activists testified during trials that they saw snipers in the Hotel Ukraina and other Maidan-controlled locations, shooting at both protesters and police. Over 100 prosecution witnesses confirmed these accounts, describing snipers targeting activists from the hotel, which was under Maidan control.

- **Forensic and Ballistic Evidence:** Government-conducted forensic examinations showed that Maidan activists were shot from steep angles—side, back, or above—consistent with the Hotel Ukraina’s position, not the ground-level Berkut police in front of the protesters. Ballistic reports found no match between bullets extracted from victims and the Kalashnikov rifles used by Berkut, contradicting claims of police involvement. One falsified forensic report, which blamed Berkut for 31 of the 49 deaths, overturned 40 prior examinations, including automated analyses showing no link.

- **Video Footage:** A Belgian TV video, shown in court, captured Maidan activists being led to an area where they were shot from the Hotel Ukraina. The victims, aware of the snipers, pleaded not to be shot. German ZDF and RDF footage further confirmed snipers firing from the hotel, though RDF later withheld key footage, and ZDF removed its broadcast. Katchanovski preserved this evidence, which also showed far-right-linked snipers shooting at a BBC crew.

- **Admissions by Snipers:** Fourteen Maidan snipers, including members of a far-right-linked company tied to the Right Sector, admitted to shooting police, initiating the massacre. This company, led by a commander later elected to Ukraine’s parliament, was filmed moving into the Hotel Ukraina during the massacre. The commander hinted at a cover-up, stating in a Ukrainian TV interview that the public was “not ready” to know the truth.

- **Georgian Snipers’ Testimonies:** Former Georgian military members, hired by Maidan opposition leaders and ex-Georgian President Mikheil Saakashvili, admitted to participating in the massacre. They claimed they were instructed to kill both police and protesters to create chaos, blaming each side for the other’s deaths. Despite using false documents, one sniper’s identity was verified by Ukrainian prosecutors, and their testimonies aligned with other evidence.

The 2023 Maidan Massacre verdict partially acknowledged these findings, confirming that snipers in the Hotel Ukraina, under Maidan control, killed and wounded activists. It also found no evidence that Yanukovych or his officials ordered the massacre, contradicting the official narrative. However, the verdict still falsely blamed Berkut for 31 deaths, a claim Katchanovski attributes to the lack of judicial independence in Ukraine, where far-right activists intimidated the court, even threatening to burn it down.

## The Coup and Western Complicity

Katchanovski’s research extends to the ouster of Yanukovych, revealing it as a coup orchestrated by the oligarchic opposition, far-right groups, and Western powers. On February 21, 2014, Yanukovych signed an EU-brokered agreement with the opposition for early elections and a unity government, with France, Germany, and Poland as guarantors. Yet, the next day, Maidan forces stormed government buildings, forcing Yanukovych to flee Kyiv. Katchanovski cites assassination attempts as the reason for his flight: his vehicle was shot at, and helicopters carrying him to Kharkiv were ordered to be shot down by the new Maidan-appointed interim leader, Oleksandr Turchynov.

The parliamentary vote to remove Yanukovych was unconstitutional, lacking proper impeachment procedures, and conducted under duress. Videos and testimonies show Maidan activists, including far-right snipers, coercing MPs from Yanukovych’s Party of Regions and the Communist Party to vote for his removal. The vote count was falsified—only 260 MPs were present, not the 600 claimed—using duplicated voting cards, as admitted by a Maidan opposition leader.

Western complicity is evident. Katchanovski cites interviews by two Ukrainian journalists with Maidan leaders from the Svoboda party, who revealed a meeting with a Western representative weeks before the massacre. The representative agreed that if 100 activists were killed, the West would stop recognizing Yanukovych’s government—a threshold met with the “Heavenly Hundred” narrative, inflated to include unrelated deaths. US Vice President Joe Biden, in his memoirs, admitted to pressuring Yanukovych to withdraw forces and leave Ukraine, holding him responsible for the massacre without evidence. Leaked calls between US officials Victoria Nuland and Ambassador Geoffrey Pyatt show them planning the post-coup government, which materialized under Arseniy Yatsenyuk, as Nuland had predicted.

## The Role of the Far Right and Media Whitewashing

The far right’s role in the massacre and coup was significant, despite their small numbers. Groups like Right Sector and Svoboda, alongside the Patriot of Ukraine (a precursor to the Azov Battalion), wielded disproportionate power through violence. Katchanovski identifies a Right Sector-linked sniper company, led by a commander later elected to parliament, as key perpetrators. This commander’s admission of hiding the truth underscores the cover-up. Svoboda MPs, including one filmed escorting snipers in the Hotel Ukraina, were directly involved, with one MP’s hotel room identified as a sniper position.

Post-coup, the far right’s influence grew. Leaders like Dmytro Yarosh of Right Sector and Andriy Biletsky of Azov openly threatened President Volodymyr Zelensky, who abandoned his 2019 peace platform under pressure. Yarosh, after threatening Zelensky’s life, was appointed to a high military position, while Biletsky, who espoused white supremacist rhetoric, led Azov’s expansion into an army corps. Western media initially reported on these groups’ fascist ideologies—BBC and The Guardian noted their neo-Nazi symbols and rhetoric—but later whitewashed their role, presenting them as patriotic defenders post-2022 invasion. USAID’s funding of 85-90% of Ukrainian media outlets, as revealed by Trump’s 2025 defunding, ensured conformity with Western narratives, suppressing evidence of far-right involvement.

## Academic Suppression and the Cost of Truth

Katchanovski’s work has faced significant backlash. Despite positive peer reviews, publishers pressured him to include discredited narratives—such as Nord Stream’s destruction by Russia or Ukraine’s democratic status—contradicting his evidence. When he refused, one publisher canceled his book contract on the Russia-Ukraine war, though another major Western academic press accepted it for publication in summer 2025. In Ukraine, his property was illegally seized through falsified court decisions, linked to far-right groups involved in the massacre. He was also placed on the Myrotvorets “hit list” for exposing the SS Galicia veteran celebrated in Canada’s Parliament in 2023, a move supported by Zelensky’s government.

Katchanovski compares this censorship to his Soviet-era experiences, where his undergraduate thesis on the inefficiency of the Soviet system led to academic repercussions. He sees parallels in the West’s current intolerance for dissent, noting the case of Estonian professor Rein Müllerson, stripped of honors for attending Russian conferences. Academic freedom, he argues, is eroding, with scholars smeared as Russian propagandists for challenging narratives, despite evidence.

## The Maidan Massacre’s Lasting Impact

Ivan Katchanovski’s research uncovers the Maidan Massacre as a false flag operation that changed the course of history, orchestrated by far-right groups and oligarchic opposition with Western backing. The massacre enabled the 2014 coup, setting off a chain of conflicts that continue today. The suppression of this truth—through media whitewashing, far-right glorification, and academic censorship—reflects a broader failure to confront inconvenient realities, perpetuating a cycle of violence and mistrust. Katchanovski’s commitment to evidence over narrative offers a critical lesson: only by facing the truth can the world hope to address the root causes of the Ukraine conflict and prevent further escalation. His open-access book provides a vital resource for understanding this pivotal moment.
