## **A New Economic Paradigm**, BRICS economic rise

In a thought-provoking interview with Prof. Glenn Diesen, market analyst and former hedge fund manager Alex Krainer examines the shifting global economic landscape, contrasting Europe’s decline with the rise of BRICS. Krainer critiques the US’s tariff strategy under Trump, highlighting its devastating impact on Europe, and explores the potential for BRICS to offer an alternative economic framework. Amidst Europe’s political and economic turmoil, Krainer sees hope in grassroots globalization, driven by technological decentralization and BRICS’ inclusive approach, which could redefine global trade and prosperity.

## Trump’s Tariffs: A Blow to Europe’s Economic Model

Krainer begins by analyzing Trump’s tariff policies, which he views as a chaotic response to the US’s unsustainable economic trajectory—marked by mounting debt, de-industrialization, and declining technological competitiveness. While acknowledging the logic behind tariffs to re-industrialize, Krainer questions whether the Trump administration’s approach is calculated or impulsive. The US lacks the industrial capacity to replace imports in the foreseeable future, and the broad, unpredictable tariffs disrupt supply chains and deter investment. The EU’s offer of “zero for zero” tariffs suggests some negotiating success, but Krainer argues the broader fallout was inevitable, as the “dry tinder” of global economic imbalances has been piling up for decades.

Europe, heavily reliant on exporting manufactured goods to the US and UK—often produced in China for cheaper labor and energy—bears the brunt of these tariffs. Krainer notes that this model, which underpinned Europe’s prosperity, has been “demolished” by Trump’s policies. The US’s actions, including the March 15, 2025, launch of Operation Rough Rider in Yemen, further exacerbate Europe’s woes.

Despite replacing Operation Prosperity Guardian, Rough Rider intensified Houthi attacks, halting Red Sea trade—a route critical for 55-60% of global trade, predominantly European. Krainer references a tabletop exercise by CENTCOM, AFRICOM, and European security organizations, which identified the Red Sea as a “global hub of competitiveness.” With only 3% of this trade being American, the blockade primarily harms Europe, a move Krainer suspects may be deliberate.

## Europe’s Decline: A Target of US Policy

Krainer posits that the US, possibly driven by Wall Street and corporate interests, is waging “general warfare” against Europe. Trump’s April 2025 statement on Air Force One—that the EU was created to “rip off” the US—reinforces this view. The tariffs, the Nord Stream 2 pipeline destruction (under Biden), and the Yemen operation align with a pattern of undermining Europe’s economic stability. Krainer cites a panel discussion on Nema’s Dialogue Works, where Colonel Larry Wilkerson and Ambassador Chas Freeman noted that the Yemen operation’s primary impact was on Europe, not the Houthis, who have endured Western attacks for over a decade without capitulating.

Europe’s leadership, Krainer argues, is unprepared for this crisis. Obsessed with the Ukraine war and maintaining a unipolar Western hegemony, they have alienated key powers—Russia through NATO expansion and sanctions, China through diplomatic snubs, and the US through attempts to “Trump-proof” NATO and escalate attacks on Russia in late 2024. European leaders like Kaja Kallas frame Russia and China as adversaries, leaving the continent isolated. The loss of colonial influence in Africa, coupled with economic uncompetitiveness—exemplified by Krainer’s sarcastic mention of “bottle caps that don’t come off”—further weakens Europe’s position.

## Europe’s Internal Crisis: A Path to Dystopia

Internally, Europe faces mounting social and political pressures. Krainer warns of a “Nazification” process, as colonial oligarchies, ruling since the 19th century, cling to power. Facing potential civil unrest in France, Germany, and Britain, these elites are likely to intensify repressive measures—digital IDs, central bank digital currencies, pervasive surveillance, censorship, and even “medical assistance in dying.” The crackdown on free speech is evident in Europe’s reaction to dissent: Krainer notes that arguing NATO expansion provoked Russia—a view shared by historical figures like Bill Clinton and Henry Kissinger—elicits accusations of parroting Russian propaganda, often invoking 1930s analogies.

The European political class’s obsession with war narratives, such as absurd stories of Putin “bathing in deer antler blood” (reported by CNN, *Business Insider*, and others), reflects panic as public trust in media and leaders declines. NATO surveys, cited by a contact of Krainer’s, reveal that 75% of people in former Warsaw Pact countries and 66% in Western nations like Spain, France, and Italy sympathize with Russia’s position in Ukraine, limiting elites’ ability to mobilize for war. Yet, Krainer fears that, as in Ukraine, populations may be coerced into conflict, with ruling elites diverting social unrest by targeting “barbarians at the gate”—namely Russia—potentially leading to a broader European war in 5-7 years.

## BRICS as a Beacon of Prosperity

Krainer sees BRICS—Brazil, Russia, India, China, South Africa, and new members like the UAE and Indonesia—as a promising alternative to the collapsing Western order. Unlike the EU, which prioritizes corporate interests, BRICS fosters a “people’s globalization.” Krainer highlights the emergence of decentralized economic networks, where small businesses and individuals bypass large corporations. Examples include a New York couple who, after failing to save their family farm, began direct-to-consumer meat sales, earning over $1 million annually by cutting out corporate middlemen. This trend, enabled by digital platforms, is growing globally, with BRICS’ payment systems facilitating point-to-point trade.

BRICS’ economic potential is immense, having already surpassed the G7 in GDP. China’s success in lifting 800 million people out of poverty has created a vast consumer base, a model it is replicating in the Global South. Krainer envisions a world with 3-5 billion affluent consumers, where producers—whether of guitars, amplifiers, or tea—can directly access global markets. This “grassroots globalization,” supported by BRICS’ infrastructure and financial mechanisms, contrasts with the West’s financialized capitalism, which Krainer critiques using Adam Smith and David Ricardo’s warnings against rent-seekers who extract value without contributing.

## Europe’s Future: A Century of Humiliation?

Krainer questions whether Europe faces a “century of humiliation,” akin to China’s 19th-century experience, but notes the internet’s transformative role. Social media and decentralized platforms undermine narrative control, as seen in Europe’s failure to rally anti-Russia sentiment despite propaganda. While European elites push censorship—proposing a billion-dollar fine against X to silence Elon Musk—Krainer believes public awareness is growing, potentially fueling social uprisings and the rise of sovereigntist movements.

Europe’s current trajectory, however, is grim. Its leadership, complacent in its reliance on the US market, has no contingency plans. Krainer predicts the EU’s disintegration, with nations like Germany, Poland, Hungary, and France forging bilateral ties with the US, China, and Russia. BRICS, with its vision of inclusive prosperity, could serve as a framework for Europe to restructure relations with Russia, a possibility Krainer discussed with Putin at the 2024 Valdai meeting. Though Putin was skeptical, Krainer remains optimistic, seeing BRICS as a magnet for entrepreneurs and consumers seeking alternatives to the West’s declining system.

## A Decentralized Future with BRICS

Alex Krainer’s analysis reveals a Europe in crisis, targeted by US policies and unprepared for a multipolar world, while BRICS emerges as a beacon of growth and decentralization. As Europe grapples with economic decline, political repression, and war hysteria, BRICS offers a model of grassroots globalization, empowering individuals and small businesses to bypass corporate structures. The internet’s role in neutralizing narrative control gives Krainer hope that Europe can avoid a dystopian fate, potentially finding a new path through BRICS-led economic integration. In a world where old structures collapse, the seeds of a more equitable, decentralized future are quietly growing.
