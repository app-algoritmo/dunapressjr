## Gerald Celente on Trump’s War Escalation and Global Economic Chaos

In a sobering interview with Prof. Glenn Diesen, Gerald Celente, a renowned economist, author, and founder of the Trends Journal, delivers a stark warning about the trajectory of global geopolitics and economics. Celente, known for his accurate predictions spanning decades, critiques the decline of journalism, Trump’s betrayal of his peace mandate, and the escalating risk of nuclear conflict driven by failing economies and war-hungry leaders. With wars in Ukraine and the Middle East intensifying, Celente forecasts economic turmoil, a surge in precious metals, and a potential realignment of global alliances, urging caution in an increasingly volatile world.

## The Decline of Journalism: A Propaganda Machine

Celente opens with a scathing critique of modern journalism, calling mainstream media outlets like The New York Times “the toilet paper record.” He contrasts the $6 Sunday edition of the Times—featuring irrelevant stories like student athletes as social media stars in its business section—with the Trends Journal’s $2.50 weekly subscription (offering a 10% discount with code “Glenn”). The decline, Celente argues, began with the 1996 Federal Communications Act under President Bill Clinton, which deregulated media ownership. Today, six companies control 92% of US media, turning journalists into “prostitutes” for corporate and government interests. This consolidation ensures conformity, with no diversity of thought despite the proliferation of outlets, leaving the public to “swallow the propaganda.”

## Trump’s Betrayal: From Peace Mandate to War Profiteer

Celente expresses deep concern over Trump’s actions post-election, noting a stark contradiction between his campaign promises and current policies. Trump, who ran on a peace mandate—vowing to end the Ukraine war in 24 hours, later extended to 100 days—has instead escalated the conflict. By May 2025, Trump secured a mineral deal with Ukraine, gaining access to its rare earth resources, while simultaneously selling weapons to Kyiv. Celente labels this a “new Biden” approach, accusing Trump of fighting Russia “to the last Ukrainian” while extorting Kyiv for economic gain. This betrayal, Celente warns, heightens the risk of nuclear annihilation, as leaders ramp up military budgets amidst failing economies.

In Europe, leaders like Germany’s new Chancellor Friedrich Merz, a former BlackRock executive, are exacerbating the crisis. Germany, in recession for two years and facing another downturn, plans to borrow a trillion dollars to bolster its military, alongside infrastructure repairs. Nordic countries, the UK, and other European nations are similarly increasing defense spending, diverting funds from productive economic sectors. Celente argues that this militarization benefits only the military-industrial complex, not the broader economy, as “bombs and missiles don’t go into the economy—they go into the pockets of the elite.”

## Middle East Tensions: A Path to Global Catastrophe

Celente’s gravest concern is the Middle East, where he predicts a US-Israel attack on Iran, potentially triggering a global catastrophe. Trump’s administration, despite initiating nuclear talks with Iran, has imposed new sanctions, banning countries buying Iranian oil from doing business with the US—a move Celente calls a “setup.” He cites Trump’s statement on May 1, 2025, reported by CNBC, threatening secondary sanctions, even as Iran denies nuclear weapon development, a claim supported by international inspectors. Celente forecasts that the US and Israel will use Iran’s non-compliance as a pretext for war, ignoring the 2015 nuclear deal Trump abrogated during his first term.

Israel’s actions, including airstrikes in Syria and violations of a Lebanon ceasefire, reported by the Financial Times on May 1, 2025, further escalate tensions. Celente highlights Israel’s “Samson Option”—a policy of nuclear retaliation if facing defeat—given its 200-400 nuclear warheads. Iran, with 91 million people and advanced military capabilities, poses a formidable challenge to Israel’s 8 million Jewish population. Celente warns that a US-Israel attack on Iran could mark “the beginning of the end of life on Earth,” as escalation risks nuclear conflict.

## Economic Fallout and Precious Metals Surge

The geopolitical tensions are driving economic instability, reflected in the soaring prices of gold and silver. Celente, who began buying gold in 1978 at $163-$183 an ounce, notes that gold could hit $4,000 this year, driven by a global “safe haven hunt.” The Financial Times reported on May 1, 2025, that Chinese investors are piling into gold ETFs at a record pace, while India, facing tensions with Pakistan, is also stockpiling gold. Silver, critical for technology and heavy industry, is following gold’s upward trend, reaching $32-$33 per ounce from a 2011 peak of $40. Unlike gold, silver isn’t recycled, making its supply constraints a key driver of price increases.

Celente links these trends to economic decline: the US faces negative GDP growth, Germany is in recession, and India’s stock market is faltering. War, he argues, is a historical response to economic failure—World War II followed the Great Depression, and the War on Terror followed the dot-com bust. Today’s leaders, from Trump to Merz, Macron, and Starmer, are “maniacs” escalating conflicts to distract from economic woes, further fueling the precious metals market.

## Shifting Alliances: Japan’s Pivot to China

Trump’s aggressive tactics, including tariffs and what Japan has called “extortion,” are straining US alliances. Japanese officials, frustrated by Trump’s inconsistent demands, have publicly labeled the US an “extortionist,” a term not used in decades. Celente sees this as a turning point, predicting Japan may pivot toward China. With China’s 1.4 billion population dwarfing the US’s 347 million, Japan sees greater economic potential in Beijing, especially as the US becomes an unreliable partner. This shift, Celente suggests, reflects a broader realignment in a multipolar world, where allies seek alternatives to US dominance.

## Trump’s Conflicts of Interest and Global Complicity

Celente exposes Trump’s conflicts of interest, noting his family’s business dealings—Trump Towers in Saudi Arabia and Qatar, and his son’s crypto ventures—supported by over $100 million from the crypto industry during the campaign. These deals, Celente argues, explain Arab nations’ silence on Israel’s genocide in Gaza, which Trump envisions as a “Mediterranean Riviera” after ethnically cleansing Palestinians, as Jared Kushner suggested in March 2024. Celente calls this a “crime syndicate,” where leaders prioritize money and power over human lives, a pattern evident in Netanyahu’s close ties to the Kushner family.

Trump’s cabinet, including Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth—a “clown boy” from Fox News—further signals war escalation. Hegseth’s threats against Iran for supporting the Houthis, despite 800 US airstrikes on Yemen, ignore the Saudi-led war that killed 500,000 Yemenis since 2014, launched under Obama. Celente sees no path for de-escalation under Trump, whose campaign aversion to war was a lie to win votes, as evidenced by his bombing of Yemen and arming Ukraine—actions he previously called mistakes.

## Investment Strategies in a Volatile World

Amidst this turmoil, Celente advises on safe investments. Precious metals—gold and silver—are top choices due to their safe-haven status. Cryptocurrencies, particularly Bitcoin, are also rising, fueled by Trump’s newfound support after receiving crypto industry funds, despite his earlier opposition. Celente warns of the broader socio-political polarization and economic decline in the US, urging investors to diversify as the “crime syndicate” of global leadership prioritizes personal gain over public welfare.

## A World on the Brink

Gerald Celente’s analysis paints a dire picture of a world teetering on the edge of nuclear conflict, driven by failing economies and war-driven leaders. Trump’s betrayal of his peace mandate, coupled with escalations in Ukraine and the Middle East, threatens global stability. The surge in gold and silver reflects this uncertainty, while shifting alliances, like Japan’s pivot to China, signal a multipolar future. Celente’s call for vigilance—investing in precious metals and questioning the “crime syndicate” of global leadership—offers a sobering guide for navigating these unprecedented times.
