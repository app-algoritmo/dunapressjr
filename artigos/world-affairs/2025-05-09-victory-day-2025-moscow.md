## Victory Day 2025: Putin’s Moscow Parade Honors WWII Sacrifice Amid Global Tensions

On May 9, 2025, at 09:56 AM CEST, the YouTube channel *Times Now World* broadcasted a live stream titled *"Victory Day 2025 LIVE: Putin Leads Russia’s Largest Military Parade in Moscow | World News 2025"*, capturing a pivotal moment in global geopolitics. The event, marking the 80th anniversary of the Soviet Union’s victory over Nazi Germany in World War II, was hosted by Russian President Vladimir Putin in Moscow’s Red Square. With Chinese President Xi Jinping as the chief guest and 29 world leaders in attendance, the parade underscored Russia’s military prowess and its strategic alliances, particularly with China, amidst ongoing tensions with the West and the war in Ukraine. The broadcast highlighted the event’s diplomatic significance, the display of advanced weaponry, and the broader implications for international relations.

## A Grand Display of Military Might

The Victory Day Parade on May 9, 2025, was described by *Times Now World* as Russia’s largest to date, a sentiment echoed by Kremlin aide Yuri Ushakov, who noted it as a sign of Russia’s “growing authority in the world.” The parade featured a stunning array of military hardware, including tanks, intercontinental ballistic missiles, and fighter jets trailing the Russian tricolor, as reported by Reuters. Russian troops, alongside contingents from 13 countries—including an honor guard from China’s People’s Liberation Army—marched in a meticulously choreographed display. Posts on X from *RT_com* captured the event’s scale, showing Cossack troops marching and military vehicles preparing on Red Square, with jets painting the sky in Russia’s national colors.

President Putin delivered a speech, surrounded by veterans, emphasizing Russia’s role in standing against “neo-Nazism and militarism,” a narrative he has consistently used to frame the war in Ukraine. Alongside Xi Jinping, Putin signed a joint statement condemning U.S. tariffs and “unilateral restrictive measures,” portraying their partnership as a stabilizing force in a “troubled world,” according to *The Washington Post*. The presence of leaders like Brazil’s Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva, Vietnam’s To Lam, and Belarus’s Aleksandr Lukashenko highlighted Moscow’s diplomatic outreach, despite Western efforts to isolate Russia over its invasion of Ukraine.

## Xi Jinping and Global Leaders: A Diplomatic Spectacle

The attendance of 29 world leaders, as confirmed by the Kremlin, marked a significant diplomatic coup for Moscow. Xi Jinping’s role as the chief guest symbolized the deepening Russia-China alliance, which has grown since their 2022 “no limits” partnership declaration. *CNN* reported that Xi’s four-day visit aimed to “deepen mutual trust” with Putin, with the two leaders signing over 20 bilateral cooperation documents on issues like global strategic stability and biosecurity. Their joint criticism of the U.S., including Trump’s “Golden Dome” missile shield, underscored their united front against Western dominance.

Other attendees included Serbia’s Aleksandar Vučić and Slovakia’s Robert Fico—the only European leaders present, despite EU warnings that their participation could jeopardize Serbia’s membership bid. Leaders from Cuba, Venezuela, and several Central Asian states also joined, reflecting Russia’s pivot to the Global South and its former Soviet allies. *Sky News* noted that this gathering was a chance for Putin to show the West that its isolation efforts had failed, a sentiment echoed in an X post by *RT_com*, where journalist Gemma White argued that the presence of dozens of world leaders signaled a Western failure to isolate Moscow.

## Ukraine’s Response and Security Concerns

The parade unfolded against the backdrop of heightened tensions with Ukraine. Putin had proposed a three-day ceasefire from May 7-9, which Ukraine’s President Volodymyr Zelensky rejected as a “theatrical performance,” according to *CNN*. Zelensky warned that Ukraine could not guarantee the safety of foreign dignitaries in Moscow, a statement that sparked diplomatic friction, with Slovakia’s Fico criticizing it as a threat. *Newsweek* reported Zelensky’s comments as a refusal to take responsibility for events on Russian territory, while Ukrainian drone strikes on Moscow in the days leading up to the parade—disrupting airports and forcing flight diversions—added to the Kremlin’s security concerns, as noted by *The Guardian*.

Despite these tensions, the parade proceeded with tight security measures. *Euronews* highlighted Russia’s fears of a Ukrainian attack, noting the cancellation of Victory Day events in occupied Crimea due to safety risks. However, the Moscow event was deemed too significant to cancel, serving as a platform to project Russian strength and rally domestic support for the war effort.

## Historical Narratives and Propaganda

The Victory Day celebrations were steeped in historical symbolism, with both Russia and Ukraine leveraging World War II narratives to frame the current conflict. Putin’s rhetoric about fighting “neo-Nazism” in Ukraine echoed the Kremlin’s long-standing propaganda, as reported by *The Washington Post*. This narrative was visually reinforced by the parade, which evoked the Soviet victory while showcasing modern military might. An X post by *SkyNews* captured the event’s intensity, noting Putin’s leadership under “tight security” following Ukrainian strikes.

Ukraine, meanwhile, countered with its own narrative. Zelensky’s earlier Knesset speech, as Havryshko noted, claimed Ukrainians rescued Jews during the war, omitting their role in the Holocaust—a distortion Havryshko criticized as revisionism. The Ukrainian government has similarly elevated nationalist figures like the UPA, erasing the Red Army’s contributions, a move Havryshko argued perpetuates denial of historical crimes.

## The Role of the Far Right and Implications for Peace

The broadcast indirectly touched on a critical undercurrent: the influence of Ukraine’s far-right groups, which Havryshko detailed in her interview. Groups like Azov, despite their neo-Nazi roots, have been rehabilitated by Western media as “freedom fighters,” a shift Havryshko attributes to their utility in the war against Russia. The parade’s global audience, including Western leaders who previously criticized such groups, highlights this contradiction. For instance, Azov’s celebration of the Waffen-SS Galicia Division—evident in events like the 2021 Kyiv march under Zelensky’s presidency—clashes with Ukraine’s claim of fighting for democratic values.

This far-right influence complicates peace prospects. Havryshko warned that Zelensky faces threats from these groups if he pursues peace, a fear reinforced by their history of violence and state support since 2022. The *Times Now World* stream, while focusing on the parade’s grandeur, indirectly showcased the geopolitical stakes, as Putin’s alliances with Xi and others signal a counterweight to Western pressure, potentially emboldening Ukraine’s far right to resist any compromise.

## A Stage for Power and Propaganda

The *Times Now World* live stream of the Victory Day 2025 Parade captured a moment of Russian defiance and diplomatic maneuvering. Putin’s display of military might, alongside Xi Jinping and 29 world leaders, projected an image of resilience and global support, challenging Western isolation efforts. However, the event also highlighted the deep historical distortions fueling the Ukraine conflict, with both sides manipulating World War II narratives to justify their actions. As Ukraine’s far-right groups gain influence, supported by a complicit West, the path to peace grows more elusive, leaving the international community to grapple with the consequences of this polarized and militarized standoff.
