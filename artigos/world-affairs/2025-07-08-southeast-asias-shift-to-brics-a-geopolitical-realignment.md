## Southeast Asia’s Strategic Shift to BRICS: A New Global Order

The 2025 BRICS Summit in Rio de Janeiro marks a pivotal moment in global geopolitics, with Southeast Asia emerging as a key player in the expanding influence of the Global South. As discussed by Lena Petrova on her channel *World Affairs in Context*, the rapid integration of Southeast Asian nations into the BRICS framework signals a seismic shift away from Western dominance and toward a multipolar world. With Indonesia as a full member and Malaysia, Thailand, and Vietnam as partner nations, the region is leveraging BRICS to secure economic resilience and strategic autonomy in an era of U.S.-China rivalry.

### BRICS: A Growing Powerhouse

Once a modest coalition of Brazil, Russia, India, China, and South Africa, BRICS has transformed into a formidable bloc. Since 2024, the addition of Egypt, Ethiopia, Indonesia, Iran, and the UAE has elevated its economic clout, representing nearly 40% of global GDP by purchasing power—surpassing the G7’s 30%. This expansion has reshaped BRICS from a symbolic “talking shop” into a credible platform for economic and strategic cooperation, catching the attention of Southeast Asian leaders seeking alternatives to Western-led institutions.

Two initiatives underpin BRICS’ appeal: the New Development Bank (NDB) and the Contingent Reserve Arrangement (CRA). The NDB funds infrastructure and green energy projects, addressing the growth needs of emerging economies like Malaysia and Thailand. Unlike Western institutions often criticized for neocolonial policies, the NDB offers a path to diversify investment portfolios, reducing dependence on China or the West. The CRA, a collective emergency fund, provides a financial safety net for member nations facing currency crises or balance-of-payment pressures. In an unstable global economy, this “insurance policy” is a compelling draw for Southeast Asia.

### Southeast Asia’s Strategic Hedge

Southeast Asia’s engagement with BRICS is not merely economic but a strategic hedge against escalating U.S.-China tensions. Countries like Vietnam and the Philippines are wary of being forced into a binary choice between Washington and Beijing, particularly on contentious issues like Taiwan or the South China Sea. BRICS offers a platform for “cooperative multipolarity,” allowing nations to work with multiple powers without aligning exclusively with East or West. This aligns with the region’s broader push for South-South cooperation, amplifying the voices of developing nations historically marginalized in global decision-making.

Indonesia’s full membership in January 2024 marked a turning point, with Malaysia, Thailand, and Vietnam quickly following as partner nations. This shift reflects a desire to broaden economic defenses and maintain sovereignty in an unpredictable world. As Petrova notes, joining BRICS is about “taking back control” in a global system that often corners smaller nations between superpowers. By aligning with BRICS, Southeast Asian countries gain leverage, partners, and a seat at the table.

### Challenges and Tensions

The pivot to BRICS is not without complications. China’s leading role in the bloc raises concerns for nations like the Philippines and Vietnam, which have territorial disputes with Beijing in the South China Sea. Aligning with BRICS could amplify China’s regional influence, unsettling U.S.-aligned governments wary of Beijing’s diplomatic clout. Additionally, deeper BRICS engagement risks undermining ASEAN centrality, the cornerstone of Southeast Asian diplomacy. ASEAN’s unity could fray if members like Malaysia pursue individual BRICS partnerships, especially as not all ASEAN states agree on engaging the bloc.

Malaysia, as the 2025 ASEAN chair, exemplifies this tightrope. Its invitation to Russian President Vladimir Putin and other BRICS leaders for the October 2025 ASEAN Summit in Kuala Lumpur could spark tensions within ASEAN, particularly with Western-aligned members like the Philippines, which view Russia’s presence as politically risky. These internal divisions highlight the challenge of balancing BRICS engagement with ASEAN’s neutral stance.

### The U.S. as the “Aggressive Elephant”

The United States looms large as a complicating factor. President Trump’s administration has taken a hostile stance toward BRICS, threatening 100% tariffs on member nations if they challenge the U.S. dollar’s dominance or introduce a shared currency. Recent tariffs of up to 49% on some ASEAN members signal Washington’s willingness to punish perceived disloyalty. For ASEAN, which has long maintained neutrality between global powers, closer BRICS ties could provoke a new wave of U.S. protectionism, especially under Trump’s second term, where tariffs are imposed “left and right.”

This aggressive posture risks alienating Southeast Asia, pushing nations toward BRICS as a counterbalance. However, full ASEAN membership in BRICS remains unlikely due to the bloc’s diversity and commitment to neutrality. Instead, individual nations will likely deepen partnerships, prioritizing economic resilience over outright opposition to the West.

### A New Global Order

Southeast Asia’s shift to BRICS is not about choosing sides but about securing sovereignty and options. The region’s leaders see BRICS as a vehicle for economic growth, stability, and diplomatic leverage in a world fraught with uncertainty. By tapping into the NDB and CRA, countries like Indonesia and Malaysia are diversifying their economic strategies, while cooperative multipolarity allows them to navigate U.S.-China tensions without being cornered.

The Rio de Janeiro BRICS Summit underscores the Global South’s refusal to remain on the sidelines. Southeast Asia, once absent from BRICS, is now stepping onto the field, driven by a vision of shared prosperity and independence. As Petrova emphasizes, this realignment is about nations asserting control over their destinies, reshaping the global order in the process. While challenges remain—China’s influence, ASEAN’s unity, and U.S. retaliation—the trajectory is clear: Southeast Asia is embracing BRICS to forge a more equitable and multipolar future.
