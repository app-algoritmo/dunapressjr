After an intense electoral campaign in the harsh German winter, the people have decided: **Friedrich Merz and the CDU/CSU emerge victorious** in the 2025 federal elections. With **29% of the vote**, the Christian Democratic Union (CDU) and its Bavarian ally, the Christian Social Union (CSU), secured a decisive victory, significantly improving their 2021 result (24.1%). The governing coalition of Olaf Scholz, known as the "traffic light" coalition (SPD, Greens, and FDP), suffered a crushing defeat.

Voter turnout was **exceptionally high, reaching 84%**, reflecting the German people's strong desire for political change. However, the political landscape remains uncertain regarding the formation of the next government, as the Bundestag's composition requires strategic alliances.

***

## **The Rise of AfD and the Collapse of SPD and the Greens

Another major highlight of this election was the **exponential growth of AfD**. The party, which received **only 10.3% in 2021**, has now **doubled its vote share**, reaching **between 19% and 20%**. Candidate Alice Weidel led a strong movement against the political establishment, making her party **the second-largest force in the Bundestag**.

However, **AfD will remain isolated**, as none of the traditional parties are willing to govern with them. Nevertheless, this positions AfD as **the main opposition force**, surpassing the SPD and the Greens.

The biggest defeat of the night belonged to the **Social Democratic Party (SPD)**, led by Olaf Scholz. With only **16% of the vote**, the party recorded **the worst result in its 135-year history**. The rejection of Scholz’s administration was evident, marking the end of his political leadership.

The **Greens also suffered a major setback**, with their candidate Robert Habeck receiving only **13% of the vote**. Once a dominant force in progressive politics, the party now faces challenges in maintaining its relevance.

Another major loser was the **FDP**, led by Finance Minister Christian Lindner. In 2021, the party received 11.5% of the vote, but now **it is dangerously close to the 5% threshold**, the minimum required to secure parliamentary representation. Its future remains uncertain, and its presence in the Bundestag could be crucial for government formation.

***

## **The Left’s Unexpected Comeback and BSW’s Failure

Despite the departure of its most prominent leader, Sahra Wagenknecht, **The Left (Die Linke) made a surprising recovery**, securing **about 8% of the vote**, exceeding earlier predictions that placed them below the 5% threshold.

On the other hand, **Wagenknecht’s political movement, BSW**, which had achieved **15% in last year’s state elections**, **failed in its attempt to enter the Bundestag**, securing only **4.5%**. This represents a major setback for the attempt to establish a new populist left in Germany.

***

## **Merz’s Future Government: What Coalitions Are Possible?

Despite his significant victory, **Friedrich Merz still needs to form a viable government**. Several coalition possibilities are being discussed:

- **CDU/CSU-SPD Coalition (Black-Red)**: With the SPD’s electoral collapse, this alternative may be the most viable for political stability.

- **CDU/CSU-Greens Coalition (Black-Green)**: This alliance, once promising, may not secure the necessary parliamentary majority.

- **CDU/CSU-SPD-FDP Coalition (Black-Red-Yellow)**: If the FDP manages to enter the Bundestag, the liberals could play a key role in forming the new coalition.

What is clear is that **Friedrich Merz will be Germany’s next chancellor** and will face the challenge of governing a politically fragmented country with a clear demand for change.

The new government will need to address crucial issues such as **the economy, immigration, security, foreign policy, and energy**, as the German people expect effective measures to restore growth and stability.

The 2025 election marks the **end of the Scholz era** and **the return of CDU/CSU to power** under the leadership of Friedrich Merz. The AfD has emerged as a significant force but remains without governing allies, while the SPD, Greens, and FDP suffer historic defeats.

The next challenge will be **forming a stable coalition** to ensure a functional government. Germany has chosen change – but what comes next remains uncertain.
