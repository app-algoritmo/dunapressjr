## Gaza Burns: Israel’s War, Genocide, and Societal Denial in the Gaza Crisis

In a sobering interview with Professor Glenn Diesen, Gideon Levy, a prominent Israeli journalist and Haaretz columnist, paints a grim picture of Israel’s ongoing assault on Gaza and its profound societal repercussions. Nineteen months after the traumatic Hamas attack on October 7, 2023, Levy argues that Israel’s campaign—marked by relentless bombardment and a humanitarian catastrophe—lacks clear objectives beyond domestic political survival and ideological vengeance. As Gaza faces what Levy terms a genocide, Israel’s internal divisions deepen, yet public discourse remains indifferent to Palestinian suffering. This article draws on Levy’s insights to explore Israel’s elusive goals, the humanitarian crisis in Gaza, and the erosion of moral accountability within Israeli society.

## Israel’s Objectives in Gaza: Revenge, Politics, and Ethnic Cleansing

Levy contends that Israel’s initial response to the October 7 attack was driven by a visceral need to punish Gaza, not just Hamas, for the trauma inflicted. However, 19 months later, with nearly 100 daily deaths—mostly civilians, including 22 children in a single day—the campaign has morphed into a brutal exercise devoid of strategic purpose.

Levy rejects the notion of a planned genocide but asserts that Palestinian lives are treated as “cheap,” mere pawns in a domestic political game. Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu’s coalition, particularly its far-right partners, openly advocates for ethnic cleansing, aiming for a Gaza without Palestinians. Netanyahu’s motives are twofold: ideological alignment with this vision and a pragmatic desire to prolong the war to preserve his fragile government, which faces collapse if hostilities cease.

Levy deems the goal of ethnic cleansing—endorsed by figures like Donald Trump as a legitimate policy—unfeasible. With 2.3 million Palestinians in Gaza and no neighboring country willing to accept them, mass transfer is logistically impossible. Yet, the pursuit of this fantasy has normalized extreme rhetoric and eroded Israel’s moral framework, with devastating consequences for Gaza’s population.

## The Humanitarian Catastrophe in Gaza

The situation in Gaza is dire, with UN statistics painting a harrowing picture: one in five residents faces starvation, and over 90% lack adequate food. Levy personalizes this crisis through the story of a 63-year-old diabetic friend in Gaza, left with just two drops of insulin, emblematic of widespread medical shortages. Bombardments continue unabated, with no discernible purpose beyond perpetuating destruction. Levy questions the endgame, noting that even a ceasefire would likely return Gaza to its pre-war “cage”—a besieged enclave with no plan for governance or reconstruction. The absence of Palestinian leadership, compounded by internal divisions, further dims prospects for a sustainable resolution.

## Israel’s Internal Fractures: A Society in Denial

Within Israel, the war has exposed deep societal rifts, yet Levy finds the lack of opposition to Gaza’s suffering most depressing. While protests against Netanyahu are vocal, focusing on hostage releases and government failures, they rarely address Palestinian victims. The Israeli left and center, Levy argues, are fixated on the hostages—seen as the only “real” victims in Gaza—ignoring the millions enduring starvation and bombardment. No Zionist politician, he notes, speaks out against the daily war crimes, reflecting a consensus on continuing the occupation and apartheid policies.

Tensions between Netanyahu’s government and the military are visible, with former IDF and Mossad officials labeling the prime minister a traitor on television. However, Levy dismisses fears of a civil war, citing the left’s passive nature and the right’s historical monopoly on political violence. The military, deeply integrated into Israeli society and revered as a “holy cow,” remains obedient, unlikely to stage a coup despite frustrations. This unity in supporting the war, even among Netanyahu’s critics, underscores a broader societal refusal to question Israel’s actions.

## Global Repercussions and Regional Tensions

Internationally, Israel faces growing alienation, particularly among global publics, though political elites in the U.S. and Europe remain supportive. Israelis, Levy observes, deflect criticism by framing it as antisemitism, shielding themselves from accountability. This narrative— “the world hates us no matter what”—minimizes concern about actions like airline suspensions or social ostracism abroad. In the region, cooperative governments in Egypt and Jordan face restive populations outraged by Gaza’s plight, but Levy sees no immediate threat of policy shifts, as these regimes prioritize stability.

The war’s escalation risks a broader conflict, with Israel engaged in Lebanon, Syria, and Yemen, and eyeing confrontation with Iran. Levy notes past instances where the military restrained Netanyahu’s ambitions against Iran, but current militancy and dependence on U.S. approval make the outcome uncertain. Trump’s rhetoric oscillates between advocating a nuclear deal with Iran and endorsing aggressive policies, leaving the region’s fate in his unpredictable hands.

## The Path Forward: A Bleak Horizon

Levy sees no viable path to peace without U.S. intervention, specifically from Trump, who holds unparalleled leverage over Israel. A ceasefire and withdrawal from Gaza could occur within days if Trump demands it, potentially securing hostage releases. However, the deeper question—“then what?”—remains unanswered. Gaza’s return to a besieged status quo, with Hamas likely retaining influence, offers no long-term solution. Levy declares the two-state solution “long dead” due to 700,000 Israeli settlers, and alternatives like a single state with equal rights are absent from Israeli discourse. The post-October 7 mindset, viewing Palestinians as subhuman and peace as impossible, entrenches this limbo.

Israel’s resilience—no economic crisis despite disruptions, no mass exodus—masks a troubled mood. Levy attributes this to societal denial and practical barriers to emigration, but warns that the lack of accountability and vision portends further violence. The war’s continuation, driven by Netanyahu’s survival and ideological zeal, risks regional escalation, with Trump as the unpredictable arbiter.

Gideon Levy’s analysis reveals a tragic convergence of purposeless violence in Gaza and moral decay within Israel. The genocide, fueled by political expediency and ideological extremism, unfolds without opposition from a society fixated on its own victimhood. As Gaza burns, Israel turns inward, its divisions overshadowed by a unified refusal to confront its crimes. The absence of Palestinian leadership and Israeli vision, coupled with Trump’s erratic influence, dims hopes for resolution. Levy’s urgent plea to save Gaza’s people underscores the moral imperative to act, yet the path to peace remains obscured by denial, destruction, and geopolitical volatility. Only a decisive shift—led by external pressure or an unlikely Israeli awakening—can halt this descent.
