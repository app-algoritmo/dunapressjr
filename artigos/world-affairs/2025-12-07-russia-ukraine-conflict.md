The Russia Ukraine Conflict has reached a critical juncture, with Russian President Vladimir Putin issuing a direct warning that his country is "ready right now" for war should Europe launch an attack. This stark message comes amid rapid Russian territorial gains in Ukraine and escalating attacks on energy infrastructure and commercial shipping.

In a detailed interview between Professor Glenn Diesen and former CIA analyst Larry Johnson, the experts dissect the shifting dynamics of the conflict, highlighting Russia's military buildup, the failures of Western strategies, and the potential for broader European involvement. As of December 2025, Russia's advances underscore a war of attrition where Moscow holds the upper hand, challenging long-held assumptions in the West about the vulnerability of the Russian economy and military.

The interview, which draws on Johnson's extensive background in intelligence and counterterrorism, paints a picture of a Russia Ukraine Conflict that has evolved far beyond its initial phases. Johnson emphasizes that recent attacks on Russian ships, including those off the coast of Senegal, represent a dangerous escalation. He attributes these incidents to European powers, particularly France, given Senegal's historical ties to French influence.

Putin has labeled such actions as "piracy," outlining a ladder of retaliatory measures that could include destroying Ukrainian ports in Odessa, imposing a full naval blockade, and even sinking foreign vessels from nations like France, Germany, or the UK. This response is not mere rhetoric; it reflects Russia's preparedness to expand the conflict if provoked further in the Russia Ukraine Conflict.

One of the most eye-opening aspects discussed is Russia's territorial progress. In November 2025 alone, Russian forces captured approximately 700 square kilometers of Ukrainian territory—twice the size of the Gaza Strip. This achievement is remarkable when compared to other conflicts. For instance, Israel has struggled to secure 350 square kilometers in Gaza over more than two years, despite facing opponents with limited weaponry and no advanced intelligence support.

In contrast, Ukraine benefits from NATO-supplied arms, artillery, and reconnaissance, yet Russian troops have accelerated their advances across multiple fronts in the Russia Ukraine Conflict. Johnson points out that this surge follows a period of deliberate buildup, where Russia transitioned from a limited special military operation in 2022 to a full-scale attritional warfare strategy.

Initially, Russia entered Ukraine with only 125,000 to 180,000 troops, aiming to pressure Kyiv into negotiations. Tentative agreements were reached in Istanbul, but Western intervention—led by figures like Joe Biden and Boris Johnson—derailed them, shifting the Russia Ukraine Conflict into a prolonged battle. Russia then focused on consolidating key areas like Mariupol and building up its forces through mobilization and recruitment. By September 2022, 320,000 reservists were called up, and training programs expanded the active ground forces to around 750,000 to 800,000 personnel deployed in Ukraine by late 2025. This methodical approach minimized casualties while maximizing industrial output, producing missiles and drones at rates that outpace usage.

Johnson critiques Western analysts, including retired U.S. generals like Ben Hodges and David Petraeus, for underestimating Russia's resilience. They have pushed for long-range missiles to target Russian supply lines, believing it would force a collapse. However, as Johnson notes, Russia has fired thousands of missiles into Ukraine over nearly four years without causing Kyiv's downfall. The West lacks the stockpiles to match this scale, making such strategies illusory in the Russia Ukraine Conflict. Moreover, Russia's economy has adapted; oil and gas revenues now form a declining percentage of GDP, debunking the myth that sanctions or attacks on refineries would cripple Moscow.

### Russian Advances in the Russia Ukraine Conflict

Delving deeper into the military dynamics, the Russia Ukraine Conflict reveals a stark asymmetry in capabilities. Russian offensives now span eight axes, from Sumy and Kharkiv in the north to Donetsk and Zaporizhzhia in the east and south. This multi-pronged assault overwhelms Ukrainian defenses, which suffer from manpower shortages, mass desertions, and depleted NATO weapon stocks. Ukrainian soldiers, often conscripted with minimal training, face well-entrenched Russian units that have captured fortified positions, leaving vast areas undefended.

The pace of Russian gains has accelerated dramatically since September 2025, countering claims of "creeping" progress. Critics in the West argue that slow initial advances indicate weakness, but Diesen and Johnson explain this as a deliberate tactic in attritional warfare. Why risk heavy losses storming lines when grinding down the enemy through sustained pressure achieves the same end? Ukraine's failed 2023 counteroffensive, which resulted in massive casualties against Russian defenses, exemplifies this. Meanwhile, Russia has stockpiled advanced weaponry, including Kinzhal hypersonic missiles, reserved for potential escalations rather than routine use in the Russia Ukraine Conflict.

Europe's response has been one of panic, pushing for negotiations while simultaneously proposing escalatory measures. A leaked 28-point peace plan demands Ukraine field 800,000 troops—more than the combined armies of Germany, France, and the UK, despite Europe's larger population and wealth. Johnson sees this as unrealistic, echoing the deceptive Minsk Agreements, which Merkel and Hollande later admitted were stalling tactics to rearm Ukraine. Russia, having learned from past deceptions, insists on firm demands: Ukrainian neutrality, demilitarization, and no NATO ties. Putin has made clear that negotiations with an "illegitimate" leader like Volodymyr Zelensky are off the table, and any successor must adhere to Ukraine's constitution.

### Western Miscalculations and the Risk of Broader War

The Russia Ukraine Conflict exposes profound miscalculations in Western policy. From the 2014 Maidan events that toppled Ukraine's government to the sabotage of peace talks, the West has pursued regime change in Moscow at Ukraine's expense. Johnson highlights corruption scandals, with estimates of $50-100 billion siphoned from $360 billion in aid, as tools to undermine Zelensky. Leaks suggest U.S. efforts to replace him, possibly with figures like Valerii Zaluzhnyi or Vitali Klitschko, to facilitate a settlement. However, Russia views these maneuvers skeptically, prioritizing security over territorial concessions.

Putin's disdain for Europe is evident; he dismissed rejoining the G8, focusing instead on Eastern partnerships with China and India. Russia's pivot eastward, solidified by deals like the Power of Siberia 2 pipeline, reduces dependence on Europe. In a potential direct confrontation, Russia's 1.5 million-strong army—bolstered without full mobilization—dwarfs European forces. Reserves could swell to 5-10 million, and life in Russia continues normally, without wartime rationing seen in historical conflicts.

The risk of escalation looms large. Attacks on Russian energy assets and ships could provoke strikes on European infrastructure. Johnson warns that Russia is uniquely positioned to devastate Europe militarily, with serial production of advanced missiles like the Kinzhal. If Europe intervenes to halt Russian advances—potentially seizing Odessa, Kyiv, or territory east of the Dnieper River—the consequences could be catastrophic.

As the Russia Ukraine Conflict enters its fourth year, the interview underscores a reality: Russia is winning through attrition, while Ukraine teeters on collapse. Western narratives of Ukrainian victories have crumbled, replaced by desperate calls for peace. Yet, without genuine concessions, the path leads to further instability. The world watches as Putin's warning echoes: Russia is prepared, and Europe must tread carefully to avoid igniting a broader war.

In reflecting on these developments, it's clear that the Russia Ukraine Conflict is not just a regional dispute but a geopolitical realignment. Russia's military and economic adaptations challenge the post-Cold War order, forcing a multipolar world where Eastern alliances gain prominence. The lessons from past deceptions—Minsk, Istanbul—limit Putin's flexibility; any perceived weakness could spark domestic backlash. For Europe and the U.S., acknowledging these shifts is essential to de-escalate.

The Russia Ukraine Conflict continues to evolve, with November 2025's gains signaling momentum for Moscow. As Johnson aptly puts it, the West's push for magic bullets ignores the grind of reality. Peace requires realism, not escalation.
