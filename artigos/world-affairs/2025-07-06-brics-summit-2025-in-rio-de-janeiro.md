## BRICS Summit: A Global Shift in Power and Perspective

As the world watches, the 17th BRICS Summit is set to take place on July 6–7, 2025, in Rio de Janeiro, Brazil—a city known for its vibrant culture and strategic importance in Latin America. This year’s summit arrives at a critical juncture, as the bloc continues to expand its membership and redefine its role in shaping global governance, economic cooperation, and sustainable development. From its origins as a financial acronym coined by Goldman Sachs in 2001, BRICS has evolved into a formidable alliance of emerging economies, now comprising nine full members and several observer states.

With over 56% of the world’s population and representing 44% of global GDP, BRICS is no longer just a counterbalance to Western-dominated institutions like the G7 or IMF. It is increasingly becoming a platform for alternative approaches to international relations, trade, and development. As the host nation, Brazil aims to steer the conversation toward pragmatic solutions, focusing on infrastructure, sustainable finance, climate cooperation, and reforming global governance structures.

***

### Expansion and Diversification: The New BRICS Landscape

What was once an exclusive club of five—Brazil, Russia, India, China, and South Africa—has grown into a dynamic coalition that includes Egypt, Ethiopia, Iran, the United Arab Emirates (UAE), and Indonesia, all of whom joined between 2024 and early 2025. Vietnam became the latest partner nation in June 2025, signaling the growing appeal of the BRICS model.

This expansion reflects not only the increasing influence of the Global South but also the desire of many nations to diversify their geopolitical and economic partnerships. Countries like Colombia, Uzbekistan, and Mexico are reportedly considering formal applications, further cementing BRICS’ gravitational pull.

The inclusion of diverse economies brings both opportunities and challenges. While it enhances the bloc’s collective bargaining power, it also requires careful diplomacy to manage differing interests—from energy policies to digital regulation and security concerns.

***

### Key Themes of the 2025 Summit: Trade, Technology, and Transformation

The agenda for the 2025 BRICS Summit is ambitious and wide-ranging. On the first day, discussions will center around global governance reforms, peace and security, and the role of multilateralism in addressing shared challenges. Economic and financial issues, including the push for local currency trade and alternatives to the U.S. dollar, remain high on the list of priorities.

A major highlight will be the session on artificial intelligence (AI) and its implications for development, labor markets, and data sovereignty. As AI reshapes industries worldwide, BRICS nations aim to foster collaborative frameworks that ensure equitable access to technology and prevent monopolization by a few global players.

On the second day, the focus shifts to climate action, health equity, and preparations for COP30—the UN Climate Change Conference to be hosted by Brazil later in 2025. The summit is expected to produce a joint declaration outlining strategies for financing green transitions, promoting renewable energy, and supporting vulnerable communities affected by climate change.

Another anticipated outcome is the BRICS Partnership for the Elimination of Socially Determined Diseases, which seeks to improve healthcare access across member states through shared research, vaccine development, and public health initiatives.

***

### The Role of the BRICS Development Bank: A Model for the Global South?

One of the most significant institutional developments within the BRICS framework is the New Development Bank (NDB), headquartered in Shanghai. Originally conceived as an alternative to the World Bank and IMF, the NDB has funded numerous infrastructure and sustainability projects across member countries.

In 2025, Brazil hopes to strengthen the bank’s capacity to offer loans in local currencies, reducing dependency on the U.S. dollar and mitigating exchange rate risks. This initiative aligns with broader efforts to promote intra-BRICS trade using national currencies, a move that could have far-reaching implications for global finance.

The NDB’s performance thus far has been mixed, with some critics pointing to delays in project approvals and governance challenges. However, supporters argue that the bank represents a crucial step toward financial sovereignty and development tailored to the needs of emerging economies.

***

### Climate Action and the Road to COP30

Climate change remains one of the most pressing issues facing the planet, and BRICS nations are uniquely positioned to lead the charge. As host of COP30, Brazil is leveraging the BRICS platform to build consensus among developing nations on climate finance, biodiversity protection, and sustainable development goals.

The summit is expected to endorse a common stance on phasing out fossil fuels, although disagreements persist—particularly regarding coal use in India and oil production in Russia. Nevertheless, the bloc’s ability to present a unified voice on climate justice could enhance its credibility in global negotiations.

Brazil’s leadership also aims to highlight the Amazon rainforest’s role in carbon sequestration and biodiversity conservation. A proposed Forest Conservation Fund, backed by BRICS contributions, may provide financial incentives for sustainable land use and indigenous community support.

***

### Geopolitical Dynamics: Moving Beyond Rivalry

While geopolitical tensions continue to shape international relations, Brazil has made a deliberate effort to shift the BRICS dialogue away from rivalry and toward cooperation. President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva has emphasized the need for economic pragmatism and shared development goals, urging members to find common ground despite their differences.

Notably absent from the summit are Russian President Vladimir Putin and Chinese President Xi Jinping, who will participate virtually. Their absence underscores the complex diplomatic landscape in which BRICS operates, balancing internal cohesion with external pressures.

Nonetheless, the summit provides a unique opportunity for member states to engage in behind-the-scenes diplomacy. Last year’s summit in Kazan saw billions of dollars in bilateral deals signed, demonstrating that BRICS can facilitate tangible economic outcomes beyond symbolic declarations.

***

### The Future of BRICS: A Platform for Choice, Not Confrontation

As the world becomes more fragmented and unpredictable, BRICS offers a compelling vision: a multipolar order based on mutual respect, shared prosperity, and democratic participation. Rather than positioning itself as an adversary to Western institutions, BRICS presents itself as a complementary force—one that empowers developing nations to shape their own destinies.

The bloc’s strength lies in its diversity and adaptability. While challenges remain—including governance reforms, policy coordination, and managing competing interests—BRICS has proven its resilience and relevance.

For many in the Global South, BRICS represents more than just an economic alliance; it symbolizes hope for a future where development is driven by local priorities rather than dictated by distant capitals. As the 2025 summit unfolds in Rio de Janeiro, the world will be watching to see if BRICS can deliver on its promise of a more inclusive, equitable, and sustainable global order.
