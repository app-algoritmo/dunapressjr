## Trump Ukraine Peace Plan: Diplomacy or Deception in the Russia-Ukraine War

In a recent interview with Professor Glenn Diesen, international affairs analyst and historian Gilbert Doctorow dissects the chaotic landscape of proposed peace talks to end the Russia-Ukraine war. After three years of a Western diplomatic boycott of Russia, the sudden push for negotiations—marked by a European ultimatum for a 30-day unconditional ceasefire and talk of a high-level meeting in Istanbul—has sparked confusion and skepticism. Doctorow argues that U.S. President Donald Trump’s approach, characterized by deliberate ambiguity and contradictory signals, creates a “fog” that may either outmaneuver opponents or lead to diplomatic failure. This article explores Doctorow’s analysis of Trump’s tactics, European posturing, and the broader implications for the conflict’s resolution.

## The European Ultimatum: Posturing Without Power

Doctorow begins by critiquing the European “coalition of the willing” for issuing an ultimatum demanding a 30-day ceasefire without political discussions or direct engagement with Moscow. He describes this as a “debacle” lacking diplomatic substance, driven by leaders like Germany’s Chancellor Olaf Scholz who seek to appear tough to domestic audiences. With Russia facing over 20,000 sanctions and Europe’s armories depleted, further threats lack credibility. Doctorow argues that this posturing reflects Europe’s delusional belief in its leverage, despite Russia’s battlefield advances, particularly in collapsing Ukrainian front lines.

The Europeans’ refusal to “pick up the phone” to Moscow underscores their strategic incoherence. Doctorow suggests that this ultimatum is less about peace and more about saving face amid a losing war. He speculates that some European leaders may be stalling to delay Ukraine’s EU membership, which could disrupt subsidy-dependent economies like Poland and France. However, the absence of military or economic wherewithal undermines their threats, rendering the ultimatum a hollow gesture.

## Trump’s Fog of Confusion: Strategy or Chaos?

Central to Doctorow’s analysis is Trump’s unconventional approach to negotiations. By deploying two envoys—Steve Witkoff, aligned with Russia’s position, and General Keith Kellogg, echoing European and Ukrainian demands—Trump sows confusion among allies and adversaries alike. Kellogg’s proposal, which includes a 15-km mutual withdrawal and NATO troops as “neutral” observers under an unconditional ceasefire, ignores Russia’s core demand for Ukrainian neutrality. Doctorow finds this framework illogical, given the U.S.’s role as a co-belligerent supplying Ukraine with arms and intelligence.

Doctorow posits that Trump’s strategy is to expose the absurdity of Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky’s demands, which cast Ukraine as the victor despite its military losses. By orchestrating a scenario where Zelensky’s conditions—such as reparations and war tribunals—are revealed as untenable, Trump could denounce him and wash his hands of the conflict. Alternatively, if Witkoff’s Russia-friendly approach prevails, Zelensky might be forced to resign, paving the way for a successor to accept defeat. However, Doctorow questions whether Trump’s “illogical” tactics reflect a cunning plan or susceptibility to the last voice in his ear, citing detractors who view him as inconsistent.

## Zelensky’s Istanbul Gambit: Panic or Publicity Stunt?

Zelensky’s abrupt announcement that he would fly to Istanbul to meet Putin with just 48 hours’ notice baffles Doctorow. After three years of refusing dialogue, this move appears either as panic driven by collapsing front lines or a calculated stunt to portray himself as a peacemaker to Trump, who has expressed frustration with Zelensky’s resistance to negotiations. Doctorow leans toward the latter, suggesting Zelensky expects Putin to decline, allowing Ukraine to blame Russia and secure more Western support.

The logistics of such a meeting are problematic. Putin, Doctorow asserts, would only attend if Trump were present, ensuring negotiations with the U.S., Ukraine’s “curator,” rather than Zelensky, whom Russian media dismiss as a dependent figure. Trump’s Truth Social post urging Zelensky to accept Putin’s invitation adds to the intrigue, but Doctorow doubts a substantive summit could materialize without extensive preparation. Instead, he sees this as part of Trump’s stage management to expose Zelensky’s intransigence.

## The War’s Endgame: Political Collapse, Not Battlefield Victory

Doctorow predicts the war will end not through a Russian military triumph but via Ukraine’s political collapse, triggered by Trump halting U.S. arms shipments and refusing to sell weapons to Europe for Ukraine. This move, if executed, could end the conflict within months, as Ukraine lacks the resources to continue. He envisions Ukraine’s military pushing Zelensky out, likely without violence, as the president flees to avoid overthrow. Doctorow dismisses notions of a swift Russian offensive, citing Putin’s attrition strategy and the transformative role of drone warfare, which limits large-scale troop movements and favors skilled operators over mass armies.

The shift to drone-centric warfare, employed effectively by both sides, has altered battlefield dynamics. Russia’s numerical advantage is less decisive, and Ukraine’s dwindling manpower cannot sustain prolonged fighting. Doctorow argues that the war’s resolution lies in Kyiv’s “halls of power,” where military frustration with Zelensky’s leadership could precipitate regime change.

## Implications for NATO and Transatlantic Relations

Doctorow foresees significant consequences for NATO and U.S.-European relations if the war ends. A key European fear is that Trump will deprioritize Europe, acting on his campaign rhetoric to reduce U.S. commitments. Doctorow suggests Trump could dismantle NATO’s threatening posture by renouncing the Biden-Schulz agreement to deploy nuclear-armed medium-range missiles in Germany, in exchange for Russia withdrawing its nuclear missiles from Europe’s borders. This move, addressing Russia’s concerns about NATO’s encroachment, could force Europe to negotiate a new continental security architecture, effectively neutering NATO’s current role without formally exiting the alliance.

Such a restructuring would expose Europe’s defense vulnerabilities, as France and the UK would also need to remove their nuclear arsenals. Doctorow argues that this aligns with Russia’s original grievance—NATO’s potential to station missiles in Ukraine capable of striking Moscow in five minutes—which precipitated the war. By resolving this issue, Trump could broker a lasting peace, though Doctorow remains skeptical of his logical consistency.

## Russia’s Economic Resilience Amid Sanctions

Doctorow’s observations from a recent trip to St. Petersburg highlight Russia’s surprising economic growth despite unprecedented Western sanctions. He attributes this to a dual economic strategy: a market-based approach led by Central Bank Governor Elvira Nabiullina, who raised interest rates to 21% to curb inflation, and a statist approach where the Finance Ministry subsidizes credits to key industries. This “complex management” has yielded a 4% growth rate, defying recessionary pressures.

Russia’s sanction-proofing since 2014, including import substitution, technological sovereignty, and conservative fiscal policies, has insulated its economy. Doctorow marvels at the availability of diverse imports—like Iranian watermelons and Greek strawberries—in Russian supermarkets, facilitated by alternative payment systems bypassing SWIFT. He credits Putin’s re-industrialization efforts, accelerated by sanctions, for creating a model Trump admires and seeks to emulate through tariffs to boost U.S. manufacturing.

Gilbert Doctorow’s analysis paints a picture of a war mired in diplomatic deception and strategic confusion, with Trump’s fog-like tactics at its core. While Europe’s ultimatums and Zelensky’s stunts lack substance, Trump’s potential to halt U.S. support could force Ukraine’s political collapse, ending the war within months. The broader implications—a restructured NATO and a reoriented transatlantic partnership—hinge on Trump’s ability to act decisively. Russia’s economic resilience, meanwhile, underscores its capacity to withstand Western pressure, strengthening its negotiating position. As the Istanbul talks loom, the question remains whether Trump’s gambit will clarify the path to peace or deepen the fog of failure. Diplomacy, not ultimatums, is the only way forward, but its success depends on leaders navigating beyond deception.
