## Scott Ritter on Why Ukraine War Negotiations Will Fail: A Deep Dive into NATO’s Role and Trump’s Strategic Ambiguity

In a compelling interview, hosted by Prof. Glenn Diesen, Scott Ritter, a former US Marine Corps intelligence officer and UN weapons inspector, provides a stark analysis of why negotiations to end the Ukraine war are doomed to fail. Ritter, drawing on his extensive military and diplomatic experience, highlights the irreconcilable positions of the parties involved, the US’s historical complicity in the conflict, and the internal divisions within the Trump administration. He also touches on the broader geopolitical landscape, including the Middle East, where the US’s strategic priorities are shifting at the expense of Palestinian lives.

## The Ukraine War: A Conflict with No Room for Concession

Ritter commends the Trump administration for engaging with Russia—a significant departure from the Biden administration’s refusal to maintain diplomatic ties. Early on, Trump’s team acknowledged the reality of the conflict, admitting that NATO expansion and Zelensky’s actions were major drivers of the war. Secretary of State Marco Rubio even described it as a proxy war between the US and Russia, with Ukraine as the battleground. This recognition, Ritter argues, should pave the way for meaningful negotiations by addressing the root causes, such as NATO’s role and the 2014 Maidan coup.

However, Ritter sees a fatal flaw in the US approach: it demands concessions from Russia, the aggrieved party and military victor, while absolving NATO and Europe of responsibility. Russia, with its strategic advantage and clear demands—Ukrainian neutrality and territorial concessions—has no incentive to compromise. Ritter emphasizes that Russia did not invite the US to mediate; as the primary aggressor through its proxy, the US lacks moral authority to dictate terms. The focus on Crimea in negotiations, Ritter speculates, reflects Trump’s desire to deflect blame, attributing its loss to Obama while avoiding responsibility for conceding other territories like Donetsk and Lugansk, which would tarnish his image.

## Trump’s Bipolar Policy: A Recipe for Failure

Ritter identifies a critical dysfunction within Trump’s administration, describing it as a “bipolar national security team” at war with itself. On one side, figures like Keith Kellogg and Mike Waltz, alongside Rubio, remain pro-NATO and sympathetic to Ukraine, clinging to the goal of using Ukraine to weaken Russia. On the other, diplomats like Steve Witoff, supported by Pete Hegseth and JD Vance, advocate for ending the conflict and normalizing relations with Russia. This lack of unified messaging—Witoff negotiating with Putin while Kellogg contradicts him on TV—creates confusion, making it impossible for Europe to align with a coherent US policy.

Europe, Ritter notes, gravitates toward the pro-NATO faction, hoping to sustain the conflict. However, without Trump exercising executive control to unify his team, Ritter predicts the collapse of the Ukraine peace initiative. He foresees Trump walking away from the conflict, leaving Ukraine and NATO to face the consequences. Russia will then impose harsher terms militarily, likely leading to Ukraine’s surrender and the end of its sovereignty as a state—a scenario Ritter sees as inevitable given Russia’s determination to eradicate Ukrainian nationalism and implement constitutional changes to prevent the resurgence of Nazi ideology, particularly that of Stepan Bandera.

## NATO’s Role and Europe’s Reluctance

Ritter criticizes the US for treating NATO as an innocent third party, despite its central role in the conflict. Trump’s first term saw expanded military assistance to Ukraine, including offensive weapons and the construction of 20 CIA bases aimed at undermining Russia—a policy Ritter calls “Donald Trump’s war.” Yet, current negotiations fail to demand concessions from NATO, such as halting weapon supplies or troop deployments during a ceasefire. Ritter questions why the US pressures Zelensky to accept non-membership in NATO without addressing NATO’s own actions, arguing that NATO should directly negotiate with Russia to resolve the conflict.

Europe, meanwhile, remains intransigent, demanding an unconditional ceasefire without a political settlement—a stance Ritter sees as a refusal to face reality. The Europeans’ attachment of psychological, moral, and economic value to Ukraine, despite its inevitable collapse, complicates negotiations. Ritter suggests that strong American leadership could force Europe to abandon Ukraine by threatening to withdraw US support entirely, but Trump’s reluctance to confront his own role in the conflict hinders this approach.

## The Middle East: Sacrificing Gaza for Strategic Goals

Ritter also addresses the Middle East, where the US is pursuing negotiations with Iran while continuing to support Israel’s actions in Gaza. He asserts that the genocide in Gaza could stop with a single phone call from Trump, as demonstrated by Steve Witoff’s earlier success in securing a ceasefire.

However, Trump’s pro-Israel stance—bolstered by a powerful Zionist lobby and a cabinet more pro-Israel than any in modern history—means he is unlikely to pressure Netanyahu. Ritter predicts that Trump will prioritize a nuclear agreement with Iran to eliminate the prospect of military conflict, allowing the US to focus on China. This agreement will come at the expense of Palestinians, as Trump gives Israel a “green light” to continue its operations in Gaza, Beirut, and Syria—actions that do not require direct US military involvement.

Ritter highlights the chilling reality of US policy: criticism of Israel is becoming illegal, reflecting the lobby’s control over American politics. Netanyahu, lacking the capacity to attack Iran independently due to Iran’s ballistic missile deterrence, has no choice but to accept Trump’s terms. However, this strategic pivot underscores a broader US goal of disentangling from the Middle East, mirroring its approach to Ukraine, to focus on China as the primary geopolitical rival.

## A Grim Future for Ukraine and Beyond

Scott Ritter’s analysis paints a bleak picture of the Ukraine war’s trajectory, with negotiations doomed by the US’s failure to address NATO’s role, Trump’s internal divisions, and Europe’s refusal to concede. Russia, as the victor, will dictate terms, likely leading to Ukraine’s dissolution and a harsh reckoning for its nationalist factions. In the Middle East, the US’s strategic withdrawal will sacrifice Palestinian lives to secure peace with Iran, prioritizing China over regional entanglements. Ritter’s insights reveal a world where strategic ambiguity and political dysfunction undermine peace efforts, leaving Ukraine and Gaza as tragic casualties of shifting global priorities.
