## How Zelensky Dismantled Democracy in Ukraine: Diana Panchenko’s Revelations

In a powerful interview with Professor Glenn Diesen, acclaimed Ukrainian journalist Diana Panchenko, named Journalist of the Year in 2020, exposes the erosion of democracy in Ukraine under President Volodymyr Zelensky. Once a leading voice, Panchenko was forced to flee due to death threats, criminal charges, and personal sanctions imposed by Zelensky for advocating peace and equality. She describes an authoritarian regime that silences dissent, jails opposition, and fuels anti-Russian hatred, while Western media ignores these violations, promoting a war-justifying narrative. This article delves into Panchenko’s revelations about repression, corruption, and the manipulation of Ukrainian identity, questioning the post-war future.

## The Destruction of Free Speech

Panchenko’s story epitomizes Ukraine’s crackdown on free expression. A year before the 2022 war, her TV channel and two others were shuttered for allegedly violating the constitution. Journalists like her, who called for peace and equal rights, were marginalized. Her advocacy for negotiations led to criminal charges carrying life imprisonment, personal sanctions from Zelensky—freezing her bank accounts and seizing her property—and death threats. Nationalists besieged her home, making it unsafe to leave. Forced to flee, she lost all citizenship rights, including voting, by Zelensky’s decree, not a court ruling.

This repression extends to thousands. Panchenko cites 400 documented political prisoners, with estimates exceeding 5,000, detained for expressing opinions or liking social media posts. She recounts the case of Pavlo, a disabled young man, jailed for “treason” in an online chat, dying in custody after torture and medical neglect. Such stories, received in “hundreds and thousands” daily, reveal secret prisons, torture, and missing journalists, obscured by Western media’s silence.

## Zelensky’s Dictatorship: Purging Opposition and Foreign Control

Panchenko labels Zelensky a dictator, consolidating power by eliminating opposition. Since the 2014 Western-backed coup, political murders—like Oles Buzyna’s—and massacres, such as Odessa’s, intimidated dissenters. Under Zelensky, figures like Viktor Medvedchuk lost citizenship, while others, like MP Oleksandr Dubinsky, were imprisoned. Assassinations, including Kryvyi Rih’s mayor Oleksandr Pavlov, and attempts, like the mercury poisoning of Constitutional Court head Oleksandr Tupytskyi, reveal a pattern of political violence.

U.S. influence is pivotal. Appointments like American Natalie Jaresko as finance minister in 2014, granted citizenship on the same day, and the dismissal of Prosecutor General Viktor Shokin for probing Burisma, linked to Joe Biden’s son, highlight external control. Shokin, Panchenko warns, faces life-threatening risks for his knowledge. Ukrainian politicians, reliant on U.S. funding, are “puppets” in a tolerated corruption system, easily blackmailed due to their embezzlement.

## Manipulating Identity and Systemic Corruption

Panchenko details a state campaign to reshape Ukrainian identity, fostering anti-Russian hatred since 1991. Schoolbooks portray Russia as a historical enemy, while older generations, recalling peaceful coexistence, are sent to the front—soldiers’ average age is 47. The Ukrainian Orthodox Church is targeted, with priests like Metropolitan Ionafan jailed for their faith, severing cultural ties with Russia. Language laws and cultural suppression marginalize Russian speakers, particularly in Donbas, where 2013 resistance sought only linguistic rights.

Corruption is rampant. Panchenko estimates Zelensky pockets billions in bribes, including $10 billion from phone fraud and $20 billion from conscription exemptions. Delayed U.S. audits, banking on a rare earths deal, perpetuate this theft, with no benefit to Ukrainians. Media, funded by Western grants—like $150,000 monthly to a “free speech” journalist—amplifies anti-Russian narratives, while society lives in fear and poverty.

## The True Ukraine: A Cry for Peace

Contrary to Western media’s heroic portrayal, Panchenko reveals an exhausted Ukraine craving peace, not victory. Protests by mothers and wives against forced conscription are crushed, with filming banned under threat of jail. Violent recruitment snatches men from streets, with bribes up to $20,000 for freedom. Intimidated by security services and nationalists, society dares not speak, while the economy collapses amid fraud and misappropriated aid.

Panchenko laments the “horrendous experiment” to erase Ukrainian identity, turning the country into a geopolitical battlefield. Western “support” rhetoric is hypocritical, especially as leaders like Germany’s Bruno Kahl suggest prolonging the war five years to rearm Europe, treating Ukrainians as disposable. Yulia Tymoshenko, once a Western darling, reflects growing resentment, condemning such cynicism.

## Post-Zelensky Future: An Uncertain Path

Panchenko sees potential for a military-led opposition from figures like Kyrylo Budanov or Andriy Biletsky, who command public and military trust. A coup, however, hinges on U.S. approval, given Washington’s grip on Ukrainian politics. Without financial audits, Zelensky’s corruption persists, delaying accountability. Panchenko fears escalation by Kiev and European forces, risking nuclear confrontation by provoking Russia. President Alexander Lukashenko’s anecdote about Putin’s refusal to bomb Kyiv, deemed sacred, underscores Russia’s restraint.

Post-war, Panchenko predicts a divided Ukraine, resentful of Western betrayal. The true Ukraine she represents yearns for peace and identity, but reclaiming its roots demands a colossal effort. Conversations like this, she believes, are vital to exposing truth and pressing for war’s end.

Diana Panchenko’s revelations unveil Zelensky’s dictatorship, silencing dissent, jailing opponents, and manipulating identity to serve Western interests. Global media, complicit in ignoring political prisoners, torture, and corruption, perpetuates a heroic narrative masking real suffering. As Ukrainians plead for peace, Western refusal to negotiate reflects geopolitical cynicism. Ukraine’s post-Zelensky future hinges on breaking foreign control and confronting corruption, but the path requires courage and global pressure to restore democracy and humanity.
