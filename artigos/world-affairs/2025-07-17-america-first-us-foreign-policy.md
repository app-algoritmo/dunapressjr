## US Foreign Policy: How "America First" Fuels U.S. Proxy Wars Against Russia, China, and Iran

The rhetoric of "America First" under Donald Trump's presidency has been presented as a pivot toward prioritizing domestic prosperity and ending foreign entanglements. However, as discussed in a compelling interview between Professor Glenn Diesen and analyst Brian Berletic, a former U.S. Marine, this slogan appears to mask a continuation of aggressive U.S. foreign policy aimed at maintaining global primacy. The interview reveals how the U.S., under the guise of "America First," is perpetuating conflicts with Russia, China, and Iran, leveraging proxy wars and strategic sequencing to counter a rising multipolar world.

### The Illusion of Peace: Rebranding U.S. Imperialism

Trump's 2024 campaign capitalized on promises to end wars and restore America's economic might. However, Berletic argues that these promises were a rebranding of long-standing U.S. imperial ambitions. He points to policy papers from think tanks like the Heritage Foundation’s Project 2025, the Marathon Initiative, and the America First Policy Institute, which explicitly outline a strategy of "division of labor" and "strategic sequencing." These documents advocate for escalating NATO funding, shifting military responsibilities to Europe, and redirecting U.S. resources toward confronting China, all while maintaining pressure on Russia and Iran.

Berletic highlights that this approach is not new. He draws parallels with past administrations, noting that Barack Obama similarly promised to end wars but continued U.S. military engagements. The "America First" narrative, according to Berletic, is a tactic to garner public support while pursuing an agenda of global dominance. For instance, Trump’s decision to continue supplying weapons to Ukraine, despite campaign promises to freeze the conflict, underscores this continuity. The Financial Times reported that allies are being prepared for war with China, and attacks on Iran are justified as part of this "America First" framework, revealing a pattern of sustained aggression.

### The Ukraine Proxy War: A U.S.-Orchestrated Conflict

The war in Ukraine serves as a prime example of U.S. strategy to contain Russia. Berletic asserts that this conflict is not a misunderstanding between Ukraine and Russia but a deliberate U.S.-engineered proxy war. Since the 2014 coup, the U.S. has captured Ukraine’s political, military, and intelligence apparatus, with the CIA reportedly controlling key operations, including assassinations targeting Russian figures. The New York Times has documented U.S. generals at the top of Ukraine’s chain of command, further evidencing American oversight.

The U.S. strategy, as outlined in the 2019 RAND Corporation paper "Extending Russia," aims to overextend Russia in conflicts along its periphery, such as Ukraine and Syria. By tying Russia down, the U.S. weakens its ability to support allies like Syria, which Berletic notes was toppled as a result of Russia’s diverted focus. Trump’s rhetoric of a ceasefire or neutrality for Ukraine, initially suggested by figures like Secretary of Defense Pete Hegseth, was, according to Berletic, a deceptive tactic to buy time for the U.S. to pivot to other fronts, such as Iran and China. Russia’s refusal to accept a ceasefire reflects an understanding of this deception, as it would only allow the U.S. to regroup and rearm.

### Europe’s Role: A Burdened Ally

A key component of U.S. strategy is shifting the financial and military burden onto Europe. Berletic explains that the U.S. is pressuring European nations to increase NATO spending to 5% of GDP, effectively "dumping" the Ukraine conflict onto them. This allows the U.S. to conserve resources for confrontations with China while maintaining its proxy war against Russia. However, this approach is straining Europe’s economies and political legitimacy. The interview highlights the paradox: European leaders, captured by U.S. influence, comply with policies that undermine their own interests, such as severing ties with Russia’s affordable energy supplies and committing to costly military engagements.

Before 2014, Europe enjoyed a constructive relationship with Russia, with projects like the Nord Stream pipelines fostering economic stability. The U.S.-instigated conflict in Ukraine disrupted this, reasserting American control over European institutions. Berletic argues that European compliance stems from a lack of sovereignty, with bureaucracies like the European Union serving Washington’s interests over those of European citizens. This dynamic raises questions about how long Europe can sustain this burden before political and economic cracks emerge.

### Targeting Iran and China: A Broader Strategy

The interview also addresses U.S. actions against Iran and China, which are integral to its strategy of containing multipolar powers. Berletic notes that the U.S. planned a surprise attack on Iran, described as a “decapitation strike,” while feigning diplomatic engagement. This mirrors the deception used in Ukraine, where ceasefire talks were a ruse to pause conflicts temporarily. In the Middle East, the U.S. is arming proxies, including al-Qaeda affiliates in Syria, to destabilize Iran and its allies, a policy that began under the Bush administration and continued through Obama, Trump, and Biden.

China faces similar pressures, with the U.S. targeting its economic and territorial integrity. The Belt and Road Initiative, a cornerstone of China’s global influence, is under attack through U.S.-backed militants in Pakistan and Myanmar. Despite recognizing Taiwan as part of China under the One China policy, the U.S. stations troops there and builds up military presence along China’s coast. Berletic emphasizes that China is already “under siege,” countering perceptions that it is not doing enough to support Russia and Iran. China’s economic support for these nations, alongside military cooperation like North Korea’s aid to Russia, demonstrates a coordinated resistance to U.S. hegemony.

### The Multipolar Response: A United Front

Russia, China, Iran, and North Korea are increasingly aligning to counter U.S. aggression. Berletic points out that these nations are not seeking to replicate NATO but to foster a multipolar world order where power is balanced. China’s economic might supports Russia and Iran against Western sanctions, while North Korea provides military assistance, such as artillery shells and troops for Russia’s operations. Iran’s ballistic missile capabilities, bolstered by Russian and Chinese support, serve as a deterrent against U.S. and Israeli attacks. This coordination is a direct response to U.S. policies that aim to isolate and weaken these nations sequentially.

The Munich Security Summit highlighted concerns about this growing alignment, with U.S. figures like Keith Kellogg noting the tightening security cooperation between Russia, China, and North Korea. However, the U.S. continues its confrontational approach, which Berletic argues is self-defeating. By pushing these nations closer together, the U.S. is accelerating the shift toward multipolarity, as evidenced by increased trade in local currencies among China, Brazil, and others, reducing reliance on the U.S. dollar.

### The Limits of Empire and the Path Ahead

The interview underscores the limitations of U.S. strategy. Europe’s economic woes and declining political legitimacy, coupled with the exposure of America’s weakened military-industrial base, suggest that sustaining multiple proxy wars is unsustainable. Berletic warns that the U.S. is overextending itself, a historical pattern of imperial decline. However, he cautions against complacency, noting that multipolarity requires active resistance from the global community to counter U.S. ambitions.

Trump’s failure to deliver on peace promises risks alienating both domestic supporters and international actors who hoped for a shift in U.S. policy. As Russia, China, and Iran shed illusions of negotiating with the U.S., they are likely to push back harder, potentially escalating tensions. The interview concludes with a sobering reminder: the real battle is not between political factions within the U.S. but between a unipolar world order and an emerging multipolar one.
