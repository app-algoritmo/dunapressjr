## U.S.-UK Trade Deal on Victory Day 2025: Trump Hails Historic Agreement

On May 8, 2025, at 11:23 AM CEST, President Donald J. Trump announced a landmark trade agreement with the United Kingdom during a White House press conference, coinciding with the U.S. commemoration of Victory Day for World War II, marking the 80th anniversary of the Allied triumph over Nazi Germany. The event, broadcast live and shared on YouTube, featured Trump alongside U.K. Prime Minister Keir Starmer, who joined via speakerphone, celebrating a "breakthrough trade deal" that Trump described as "tremendous" for both nations. The agreement, rooted in "reciprocity and fairness," aims to boost market access, protect jobs, and strengthen economic and security ties, reinforcing the historic "special relationship" between the U.S. and U.K. on a day symbolizing their shared wartime victory.

## A Historic Announcement on Victory Day

Trump opened the conference by emphasizing the symbolic timing, noting, "This is a very special day because it’s Victory Day, World War II, May 8th," and highlighting the serendipity of signing the trade deal on the same day the Allies declared victory in Europe 80 years ago. He acknowledged the U.S. and U.K.’s roles as "great allies" in that victory, calling it "the greatest victory of them all." Starmer echoed this sentiment, stating, "It was about this time of day exactly 80 years ago that Winston Churchill announced victory in Europe," underscoring the enduring partnership now extended to trade. The U.S. lost over 250,000 soldiers in WWII, while the Soviet Union’s staggering 26-27 million deaths were commemorated in Moscow’s parade the following day, highlighting competing global narratives of sacrifice. Trump also mentioned a recently ratified rare earth deal with Ukraine, signaling his administration’s broader trade strategy.

## Details of the U.S.-UK Trade Agreement

The trade deal, the first since Trump’s "Liberation Day" tariffs on April 2, 2025, opens significant market access for both nations. Secretary of Commerce Howard Lutnick detailed that the U.K., the world’s sixth-largest economy and the U.S.’s fourth-largest export market, will provide $5 billion in new opportunities for American exporters, particularly in agriculture. U.S. beef exports gain a tariff-free quota of 13,000 metric tonnes, ethanol tariffs drop from 19% to zero, and non-tariff barriers—previously deemed "unfairly discriminatory"—are reduced. The U.K. benefits from a lowered U.S. tariff on autos from 25% to 10% for 100,000 vehicles, protecting brands like Rolls-Royce and Jaguar, and tariff-free access for Rolls-Royce engines in Boeing planes, with a $10 billion Boeing order announced later that day by a U.K. airline.

The deal also aligns the U.K. with U.S. economic security priorities, a "first-of-its-kind" move, ensuring a shared industrial base, export controls, and protections for key sectors like steel. The U.K. nationalized British Steel as part of the agreement, adopting U.S.-style tariffs and quotas to revive its industry, saving thousands of jobs. However, a 10% baseline tariff on most British goods remains, with final details still being finalized, as Starmer noted, “We can finish hashing out some of the details.” Economists like Michael Pearce from Oxford Economics estimate a modest 6% increase in U.S. exports to the U.K., which totaled $80 billion in 2024, but warn of limited overall impact amid broader tariff-driven economic risks.

## Diplomatic and Strategic Implications

The agreement reinforces the U.S.-U.K. "special relationship," with Trump calling the U.K. "one of our greatest allies" and Starmer emphasizing their closeness in defense, intelligence, and now trade. Starmer highlighted the tech synergy, noting both nations host trillion-dollar tech sectors, and proposed a future technology partnership to drive innovation and jobs. The deal, negotiated in just 45 days—a record Trump attributed to his dealmaking acumen—sets a template for fair trade, though Trump clarified the 10% tariff rate is not a universal standard, with countries like China facing 145%. The U.K. ambassador praised Trump’s 11th-hour intervention, which pushed the deal “to another level,” ensuring mutual benefits.

The announcement came amid global trade tensions, following Trump’s universal 10% tariff and higher rates for specific nations. The U.K. deal, finalized before the July 8 deadline, contrasts with the European Union’s struggles, which Trump criticized for treating the U.S. “extremely unfairly.” Upcoming talks with China, led by Scott Bessent in Switzerland, were described as “substantive,” with Trump expressing optimism for a “very good relationship” despite past disruptions from COVID-19. The deal also indirectly responds to Russia’s Victory Day Parade on May 9, where Putin hosted 29 leaders, signaling a Western counterweight to Moscow’s influence.

## Historical Context and Domestic Challenges

Victory Day provided a poignant backdrop, with Trump renaming May 8 to honor WWII’s end, though his broader push to rebrand Veterans Day as “Victory Day for World War I” drew criticism for overshadowing veterans. The U.S. and U.K.’s wartime alliance was a recurring theme, with Starmer noting celebrations 80 years ago when “people went out into the streets, putting bunting up.” Trump’s administration leveraged this history to frame the deal as a continuation of shared values, though his domestic focus included economic revitalization. He touted a $10 trillion investment surge in the U.S., driven by tariffs, particularly in chips and autos, aiming to reclaim industries lost to countries like Taiwan.

Trump faced domestic scrutiny, with his approval ratings underwater and economic concerns like a Q1 2025 GDP contraction of 0.3%. He dismissed fears of price hikes from companies like Ford and Mattel, arguing tariffs are misunderstood and often absorbed by companies, not consumers. He highlighted falling prices for gas, groceries, and lumber, crediting his policies, and pushed for Federal Reserve rate cuts, calling Chairman Jerome Powell “too late Powell” for resisting. Trump also defended his nominees, including a Surgeon General pick without residency, citing her academic credentials, and expressed disappointment over the stalled U.S. Attorney for D.C. nomination.

## A Foundation for Future Collaboration

The U.S.-U.K. trade deal on Victory Day 2025 marks a historic step in strengthening bilateral ties, rooted in shared history and economic reciprocity. Trump and Starmer’s agreement, while modest in immediate impact, lays a foundation for deeper collaboration in trade, technology, and security, with potential to expand as details are finalized. Amid global trade wars and the Ukraine conflict, the deal signals a renewed Western alliance, though challenges like tariff impacts and domestic economic pressures loom. As Trump navigates further negotiations, this “tremendous” deal underscores his administration’s push for fair trade, with the U.K. as a key partner in a shifting global landscape.

## Victory Day for World War II, 2025

BY THE PRESIDENT OF THE UNITED STATES OF AMERICA

A PROCLAMATION

Today, our Nation proudly commemorates the 80th anniversary of the Allied Powers’ triumph over national socialism and fascism, and the end of World War II in Europe — one of the most epic victories for forces of freedom in the history of the world.  On this Victory Day for World War II, we celebrate the unmatched might, strength, and power of the American Armed Forces, and we commit to protecting our sacred birthright of liberty against all threats, foreign and domestic.

In the wake of the December 7, 1941 attack on Pearl Harbor, the United States righteously entered the fray of what would become the apex of the eternal battle between good and evil.  After nearly 4 years of the darkest and bloodiest chapters ever recorded in human history, more than 250,000 Americans lost their lives in the fight against the Nazi regime.  Today and every day, we pay tribute to all those who made the ultimate sacrifice for their Nation, their liberty, and the survival of Western civilization.  Without the sacrifice of our American soldiers, this war would not have been won, and our world today would look drastically different.

May 8, 1945 marks the Allies’ acceptance of Germany’s unconditional surrender — the beginning of the end of years of long, gruesome, and brutal warfare.  The millions of souls senselessly lost serve as a reminder of why we must pursue peace through strength.  I remain steadfastly devoted to stopping the years of endless foreign wars and preventing the further loss of lives.  As I stated during my Inaugural Address, we will measure our success not only by the battles we win but also by the wars we end — and my proudest legacy will be that of a peacemaker.

As we commemorate Victory Day for World War II, we offer our unending thanks to every patriot from the Greatest Generation who left behind his home and family to fight for our freedom in distant lands.  We honor the memories of all those who perished.  Above all, we renew our commitment to keeping America and the entire world safe, secure, prosperous, and free.

NOW, THEREFORE, I, DONALD J. TRUMP, President of the United States of America, by virtue of the authority vested in me by the Constitution and the laws of the United States, do hereby proclaim May 8, 2025, as a day in celebration of Victory Day for World War II.

IN WITNESS WHEREOF, I have hereunto set my hand this seventh day of May, in the year of our Lord two thousand twenty‑five, and of the Independence of the United States of America the two hundred and forty-ninth.

DONALD J. TRUMP
