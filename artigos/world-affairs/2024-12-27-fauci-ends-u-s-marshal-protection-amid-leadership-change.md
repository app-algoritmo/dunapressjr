Former COVID czar Anthony Fauci has ended his two-year, $15 million U.S. Marshal protection just as Trump’s efficiency team prepares to take over federal operations. Fauci, who retired from public service, now relies on private security outside his $2.2 million Washington, D.C., home.

The decision to discontinue federal protection has raised eyebrows, given Fauci’s high-profile role during the pandemic. Critics argue that the move reflects shifting political priorities as Trump allies regain influence in federal operations. Supporters, however, see it as a natural transition for a retired official.

Fauci’s legacy remains polarizing, with some praising his contributions to public health and others criticizing his handling of the pandemic. The end of his protection detail marks a symbolic close to his chapter in public service, as the Biden administration transitions power to a Republican-led House.

As Fauci adapts to private life, his influence on public health policy continues through speaking engagements and advisory roles. However, his name remains a lightning rod for political debates, ensuring his impact on U.S. health policy will be discussed for years to come.
