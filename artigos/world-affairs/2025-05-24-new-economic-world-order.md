## New Economic World Order: Europe’s Suicide in the U.S.-China Trade

In a compelling interview with Professor Glenn Diesen, economist and former Greek Finance Minister Yanis Varoufakis dissects the unraveling of the post-World War II economic order and Europe’s self-destructive response to it. As the United States, under Donald Trump, launches a disruptive trade war to reassert global dominance, Europe flounders, lacking the political cohesion and investment to adapt. Varoufakis argues that the U.S. seeks to rebalance its parasitic financial system, while Europe’s failure to invest in future technologies and pursue strategic autonomy risks economic irrelevance. This article explores Varoufakis’s analysis of the emerging economic world order, Europe’s decline, and pathways to recovery.

## The U.S. Objective: Maintaining Global Hegemony

Varoufakis traces the U.S.’s post-1945 strategy to sustain global dominance through the Bretton Woods system, which ensured American manufacturing dominance by recycling surpluses to Europe and Japan via Marshall Aid and loans. This system collapsed in 1971 when President Nixon, facing deficits, dismantled fixed exchange rates, ushering in an inverted recycling mechanism. U.S. deficits fueled demand for German, Japanese, and later Chinese exports, with profits returning to Wall Street, inflating real estate and toxic derivatives. The 2008 financial crisis exposed this system’s fragility, with quantitative easing bailing out bankers while austerity crushed the majority.

Trump’s trade war, Varoufakis argues, echoes Nixon’s shock, aiming to reverse de-industrialization and rebalance the dollar’s dominance against a shrinking manufacturing base. However, success hinges on sustained policy across administrations, a feat Nixon achieved but Trump may not. The U.S.’s “cloud capital”—tech giants like Amazon and Google—extracts rents without producing tangible goods, further hollowing out the economy. Varoufakis sees Trump’s recognition of this unsustainability as astute, but his execution as uncertain.

## Europe’s Paralysis: A Cartel Without a Plan

Europe, Varoufakis contends, is ill-equipped to navigate this upheaval. Expelled from the dollar zone in 1971, Europe created the euro to stabilize its cartel-like structure, rooted in the European Coal and Steel Community and agricultural subsidies. However, the euro’s lack of a common treasury and democratic oversight triggered the euro crisis, met with harsh austerity and money printing. This enriched corporate elites—Volkswagen executives profited from stock buybacks—while stifling investment and toxifying politics.

Facing Trump’s tariffs, Europe lacks a coherent response. Leaders like Germany’s Olaf Scholz and France’s Emmanuel Macron clash without authority, while European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen consolidates power without legitimacy. Varoufakis criticizes Europe’s focus on “military Keynesianism”—buying tanks and torpedoes—as a futile distraction from its €600 billion annual investment shortfall. The absence of a decision-making center leaves Europe vulnerable, unable to counter U.S. or Chinese economic strategies.

## The Fourth Industrial Revolution: Europe’s Technological Lag

The rise of digital technologies and the fourth industrial revolution—encompassing green energy, electric vehicles, and cloud capital—underscores Europe’s decline. Once a leader in solar panels, Germany ceded ground to China, which invested heavily while Europe stagnated. Varoufakis dismisses claims of Chinese “dumping” as nonsense; China’s technological superiority stems from decades of strategic investment. Europe’s reliance on regulation over innovation has failed, with no equivalent to China’s Tencent or Alibaba. Attempts to tax U.S. big tech risk retaliation, as Europe lacks its own digital infrastructure, making threats like cutting off YouTube or X credible and devastating.

## China’s Strategic Advantage and Europe’s Missed Opportunities

In the U.S.-China economic war, Varoufakis sees China as the “adult in the room,” responding rationally with a macroeconomic and investment plan. China’s strategic autonomy—diversifying partnerships and developing tech giants—contrasts with Europe’s self-imposed isolation from Beijing. Varoufakis proposes three urgent steps for Europe: first, ignore Trump’s provocations and eliminate U.S.-prompted tariffs on Chinese goods like solar panels; second, coordinate with China’s central bank for a joint stimulus to counter recessionary pressures; and third, foster joint ventures, like Volkswagen partnering with BYD, to produce advanced technologies in Europe.

Additionally, Varoufakis advocates a €600–700 billion annual investment program via European Investment Bank bonds, backed by the European Central Bank, to fund green and digital projects. He also proposes a digital wallet for every European, offering central bank rates to compete with commercial banks and emulate China’s digital currency model. These measures could close the investment gap and restore competitiveness, but entrenched interests block progress.

## Why Europe Fails: Ideology and Expediency

Varoufakis attributes Europe’s inaction to ideology and expediency. Bankers in Frankfurt and Paris oppose digital payment reforms that threaten their monopoly, as Varoufakis experienced when demonized for proposing similar measures in 2015. Germany’s financial-industrial elite resists common debt, preserving an exit strategy from the euro to maintain leverage over deficit countries like Italy and France. This fear of shared liability—unlike the U.S., where states like California are bound by federal debt—stifles a pan-European investment program. Toxic politics, fueled by austerity and north-south, east-west divides, further paralyzes decision-making.

Yanis Varoufakis’s analysis reveals a world economy at a crossroads, with the U.S. fighting to preserve hegemony and China advancing strategically, while Europe sleepwalks into irrelevance. Trump’s trade war exposes the fragility of the dollarized system, but Europe’s lack of investment, political cohesion, and technological innovation risks permanent decline. Varoufakis’s proposals—partnering with China, launching a massive investment program, and digitizing finance—offer a lifeline, but ideological resistance and elite self-interest stand in the way. As the global order reshapes, Europe’s failure to act threatens economic suicide, leaving it a bystander in a world dominated by U.S.-China rivalry.
