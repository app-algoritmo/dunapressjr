## **United States-Japan Joint Leaders’ Statement

On February 7, 2025, the President of the United States, Donald J. Trump, and the Prime Minister of Japan, Ishiba Shigeru, held their first official meeting in Washington, D.C. During the meeting, both reaffirmed their commitment to pursuing a new golden age in U.S.-Japan relations, promoting a free and open Indo-Pacific, and contributing to peace and prosperity in a troubled world. ([whitehouse.gov](https://www.whitehouse.gov/briefings-statements/2025/02/united-states-japan-joint-leaders-statement/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

## **Cooperation for Peace

The leaders emphasized their mutual desire to strengthen bilateral security and defense cooperation under the U.S.-Japan Treaty of Mutual Cooperation and Security. They reaffirmed that the U.S.-Japan Alliance remains a cornerstone of peace, security, and prosperity in the Indo-Pacific region and beyond. Japan reiterated its unwavering commitment to fundamentally reinforcing its own defense capabilities, which was welcomed by the United States.

The United States underscored its unwavering commitment to Japan’s defense, using its full range of capabilities, including nuclear capabilities. Both leaders reaffirmed that Article V of the U.S.-Japan Treaty of Mutual Cooperation and Security applies to the Senkaku Islands and expressed strong opposition to any action that seeks to undermine Japan’s long-standing and peaceful administration of these islands.

To address an increasingly severe and complex security environment, the leaders confirmed their intention to strengthen U.S.-Japan deterrence and response capabilities. This includes modernizing command and control frameworks for both nations’ forces, increasing bilateral presence in Japan’s Southwest Islands, enhancing readiness through more realistic training and exercises, and promoting defense equipment and technology cooperation, including co-production and co-development. These initiatives will reinforce allied supply chains and strengthen U.S. and Japanese defense industrial capacity.

## **Cooperation for Growth and Prosperity

The leaders affirmed that bilateral economic cooperation, including economic security, is an indispensable part of the U.S.-Japan alliance. As close economic partners, the United States and Japan are the largest sources of foreign direct investment for each other and generate high-quality jobs. Their industries play a vital role in each other’s supply chains.

To further strengthen economic ties and elevate the partnership to the next level, the leaders aim to promote business opportunities, significantly increase bilateral investment and employment, and strengthen industrial bases. They will collaborate on critical technologies such as artificial intelligence, quantum computing, and cutting-edge semiconductors. They also committed to countering economic coercion and promoting growth in the Indo-Pacific region based on a free and fair economic order.

The leaders announced their intention to strengthen energy security by leveraging the United States’ affordable and reliable energy resources and increasing U.S. liquefied natural gas (LNG) exports to Japan in a mutually beneficial manner. They also welcomed efforts to diversify critical mineral supply chains and collaborate on the development and deployment of advanced nuclear reactor technology.

## **Coordination in the Indo-Pacific

The leaders shared views on the severe and complex security environment and expressed their determination to continue cooperating toward a free and open Indo-Pacific. As part of this cooperation, they intend to advance multilayered partnerships with like-minded countries, including Japan-Australia-India-U.S. (Quad), Japan-U.S.-Republic of Korea (ROK), Japan-U.S.-Australia, and Japan-U.S.-Philippines. Through these alliances, the United States, Japan, and their partners aim to deliver high-quality infrastructure investments in the region, including the deployment of Open Radio Access Networks (Open RAN) in third countries.

The leaders reiterated their strong opposition to any attempts by the People's Republic of China (PRC) to change the status quo by force or coercion in the East China Sea. They reaffirmed their opposition to the PRC’s unlawful maritime claims, militarization of reclaimed features, and provocative activities in the South China Sea.

The leaders emphasized the importance of maintaining peace and stability across the Taiwan Strait as an essential element of international security and prosperity. They encouraged a peaceful resolution of cross-Strait issues and opposed any attempts to unilaterally change the status quo by force or coercion. They also expressed support for Taiwan’s meaningful participation in international organizations.

## **Invitation to Visit Japan

President Trump accepted an invitation from Prime Minister Ishiba for an official visit to Japan in the near future.
