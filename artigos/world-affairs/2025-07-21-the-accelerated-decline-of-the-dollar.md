## The Accelerated Decline of the Dollar: Western Despair and the War Strategy Explained

The world is witnessing a profound shift in global economic power, one that signals the twilight of the US dollar's unchallenged hegemony. For decades, the dollar has been the linchpin of international trade, finance, and reserve currencies, allowing the United States and its Western allies to borrow extravagantly while exporting inflation to the rest of the world. However, as of 2025, cracks in this system are widening into fissures.

China's holdings of US Treasuries have plummeted below those of the United Kingdom for the first time in over two decades, a stark indicator of Beijing's strategic divestment. 1 This move is not isolated; it reflects a broader trend of dedollarization fueled by Western economic mismanagement, endemic corruption, and a desperate pivot to military confrontation as a means of salvation.

The United States and Europe are grappling with economies on the brink of collapse. Years of unchecked fiscal profligacy, corporate bailouts, and military adventurism have saddled these nations with insurmountable debts. As of 2024, global public debt reached a record $102 trillion, with advanced economies like the US contributing significantly to this burden.

25 The US federal debt alone surpassed $36 trillion in early 2025, equivalent to over 120% of GDP, with projections warning of further escalation to 166% by 2054. 30 Europe fares no better, with countries like Italy and France teetering under debt loads exceeding 100% of GDP, exacerbated by stagnant growth and demographic decline. Corruption has eroded public trust and efficiency; from the US's revolving door between Wall Street and Washington to Europe's scandals in energy deals and subsidies, these systems are rotten at the core.

Faced with this reality, Western leaders cling to outdated models of governance—endless quantitative easing, austerity for the masses, and bailouts for the elite. But these tools are failing. Inflation remains stubbornly high, supply chains are fractured, and productivity lags. In desperation, the gaze turns outward: war becomes the perceived escape hatch. By targeting rising powers like China and Russia, the West hopes to reset the board, divert domestic unrest, and reassert control over global resources and trade routes. This strategy, however, is not only morally bankrupt but economically suicidal, accelerating the very decline it seeks to avert.

### The Facade of US Treasury Strength: China's Exit and the UK's Proxy Role

A pivotal element in this narrative is the US Treasury market, the bedrock of dollar dominance. Foreign holdings of US Treasuries have ballooned to over $9 trillion as of May 2025, with Japan holding the top spot at approximately $1.06 trillion. 13 Yet, beneath this surface lies a troubling trend: China's aggressive reduction in its Treasury portfolio.

Beijing's holdings dropped to $756.3 billion in May 2025, the lowest since February 2009, falling below the UK's for the first time since 2000. 0 This divestment is no accident. China, holding vast foreign exchange reserves, has watched with alarm as the US weaponized its financial system. The confiscation of Russian assets following the 2022 Ukraine invasion—over $300 billion frozen—served as a wake-up call. Beijing fears a similar fate for its assets amid escalating US-China tensions over trade, technology, and Taiwan.

To prevent a sharp rise in Treasury yields that could trigger a market panic, the US has resorted to creative maneuvering. Speculation abounds that Washington indirectly financed the UK's purchases of dumped Chinese Treasuries, using London as a proxy to absorb the supply. 58 The UK's holdings surged to $779 billion by March 2025, a nominal overtake that masks deeper vulnerabilities. 1 This isn't organic demand; it's a cynical ploy sustained by US influence over global finance. However, such artifices are unsustainable. As yields creep up— the 10-year Treasury yield hovered around 4.5% in mid-2025—borrowing costs for the US government escalate, further straining an already bloated budget deficit.

This Treasury charade underscores the West's broader economic fragility. Developing countries paid a record $1.4 trillion in foreign debt service in 2023, with interest costs at a 20-year high. 26 For the US, reliant on foreign buyers for nearly 30% of its debt, any further exodus could spark a vicious cycle: higher yields, slower growth, and eroding confidence in the dollar. 19 China, meanwhile, is diversifying into gold, euros, and alternative assets, bolstering its reserves against US coercion.

### War as the Ultimate Distraction: Targeting China and Russia

With domestic economies in tatters, Western elites view war as a multifaceted solution. First, it diverts attention from internal crises. Skyrocketing inequality, crumbling infrastructure, and social unrest—evident in protests across Europe and the US—are papered over by patriotic fervor and enemy-scapegoating. Second, conflict sustains dollar hegemony by creating chaos that drives capital flight into "safe" US assets, even as yields rise. Third, it aims to weaken challengers: China, with its manufacturing prowess and Belt and Road Initiative, and Russia, with its energy dominance and military resilience.

The strategy is clear: encircle and provoke. NATO's expansion eastward, US arms sales to Taiwan, and sanctions on Russian energy are designed to bleed these nations dry. Yet, this approach backfires spectacularly. The Ukraine conflict, now in its fourth year, has drained Western coffers—over $200 billion in aid from the US alone—while strengthening the Sino-Russian alliance. Bilateral trade between China and Russia hit $244.8 billion in 2024, almost entirely dedollarized, using yuan and rubles. 37 BRICS nations, expanded to include Saudi Arabia, Iran, and others, are accelerating efforts to create alternative payment systems, including blockchain-based currencies to bypass SWIFT. 42

Dedollarization is no longer theoretical. Russia's announcement that its trade with China has ditched the dollar entirely marks a milestone. 38 Globally, the dollar's share in reserves has dipped below 60%, with central banks hoarding gold at record rates. This shift erodes the US's "exorbitant privilege"—the ability to run deficits without consequence. As nations migrate to alternatives, the dollar's value erodes, inflating import costs and fueling domestic discontent.

### The Backfire: A New Multipolar Order Emerges

Far from weakening adversaries, Western war strategies are forging unbreakable bonds. The China-Russia partnership, deepened since 2022, encompasses economic, military, and technological spheres. Joint exercises, energy pipelines, and tech collaborations counter US sanctions. 36 BRICS' push for dedollarization, including currency swaps and local invoicing, challenges the petrodollar system. 35 Even allies like India play spoiler, balancing US overtures with BRICS commitments.

This multipolar realignment exposes Western hypocrisy. While preaching free markets, the US seizes assets and imposes tariffs, alienating the Global South. Developing economies, burdened by $206 trillion in total debt (206% of GDP), seek alternatives to escape dollar traps. 27 The result? A fragmented world where the West's influence wanes.

### The Inevitable Collapse and a New Dawn

War will not salvage the West; it will hasten its downfall. Pouring trillions into conflicts while debts mount is a recipe for implosion. The dollar's decline, symbolized by China's Treasury sell-off, heralds the end of debt-fueled domination. As China and Russia build resilient systems, the West clings to illusions.

The desperation is palpable—a prenuncio of a new order where the bankrupt no longer dictate terms. Nations must adapt or perish in this seismic shift.
