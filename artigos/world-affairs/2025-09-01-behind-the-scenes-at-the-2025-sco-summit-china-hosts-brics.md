## 2025 SCO Summit Behind-the-Scenes: Xi, Putin, Modi Talks and Potential Geopolitical, Economic Surprises

As the world grapples with escalating trade wars and shifting alliances, the 2025 Shanghai Cooperation Organization (SCO) Summit in Tianjin, China, from August 31 to September 1, has emerged as a pivotal gathering. Hosted by Chinese President Xi Jinping, the event brings together leaders from over 20 countries, including key BRICS members Russia, China, and India—represented by President Vladimir Putin and Prime Minister Narendra Modi. While the SCO isn't identical to BRICS, the overlap in membership and agendas makes this summit a de facto platform for these powerhouses to coordinate amid global uncertainties. Behind the formal ceremonies and public statements lies a web of private bilaterals, strategic whispers, and potential game-changers that could reshape social, geopolitical, and economic landscapes.

Tianjin, a bustling port city on the Bohai Sea, was chosen for its symbolic nod to China's economic prowess and strategic location. The summit kicked off on August 31 with a grand welcoming ceremony, where leaders posed for group photos that captured the optics of unity: Putin and Modi holding hands as they greeted Xi, a moment that went viral and underscored the deepening ties among these nations. This wasn't just pageantry; it set the tone for intense discussions on security, trade, and countering Western dominance. With U.S. President Donald Trump's tariffs biting hard—averaging 18.2% and threatening more—the summit has become a forum for the Global South to rally.

Behind the scenes, the action unfolded in a series of bilateral meetings. Modi and Xi's encounter on August 31 marked Modi's first visit to China in over seven years, a significant thaw after years of border tensions along the Line of Actual Control (LAC). Sources close to the talks revealed a focus on "resetting" relations: Modi emphasized border peace as essential for progress, while Xi called for enhanced strategic communication. They discussed counter-terrorism, addressing trade imbalances (India's deficit with China stands at over $100 billion annually), and boosting people-to-people ties. A highlight was Modi's invitation to Xi for the 2026 BRICS Summit in India, which Xi accepted, signaling potential for warmer ties. This meeting wasn't impromptu; it followed weeks of diplomatic groundwork, including recent disengagements along the LAC, hinting at a deliberate effort to move past the 2020 Galwan clash.

Putin's interactions added another layer of intrigue. Arriving amid the ongoing Ukraine conflict, he held private talks with Xi before the group photo, discussing energy partnerships and mutual support against sanctions. Putin also met Modi on the sidelines, building on their recent engagements. In candid footage leaked from the summit, the trio—Xi, Putin, and Modi—were seen in deep conversation, a visual that analysts dubbed "powerful optics" of RIC (Russia-India-China) solidarity. Putin used the platform to defend Russia's actions in Ukraine, claiming an "understanding" with Trump on ending the war, while slamming U.S. "bullying" and discriminatory sanctions. These remarks, made during plenary sessions, reflect the summit's anti-Western undercurrent, with Xi urging rejection of a "Cold War mentality" and promoting AI cooperation among members.

The SCO, often seen as China's counter to the U.S.-led G7, includes Pakistan, Iran, Kazakhstan, and others, representing over 40% of the world's population and a quarter of global GDP. This year's agenda emphasized security, economic integration, and connectivity. No new members were admitted—Armenia's bid was blocked by Pakistan, Azerbaijan's by India—highlighting internal rivalries that could surprise observers if resolved unexpectedly. Discussions on a SCO development bank and alternative payment systems aim to reduce dollar dependence, a direct response to U.S. financial pressures.

Now, to the potential surprises. Geopolitically, the summit could yield breakthroughs in India-China relations. If border talks lead to a formal agreement, it might stabilize Asia's flashpoints, allowing India to focus on Indo-Pacific strategies without a two-front threat from China and Pakistan. A surprise could be a joint RIC statement on Ukraine, with Modi—fresh from a call with Zelenskyy—pushing for peace, aligning with Putin's Trump reference. This might signal a multipolar mediation effort, challenging Western narratives. Another wildcard: Iran's fuller integration, potentially escalating Middle East tensions if SCO backs Tehran against Israel amid ongoing conflicts.

Economically, energy deals are the talk of the town. Putin is courting billions in oil and gas agreements with China and India, Russia's top buyers since Western sanctions. A surprise announcement could involve expanded Power of Siberia pipelines or Indian investments in Russian Arctic projects, bolstering energy security amid global volatility. With Trump's tariffs looming—potentially 50% on BRICS goods—the summit might unveil a coordinated response, like accelerated de-dollarization through BRICS' New Development Bank or crypto-based trade settlements. Pakistan's Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif highlighted Gaza, but economic pleas for connectivity (like CPEC extensions) could surprise if they gain traction, linking SCO economies further.

Socially, the summit isn't just about hard power. Modi and Xi's talks on resuming the Kailash Mansarovar Yatra—a sacred pilgrimage for Hindus disrupted by COVID and tensions—could be a feel-good surprise, fostering cultural exchanges. Broader people-to-people initiatives, like educational visas or joint cultural festivals, might emerge, countering the narrative of rivalry. In a world divided by misinformation, SCO's focus on countering extremism could lead to social media pacts, surprising with cross-border content collaborations.

Critics, however, point to the summit's challenges. The SCO's murky reach—balancing diverse interests from authoritarian China to democratic India—raises questions about its effectiveness. Internal frictions, like India-Pakistan tensions (Sharif reportedly ignored Modi in one instance), could derail unity. Yet, as Xi positions China at the center of an anti-Western order, the event flaunts Beijing's global pull through statecraft and military displays.

As the summit wraps on September 1, with Xi's closing remarks promoting a "more democratic international order," the real outcomes will unfold in the coming months. Will we see a stronger RIC troika challenging U.S. hegemony? Economic pacts shielding against tariffs? Or social bridges healing old wounds? The behind-the-scenes maneuvering in Tianjin suggests yes, but in geopolitics, surprises are the norm.

This gathering isn't isolated; it builds on recent BRICS expansions and SCO's evolution since 2001. With leaders like Nepal's and Maldives' engaging Modi on Global South issues, it reinforces multipolarity. Economically, amid projections of slowing global growth, SCO's emphasis on connectivity—rail, roads, digital—could surprise with infrastructure announcements tying Eurasia closer.

Geopolitically, a subtle shift: Putin's criticism of NATO as the "root" of the Ukraine war, echoed in summit corridors, might inspire a SCO peace initiative. Socially, if AI cooperation translates to joint tech education programs, it could bridge generational divides in member states.

In essence, the 2025 SCO Summit is more than a meeting—it's a microcosm of a changing world order. As Modi departs, inviting Xi to India, the stage is set for potential surprises that could redefine alliances. Stay tuned; the ripples from Tianjin will be felt far and wide.
