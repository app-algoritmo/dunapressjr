The regulation of medical devices — especially those powered by artificial intelligence (AI) and software — has become a central topic in the transformation of the healthcare sector. With rapid technological advances, Brazil’s National Health Surveillance Agency (ANVISA) faces the challenge of ensuring safety, efficacy, and innovation in an increasingly dynamic and globalized environment.

In an interview between Dr. Natália Bellan and Dr. Rafael Fernandes — a specialist with over 20 years of experience at ANVISA — key issues were discussed regarding how the agency is adapting to regulate this new wave of technology-driven healthcare solutions.

***

## **What Are Medical Devices?

A medical device can be defined as any instrument, apparatus, or software intended for human use for medical purposes, including diagnosis, prevention, monitoring, treatment, or relief of disease. This includes everything from simple items like syringes and gauze to complex equipment such as MRI machines and AI-based medical software.

Dr. Rafael Fernandes explained that medical devices are classified according to the level of risk involved — the higher the risk, the stricter the regulation. For example, baby bottles and pacifiers are considered low-risk medical devices, while equipment like pacemakers require more rigorous controls.

***

## **The Rise of Medical Software

With the evolution of technology, medical software has gained prominence in the healthcare sector. Mobile apps that monitor heartbeats for medical purposes, automated diagnostic systems, and medical image storage software are already regulated as medical devices by ANVISA.

Rafael emphasized that there is no fundamental difference between regulating hardware and software. However, the real challenge emerges when these software tools incorporate artificial intelligence and continue to learn through use.

“The manufacturer releases a product, and that product evolves — it goes in some direction,” said Rafael, referring to the adaptive nature of AI.

This introduces significant implications for oversight, as the traditional model of importation and physical control does not apply to software downloaded directly from the internet. Therefore, ANVISA must rethink its regulatory approach to keep pace with these changes.

***

## **Artificial Intelligence: New Challenges and Opportunities

Artificial intelligence adds new layers of complexity to regulation. Known as **Software as a Medical Device (SaMD)**, AI-based tools present unique characteristics:

- **Continuous adaptation**: Algorithms that learn and change based on user data.

- **Transparency**: It is necessary to disclose information about the data used in AI training to identify possible biases.

- **Controlled processing**: Ensuring that automatic decisions made by algorithms are safe and reliable.

Rafael stressed that transparency is crucial.

“The more transparency we have, the better we can identify bias and allow researchers to detect deficiencies in training,” he stated. This approach helps prevent failures that could put patients at risk.

***

## **ANVISA’s Role: Balancing Innovation and Safety

ANVISA acts as a bridge between innovation and safety, striving to balance the need to protect patients without stifling technological development. According to Rafael, the agency has internalized international discussions held in forums such as the MDRF (Medical Device Regulatory Forum) and other global bodies, creating specific norms for innovative products.

“If nobody complains, the regulation probably isn’t right. Everyone should complain — and more or less equally,” Rafael joked, referring to the delicate art of creating rules that satisfy both industry and healthcare professionals.

Additionally, ANVISA also participates in events and trade fairs to engage with the market and prepare it for upcoming regulatory changes.

“We’re always out there promoting what we’re going to do next so the market can prepare,” said Rafael.

***

## **Technovigilance and Continuous Monitoring

Another important pillar of regulation is **technovigilance** — the continuous monitoring of medical devices after they reach the market. ANVISA uses the Notivisa system, where citizens can report adverse events or technical problems with products.

This information is valuable for identifying flaws and correcting them quickly. Rafael noted that ANVISA already uses artificial intelligence to automatically analyze these reports, making the process more efficient and faster.

***

## **3D Printing and Customized Devices

The conversation also touched on the issue of prosthetics and surgical guides produced via 3D printing. These are examples of personalized medical devices, specifically designed for each patient. Rafael explained that ANVISA already has a specific resolution for this type of product, recognizing its unique nature and the need for differentiated regulation.

***

## **The Future of Regulation in Brazil

Despite progress, Rafael highlighted several challenges faced by ANVISA:

- **Reduction of technical staff**: The agency has lost nearly half of its technical workforce in recent years, complicating the oversight of growing innovations.

- **Increasing technical complexity**: Healthcare professionals must configure advanced equipment, even without formal technology training.

- **Demand for agile regulation**: Technology moves faster than legislation, requiring faster regulatory processes.

For Rafael, the future requires a shift in the pace of regulation.

“We need to change the rhythm of regulation to have a faster one,” he concluded.

***

## **Final Recommendations

At the end of the interview, Rafael offered some practical advice:

- **For patients**: Check whether the product has ANVISA registration before using it. Just visit the agency's website and search by product number.

- **For healthcare professionals**: Stay alert to regulatory updates and participate in dialogue with ANVISA.

- **For the industry**: Prepare in advance for upcoming changes and take advantage of communication channels with the regulatory agency.

***

The regulation of medical devices, especially those based on software and artificial intelligence, is a complex frontier essential for patient safety. With leadership from experts like Dr. Rafael Fernandes, ANVISA stands at the center of this revolution, working to maintain the balance between innovation and health protection. The future depends on constant collaboration between government, industry, and society, moving toward a safer and technologically advanced sanitary system.
