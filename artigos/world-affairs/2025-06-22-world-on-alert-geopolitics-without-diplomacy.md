## World on Alert- Global Geopolitical Updates: June 16 to 22, 2025

We are on June 22, 2025, at 12:56 PM CEST, and the global geopolitical landscape over the past seven days reflects an intensification of tensions challenging worldwide stability. This period, from June 16 to 22, has been marked by events blending armed conflicts, economic disputes, and power dynamics, necessitating cautious analysis amid often polarized narratives.

The most immediate highlight is the escalation in the Middle East, with U.S. attacks on Iranian nuclear facilities widely reported since the week’s start. These events have triggered a wave of uncertainty, driving up gold and oil prices—the Brent crude rose by up to 18% since June 10, reaching US$79.04 per barrel on June 19, according to market analysts. The possibility of blocking the Strait of Hormuz, which carries over 30% of the world’s oil, has been raised as a real threat, impacting energy-dependent economies. Meanwhile, President Donald Trump has signaled a two-week pause to decide on further actions, offering temporary market relief, though caution lingers.

Parallelly, the Russia-Ukraine war continues to shape Europe. In Asia, China is strengthening ties with Kyrgyzstan, developing a sanctions-resistant trade corridor under the Belt and Road Initiative, while keeping a close watch on Taiwan. Social media posts suggest an atmosphere of apprehension, with some speculating about a potential escalation to a large-scale war, though such claims lack official confirmation and should be treated with skepticism.

Economically, a 11% drop in global investment to US$1.5 trillion in 2024, as reported by UNCTAD, reflects the impact of geopolitical tensions and trade barriers. Carbon dioxide levels hit 430 parts per million in June, raising climate change alarms, while events like the Paris Air Show (June 16-22) were overshadowed by geopolitical controversies, including boycotts of Israeli companies. Additionally, cyberspace has become a battleground, with hacktivists launching DDoS attacks on Israel and a US$90 million cryptocurrency heist, highlighting an evolving hybrid war.

These developments, combined with the 22-year sentence handed to former Tunisian President Moncef Marzouki for criticizing the government and the growing Sino-American rivalry, indicate a world in transition. Despite global markets showing resilience—with the MSCI World Index dropping just 1% after recent highs—volatility persists, and diplomacy seems stalled. The dominant narrative suggests rising risks, but the lack of definitive data invites questions about whether we face a power redefinition or a transient turbulent phase. Stay vigilant and critically assess the information coming your way.
