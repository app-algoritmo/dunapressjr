## *The proposal to revoke citizenship from individuals considered a threat to the State gains momentum

In a move that promises to reshape Sweden's migration and security policies, the Swedish parliament is set to vote on a proposal to amend the country's 1924 constitution. This reform would allow the government to revoke the citizenship of individuals deemed a threat to national security. The initiative has garnered widespread political support, with backing from all parliamentary parties.

### **A Historic Decision with Broad Political Support

The proposed constitutional reform aims to equip Sweden with legal tools to address internal threats, particularly in a global context where national security and migration are prominent issues. According to the proponents of the measure, this change will strengthen the state's ability to protect its citizens and preserve constitutional order.

The initiative has faced criticism from some sectors, warning of potential violations of human rights and international standards. However, the political consensus appears to lean towards the need to act against current challenges, including organized crime and radicalization networks.

### **What Does This Change Mean?

If approved, Sweden would join the list of European countries that have implemented strict measures to deal with individuals considered threats. The law would allow the revocation of citizenship even for those with dual nationality, ensuring that individuals do not become stateless, in compliance with international agreements.

This change also reflects a growing sentiment among the Swedish population. Recent surveys indicate that a significant majority of citizens support stricter measures to reinforce national security, reinforcing the narrative of "taking back control of the country."

### **The Future of Sweden

The vote is scheduled for the coming weeks, and while approval is expected due to widespread political support, the impact of this reform will spark debates not only in Sweden but across Europe. Sweden, known for its inclusive welfare model, now seems to be redefining the limits of its migration and security policies.

With this reform, the country takes a firm step toward stricter control of its citizenship, reflecting a shift in the era that could set precedents on a global scale. The final decision will mark a milestone in Sweden's political history, opening a new chapter for the Scandinavian nation.
