## China’s Victory in the US-China Economic War: Sean Foo on a Shifting Global Order

## Sean Foo on China’s Victory in the US-China Economic War: A New Global Order Emerges

In a compelling interview with Prof. Glenn Diesen, financial analyst Sean Foo from Singapore, an expert on China’s economy, declares that China has effectively won the US-China economic war. Foo analyzes the intensifying conflict, highlighting the US’s shortsighted policies, China’s strategic resilience, and the shifting global economic architecture. As the US doubles down on tariffs and tech sanctions, China leverages its diversified supply chains, technological advancements, and the BRICS framework to weather the storm, positioning itself as a leader in a multipolar world.

## The US-China Economic War: A Misstep in Strategy

Foo frames the US-China economic war as a misguided escalation, with Trump doubling down on Biden’s mistakes from 2020-2024. The US, Foo argues, is attempting to compress 30-40 years of economic progress into mere months, a “haphazard, shortsighted” approach that ignores the long-term dynamics of global trade. Initially focused on the Ukraine conflict, the US has shifted its attention to China, aiming to curb its rise through tariffs, tech sanctions, and supply chain repatriation. However, Foo contends that these policies are backfiring, as China’s economic resilience and strategic foresight have rendered the US’s efforts ineffective.

China’s journey since joining the World Trade Organization (WTO) in 2001 illustrates its adaptability. Starting with low-value manufacturing like apparel, China climbed the global value chain, progressing to electronics, smartphones, electric vehicles (EVs), and semiconductors. Using its trillion-dollar annual trade surplus, China invested globally through the Belt and Road Initiative, building factories in Vietnam, the Middle East, Russia, and Latin America. This diversification has reduced China’s dependence on US trade—exports to the US now account for only 2.5-3% of China’s GDP. Even if trade halted entirely, Foo estimates China’s GDP growth would still hold at 2-3%, bolstered by potential stimulus measures.

## China’s Strategic Response: Patience and Diversification

Foo highlights China’s calculated response to the US’s aggressive policies. Over the past month, as Trump imposed a 10% global tariff (effectively 20-25% for some), China has “sat back,” recognizing the vulnerabilities of the hyper-financialized US economy. The US’s interconnected financial system—reliant on Wall Street, bonds, and the stock market—has been destabilized by these tariffs. Global investors, fearing a trade war’s impact, have pulled out of US assets, causing a “total repudiation” of the dollar, bonds, and stocks. Foo notes that Trump’s claim of a “kabuki theater” to “trap China” was a face-saving reaction to this market meltdown, as the US underestimated the global backlash, with countries like Canada, Mexico, and China pushing back through retaliatory tariffs.

China, meanwhile, is redirecting its trade surplus away from the US, which historically reinvested in US bonds, fueling America’s financialization. The 2022 freezing of $300 billion in Russian assets by the G7 was a “wake-up call” for Beijing, signaling that its own reserves could be targeted. China has since invested heavily in gold—pushing prices from $2,500 to $3,500 per ounce—while also funding projects in Saudi Arabia, Latin America (Argentina, Brazil), Russia, and Southeast Asia. This outward approach contrasts with the US’s inward turn, as China builds factories, diversifies supply chains, and “sanction-proofs” its economy by deepening economic ties with other nations, making them less likely to comply with US demands.

## The Tech Sector: China’s Resilience and Innovation

A key battleground in this economic war is the tech sector, where the US aimed to cripple China’s technological ascent. Initially, US leaders like Google’s CEO advocated for semiconductor bans to undermine giants like Huawei, hoping to set back China’s tech development by decades. However, Foo notes that this strategy has failed spectacularly. China has rapidly closed the technological gap, with innovations like DeepSeek—an AI model tailored for practical applications in energy and healthcare—disrupting the G7’s tech dominance. Intel’s recent layoffs underscore the US’s struggles, as cutting off China’s chip supply has hurt American companies’ revenues.

China’s success stems from its cost-effective supply chains and innovation ecosystem. Unlike the US, where high research and production costs burden companies, China benefits from low-cost raw materials, mines, refineries, and alternative energy suppliers like Russia and Saudi Arabia. Foo explains that US chip sanctions, which restricted China to low-value chips while prioritizing G7 nations, backfired by galvanizing China’s tech sector. Forced to innovate, China collaborated with Huawei, universities, and small suppliers, accelerating its semiconductor development. The US, Foo argues, has “forced China into rapid technological innovation” from which it cannot recover, ensuring China’s eventual self-sufficiency.

## Economic Nationalism: The US’s Struggle to Re-Industrialize

Foo draws parallels to 19th-century economic nationalism, where nations used tariffs and subsidies to build industrial sovereignty. China has followed this model, using its trade surplus to finance strategic industries like semiconductors and robotics. The US, however, is struggling to replicate this approach. Biden’s $50-100 billion semiconductor fund was a step in the right direction, but Trump’s chaotic tariffs—unpredictable and lacking long-term planning—deter investment.

Foo notes that US companies like Intel suffer from losing China as a market, while the government’s simultaneous trade war and re-industrialization efforts strain funding. Trump’s failure to cut deficits, despite promises, exacerbates the issue, as the Department of Government Efficiency (DOGE) has scaled back from $2 trillion to $150 billion in cuts, with Elon Musk stepping back to focus on Tesla.

The US’s hyper-financialized economy, reliant on global dollar flows, is ill-equipped for this multi-front war. Foo suggests a more gradual approach: focusing on one sector at a time, building supply chains, and securing raw materials, as China did over decades. However, this requires austerity and patience—qualities the US lacks under Trump’s “short-circuit” strategy, driven by political timelines rather than economic realities.

## China’s Strategic Industries: Semiconductors and Beyond

Foo identifies semiconductors as China’s primary focus, likening them to the “new oil” of the future. The DeepSeek AI model exemplifies China’s pragmatic approach, integrating AI into industries like energy and healthcare to boost productivity and fund further innovation. China aims to remain a production giant while rebalancing its economy toward consumption, with semiconductors and robotics ensuring its competitive edge. In the EV sector, Chinese brands like BYD and NIO have outpaced Western competitors like Volkswagen, thanks to a hyper-competitive ecosystem that drives down costs. Foo cautions investors against targeting specific Chinese companies due to this “Hunger Games on steroids” competition, recommending broader market investments or Chinese banks instead.

## The Role of BRICS: A New Economic Architecture

The BRICS alliance—Brazil, Russia, India, China, South Africa, and new members like the UAE and Indonesia—plays a pivotal role in China’s strategy. Foo predicts that BRICS will surpass the G7 in influence, already outpacing it in GDP. The US’s trade war, by reducing global dollar flows, erodes the Breton Woods system, where the US provided liquidity in exchange for goods. BRICS is creating an alternative financial architecture, including payment systems to bypass SWIFT and dollar-based trade. While BRICS may not develop its own currency soon, Foo foresees a decline in the dollar’s dominance as countries shift to alternative settlement mechanisms.

China’s investments in BRICS nations, particularly in infrastructure and supply chains, strengthen this framework. With growing populations and rising living standards, BRICS countries offer expanding markets, contrasting with the West’s stagnation. Even the EU, facing a shrinking US market and tariff walls, is engaging more with China, signaling a bifurcated world where the US risks isolation in its pursuit of self-sufficiency, while BRICS fosters freer trade.

## China’s Triumph and a Multipolar Future

Sean Foo’s analysis underscores China’s victory in the US-China economic war, driven by its strategic diversification, technological resilience, and global partnerships through BRICS. The US, hampered by shortsighted policies and a hyper-financialized economy, is losing ground, as tariffs and tech sanctions backfire. China’s focus on semiconductors and robotics, coupled with its outward investment strategy, positions it as a leader in a multipolar world. As the US faces economic and geopolitical isolation, Foo’s insights—available on his YouTube channel, Sean Foo Gold—offer a critical perspective on the shifting global order, where BRICS may soon redefine economic power dynamics.
