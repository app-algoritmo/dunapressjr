The [United States has officially](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/04/modifying-reciprocal-tariff-rates-to-reflect-trading-partner-retaliation-and-alignment/) modified its **tariffs** policy to respond to China’s retaliatory measures and to encourage fair trade alignment with over 75 global partners. Through an executive order issued in April 2025, the Biden administration aims to address long-standing trade imbalances, support national manufacturing, and reinforce global partnerships committed to economic reciprocity.

***

In a landmark trade policy shift, the White House has announced a series of tariff modifications aimed at countering retaliatory measures from the People’s Republic of China (PRC) while rewarding more than 75 countries that have expressed willingness to align with the United States on trade reciprocity and national security.

This decision, issued through a Presidential Executive Order dated April 10, 2025, reflects a nuanced and strategic shift in U.S. economic defense policy, reinforcing America's stance on fair trade while navigating a complex global environment.

***

## The Emergency Behind the Order

The foundation of this order stems from Executive Order 14257 (April 2, 2025), which declared a national emergency due to ongoing and significant U.S. goods trade deficits. The administration argued that such deficits threaten both economic and national security, and are exacerbated by foreign industrial policies—particularly from China—that suppress domestic manufacturing and encourage overproduction abroad.

This initial order allowed the President to impose reciprocal tariffs to correct what the administration considers unfair and non-reciprocal trade arrangements.

***

## China’s Retaliation and the U.S. Response

On April 9, 2025, the Chinese government retaliated by announcing an **84% tariff** on all U.S. imports, effective the following day. In response, the U.S. government took decisive action:

- Increased the tariff rate on Chinese imports from **84% to 125%**.

- Implemented these changes immediately for goods entering the U.S. after **12:01 a.m. EDT, April 10, 2025**.

- Suspended specific trade categories (HTSUS headings 9903.01.43 through 9903.01.76) to focus retaliation precisely on offending trade partners.

The modification directly targets China, including goods from Hong Kong and Macau, while maintaining compliance with international trade law.

***

## Support from International Partners

In contrast to China's stance, over **75 international trading partners**—many listed in *Annex I of Executive Order 14257*—have taken steps to **align with U.S. trade values**. These countries have engaged in dialogue and cooperative efforts to remedy structural trade imbalances and align with shared economic and security priorities.

As a gesture of goodwill, the United States has temporarily **suspended country-specific tariff rates for these cooperating nations** and applied a standard 10% ad valorem duty for a 90-day period, effective from April 10 to July 9, 2025.

***

## Temporary Adjustments and Global Implications

These temporary adjustments, including the suspension of previous tariff categories, aim to:

- Encourage ongoing trade alignment with cooperating nations.

- Exert pressure on countries that adopt retaliatory or aggressive economic postures.

- Protect U.S. domestic industries and secure long-term economic stability.

The clear message: countries that act in good faith will benefit from flexibility, while those who choose confrontation—like the PRC—will face intensified trade defenses.

***

## Postal Tariffs and Synthetic Opioids

In a related action under **Executive Order 14256**, the administration also increased tariffs on **low-value imports related to synthetic opioids** from China:

- The **tariff on de minimis goods** will rise from **90% to 120%**.

- Per-item postal tariffs will increase from **$75 to $100 (May 2 – May 31, 2025)**, and from **$150 to $200 (starting June 1, 2025)**.

These actions are designed to close loopholes in small package imports and strengthen enforcement on products linked to the synthetic opioid crisis.

***

The latest tariff modifications represent a **multi-dimensional response** to evolving global trade dynamics. While affirming a hardline stance against retaliatory and unfair practices—particularly from China—the United States has simultaneously opened a diplomatic channel for cooperation with willing partners.

As the world economy enters a period of recalibration, these measures serve not only as a shield but also as an invitation: fair trade, reciprocal engagement, and shared prosperity are possible—but not unconditional.
