## Vietnam’s Entry into BRICS: Impacts on Global Growth and Multipolarity

On June 13, 2025, Vietnam was announced as the tenth partner country of the BRICS, a grouping that brings together some of the world’s leading emerging economies, currently composed of 11 member countries: Brazil, Russia, India, China, South Africa, Egypt, United Arab Emirates, Ethiopia, Indonesia, Iran, and Saudi Arabia. This decision, formalized by the Brazilian presidency of the bloc during the 16th Summit held in Kazan, Russia, in October 2024, marks a new chapter in the strategic expansion of the BRICS and reinforces its relevance in the global geopolitical and economic landscape. This article analyzes the importance of Vietnam’s inclusion as a partner country, the potential impacts of this accession on the bloc’s growth, and the implications for the international order.

## The Context of Vietnam’s Entry into the BRICS

The BRICS, initially conceived as BRIC in 2001 by economist Jim O’Neill, emerged as a concept to highlight the economic growth potential of Brazil, Russia, India, and China. With the inclusion of South Africa in 2011, the group solidified as a mechanism for cooperation among emerging economies, focused on promoting socioeconomic development, South-South cooperation, and a more inclusive global order. Since then, the bloc has sought to expand its influence, culminating in the 2024 expansion, which integrated Egypt, the United Arab Emirates, Ethiopia, Iran, and Saudi Arabia, in addition to Indonesia in 2025.

The “partner country” category was created at the Kazan Summit to allow nations aligned with the BRICS’ objectives to participate in multilateral forums without full membership. Vietnam, with its dynamic economy and population of nearly 100 million, was selected for its relevance in Asia and its integration into global value chains. As a partner, Vietnam is entitled to participate in summits, ministerial meetings, and other discussion forums of the BRICS, as well as endorse joint declarations, strengthening its influence without requiring full membership commitments.

## The Importance of Vietnam in the BRICS Context

Vietnam’s inclusion as a partner country is strategic for several reasons. First, Vietnam is one of the fastest-growing economies in Southeast Asia, with a GDP that has grown at an average rate of about 6% per year over the past two decades. Its economy is heavily export-oriented, with a focus on electronics, textiles, and footwear, and is deeply integrated into global production chains. In 2024, the country was one of the largest exporters of smartphones and electronic components, competing with giants like China.

Additionally, Vietnam is a significant geopolitical player in Asia. Its position in the South China Sea, where it maintains territorial disputes with China, and its balanced foreign policy, which seeks partnerships with both the West and Asian powers, make the country a potential mediator in regional issues. Joining the BRICS as a partner strengthens the bloc’s presence in Southeast Asia, a region strategic for global trade and competition between major powers, such as China and the United States.

Vietnam also shares the BRICS’ values, such as a commitment to South-South cooperation, sustainable development, and reform of global governance. Its participation in the bloc is seen as an opportunity to amplify the voice of emerging economies in international forums, such as the G20 and the UN, where the BRICS seeks greater representation.

## Impacts on the BRICS’ Growth

Vietnam’s entry as a partner country has the potential to boost the BRICS’ growth in several dimensions: economic, geopolitical, and institutional. Below, we explore these impacts in detail.

## 1. Economic Impact

The BRICS already accounts for about 37% of global GDP in purchasing power parity and 48.5% of the world’s population, with international trade representing 24% of global exchanges. Vietnam’s inclusion, even as a partner, strengthens the bloc by adding a vibrant economy with strong global integration. In 2024, Brazil’s trade with the BRICS totaled $210 billion, of which 36% were exports and 34% imports. Vietnam’s participation could increase these figures, especially for countries like Brazil and China, which already maintain trade ties with the Asian nation.

Vietnam is a major importer of commodities, such as soybeans and meat, sectors in which Brazil is a leader. The partnership could facilitate more effective trade negotiations, reducing tariff barriers and promoting bilateral agreements. Additionally, Vietnam is a manufacturing hub, which could attract investments from the New Development Bank (NDB), known as the “BRICS Bank,” for infrastructure and technology projects. The NDB, chaired by former Brazilian President Dilma Rousseff, has funded sustainable development initiatives, and Vietnam, with its need for logistical modernization, is an ideal candidate to receive these resources.

Vietnam’s presence also reinforces the BRICS’ agenda of reducing dependence on the U.S. dollar in international trade. The bloc has promoted the use of local currencies, and Vietnam, with its export-oriented economy, could benefit from alternative payment systems, such as the one proposed by the BRICS to replace SWIFT. This could increase the bloc’s economic resilience in the face of Western sanctions, particularly for members like Russia and Iran.

## 2. Geopolitical Impact

Geopolitically, Vietnam’s entry expands the BRICS’ influence in Southeast Asia, a critical region for the competition between China and the United States. China, which leads the bloc’s expansion agenda, sees the inclusion of countries like Vietnam as a way to counterbalance the G7’s influence and consolidate its leadership in the Global South. However, Vietnam’s balanced stance, maintaining strategic partnerships with the U.S. and the European Union, could mitigate the perception that the BRICS is an anti-Western bloc, attracting greater global legitimacy.

The partnership also strengthens the BRICS’ narrative as a defender of a multipolar international order. Vietnam, with its history of colonial resistance and independent foreign policy, aligns with the bloc’s vision of reforming institutions like the UN Security Council and the IMF, giving more voice to developing nations. This convergence of interests could increase the BRICS’ internal cohesion, despite divergences among members, such as tensions between China and India.

## 3. Institutional Impact

The partner country category is an institutional innovation that allows the BRICS to expand its influence without diluting the decision-making power of full members. Vietnam, as a partner, can contribute to strengthening forums like the BRICS Business Forum and cooperation in areas such as technology, education, and culture. Its experience in free trade agreements, such as the Comprehensive and Progressive Agreement for Trans-Pacific Partnership (CPTPP), could inspire the BRICS to explore new forms of economic integration without forming a formal economic bloc.

Furthermore, Vietnam’s inclusion reinforces the NDB’s role as an alternative to Western financial institutions. Projects in Vietnam could attract funding from the bank, increasing its relevance and lending capacity. This is particularly important at a time when the BRICS seeks to expand its influence in strategic sectors, such as renewable energy and critical minerals.

## Global Implications

Vietnam’s entry into the BRICS as a partner country has implications that transcend the bloc, affecting the global order on several levels:

1. **Strengthening the Global South**: Vietnam bolsters the Global South’s representation in the BRICS, amplifying the voice of developing nations in debates on trade, climate, and security. This could pressure for reforms in global institutions, aligning with the bloc’s agenda.

2. **Geoeconomic Competition**: The partnership intensifies competition between the BRICS and the G7, particularly regarding control of supply chains and strategic resources, such as rare earths, where the bloc holds 72% of global reserves. Vietnam, with its electronics industry, complements this advantage.

3. **Dedollarization**: Vietnam’s participation could accelerate the BRICS’ efforts to create an alternative financial system, reducing dependence on the dollar. This is particularly relevant in a context of Western sanctions against bloc members.

4. **Regional Balance**: In Southeast Asia, Vietnam could act as a bridge between the BRICS and ASEAN, promoting greater regional integration and cooperation in areas like trade and infrastructure.

## Challenges and Limitations

Despite the benefits, Vietnam’s entry as a partner faces challenges. First, the “partner country” category limits its influence in BRICS decisions, which remain in the hands of full members. Additionally, regional tensions in the South China Sea could complicate Vietnam’s relationship with China, the main driver of the bloc’s expansion. Finally, the lack of internal cohesion within the BRICS, evidenced by divergences between China and India or differing stances on global conflicts, could hinder the full integration of partners like Vietnam.

## Conclusion

Vietnam’s entry as a partner country of the BRICS is a milestone in the bloc’s trajectory to establish itself as a global geopolitical and economic force. Its dynamic economy, strategic position in Asia, and alignment with Global South values strengthen the BRICS economically, geopolitically, and institutionally. The impacts include greater trade integration, expanded influence in Southeast Asia, and progress in the dedollarization and global reform agenda. However, challenges such as regional tensions and the bloc’s internal cohesion must be overcome to maximize the benefits of this partnership.

With Vietnam on board, the BRICS takes another step toward a more multipolar international order, challenging Western hegemony and promoting the sustainable development of emerging economies. The 2025 summit, under Brazilian presidency, will be crucial in defining how this partnership will be implemented and how Vietnam will contribute to the bloc’s continued growth.
