## A Breakthrough for Parkinson’s Symptoms

A New Hope for Parkinson’s Patients. For millions living with Parkinson’s disease, a chronic and progressive neurological condition, managing symptoms like tremors, rigidity, and slowed movements can be a daily challenge. While there is no cure for Parkinson’s, recent advancements in medical technology are offering new hope. One such breakthrough is the use of focused ultrasound, a non-invasive treatment that has shown remarkable results in reducing motor symptoms. In 2025, a 72-year-old man named Orlando Avendaño, who had struggled with tremors for 30 years, experienced life-changing relief after undergoing this procedure at the Delray Medical Center in Florida. This article explores how focused ultrasound is transforming the lives of Parkinson’s patients and what it means for the future of treatment.

## What Is Focused Ultrasound and How Does It Work?

Focused ultrasound (FUS) is a cutting-edge, non-invasive technique that uses high-frequency sound waves to target specific areas of the brain. For Parkinson’s patients, the procedure focuses on regions like the globus pallidus or subthalamic nucleus, which are linked to motor symptoms such as tremors and rigidity. During the treatment, the patient lies in an MRI scanner wearing a special helmet that directs ultrasound waves through the skull to the target area. The MRI provides real-time imaging, allowing doctors to precisely heat and ablate the dysfunctional tissue while preserving healthy areas. The patient remains awake, providing feedback to ensure the procedure effectively reduces symptoms.

Practical tip: If you’re considering this treatment, consult a neurologist to determine if you’re a candidate. Focused ultrasound is typically recommended for patients with motor symptoms like tremors that don’t respond well to medication.

Benefit: The procedure requires no incisions, anesthesia, or hospital stay, offering a safer alternative to traditional brain surgery.

## Orlando’s Story: A Life-Changing Transformation

Orlando Avendaño, a 72-year-old from the United States, had lived with debilitating tremors for three decades, a common symptom of Parkinson’s disease. These tremors made simple tasks like writing or using utensils nearly impossible, significantly impacting his quality of life. In 2025, Orlando underwent focused ultrasound treatment at the Delray Medical Center in Florida, a procedure that took just a few hours. Using a helmet-like device guided by MRI, doctors targeted the areas of his brain responsible for the tremors. The results were immediate—Orlando described the experience as "unbelievable," as his tremors disappeared during the session. For the first time in years, he could write steadily and eat without difficulty, reclaiming a sense of normalcy in his daily routine.

Practical tip: Research centers offering focused ultrasound, as it’s available in 79 neurological centers across the U.S. as of 2025, with growing adoption in Europe and Asia.

Benefit: Patients like Orlando can experience significant symptom relief, improving their independence and quality of life.

## The Science Behind the Breakthrough

Focused ultrasound has been studied extensively for its potential in treating neurological conditions. The technique was approved by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) in 2018 for Parkinson’s-related tremors and expanded in 2021 to address other symptoms like rigidity and dyskinesia. A 2023 study published in the New England Journal of Medicine found that 70% of Parkinson’s patients treated with FUS experienced symptom improvement, with two-thirds maintaining benefits for at least a year. The procedure works by destroying small, targeted areas of brain tissue that cause abnormal movements, without the need for invasive surgery. While effective for many, it’s not suitable for all patients—those in advanced stages of Parkinson’s or with cognitive impairments may not be candidates due to the procedure’s limitations.

Practical tip: Stay informed about eligibility criteria by discussing with a neurologist, as the treatment’s effectiveness varies based on individual symptoms and disease progression.

Benefit: Focused ultrasound offers a precise, low-risk option for symptom management, with minimal recovery time.

## Limitations and Considerations of Focused Ultrasound

While focused ultrasound is a groundbreaking treatment, it’s not a cure for Parkinson’s disease. The procedure addresses specific symptoms like tremors but does not halt the underlying degeneration of dopamine-producing cells in the brain. Some patients may experience side effects, such as temporary headaches, numbness, or speech difficulties, though these are often mild and resolve over time. In Orlando’s case, the treatment was a success, but long-term studies are still needed to assess its durability and broader applicability. Additionally, the cost can be a barrier—in the U.S., the procedure ranges from €25,000 to €35,000, and it’s not yet widely available in regions like Brazil, where regulatory approval is pending.

Practical tip: If cost is a concern, explore clinical trials or research programs that offer the treatment at reduced rates, such as those in Europe or Japan.

Benefit: Awareness of limitations helps set realistic expectations, ensuring patients make informed decisions.

## The Future of Parkinson’s Treatment

The success of focused ultrasound marks a significant step forward in Parkinson’s care, but researchers are also exploring other avenues, such as stem cell therapy, gene editing, and immunotherapy, to slow or reverse the disease’s progression. In Norway, where long winters can exacerbate feelings of isolation for Parkinson’s patients, integrating treatments like FUS with holistic approaches—such as community support and exercise—could further improve outcomes. As technology advances, we may see focused ultrasound applied to both sides of the brain in staged sessions, potentially addressing a wider range of symptoms. For now, this treatment offers a glimmer of hope, helping patients like Orlando reclaim parts of their lives that stress and symptoms had taken away.

Practical tip: Stay updated on emerging treatments by following organizations like the Michael J. Fox Foundation, which funds Parkinson’s research globally.

Benefit: Patients can access cutting-edge options as they become available, improving their long-term well-being.

The story of Orlando Avendaño highlights the transformative potential of focused ultrasound in managing Parkinson’s symptoms, offering a non-invasive way to reduce tremors and improve quality of life. While it’s not a cure, this breakthrough treatment provides significant relief for many patients, allowing them to regain independence in daily activities. From the fjords of Norway to urban centers worldwide, focused ultrasound is paving the way for a future where Parkinson’s symptoms are more manageable, even if the disease itself remains.
