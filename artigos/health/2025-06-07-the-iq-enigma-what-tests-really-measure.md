## By "A Curious Observer" about QI Test

In 1905, French psychologist Alfred Binet created the first IQ test to identify students who needed educational support. More than a century later, these tests still generate fascination, controversy, and questions: *Do they truly measure intelligence? Who benefits from them? And why do so many people revere — or reject — them?*

### What Is an IQ Test?

Intelligence Quotient (IQ) tests assess abilities such as:

- **Logical reasoning** (numerical sequences, visual patterns).

- **Working memory** (retention and manipulation of information).

- **Processing speed** (quick responses to stimuli).

- **Verbal knowledge** (vocabulary, comprehension).

*Classic examples:*

- **Raven's Matrices**: A non-verbal test that requires completing visual patterns.

- **Wechsler Adult Intelligence Scale (WAIS)**: The most widely used today, with diverse subtests.

- **Mensa**: A society that requires an IQ above the 98th percentile of the population for entry.

### The Importance — and Limitations

## Pros:

- **Diagnostic tool**: Identifies educational needs (such as giftedness or learning difficulties).

- **Scientific studies**: Correlates with academic and professional performance (in certain contexts).

## Cons:

- **Doesn't measure creativity, empathy, or practical wisdom**.

- **Cultural bias**: Questions may favor groups with certain educational backgrounds.

- **"Dynamic intelligence"**: IQ doesn't predict resilience, ethics, or the ability to learn from mistakes.

*Real-world example:* Physicist Richard Feynman had an IQ of 125 — considered "above average," but not genius. His genius, however, came from his curiosity and unique approach to problems.

### My Experience: A Personal Test

"When I decided to take an IQ test, I expected a confirmation — or denial — of my self-perception. The result was interesting, but the biggest lesson came later: I realized that **anxiety** during the test affected my performance, and that no number captures my passion for writing or my difficulty in drawing. An IQ is a blurred photograph of the mind: it shows outlines, but not the entire landscape." "Paulo Fernando de Barros IQ Test 117"

### The Future of Testing

Neuroscientists argue that, in the future, intelligence will be mapped by:

- **Emotional assessments** (like EQ — Emotional Quotient).

- **Analysis of adaptive skills** (solving real-world problems).

- **Collective intelligence** (how we collaborate in groups).

IQ tests are useful but reductionist tools. They don't define human potential — after all, stories like those of Einstein (whose IQ was estimated, not tested) or high-IQ but unhappy individuals prove that real intelligence lies in how we use what we have.
