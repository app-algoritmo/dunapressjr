In a world increasingly shaped by artificial intelligence and robotics, understanding the relationship between **taxes and automation** is crucial. As machines take over production, the question arises: how will societies sustain themselves? The answer is not magic—it's the intelligent redistribution of wealth through fair taxation. This is the foundation of a future where humans are free to live, learn, and create.

## The Role of Taxes in an Automated Future

With the rise of artificial intelligence and robotics, we stand on the brink of an unprecedented revolution. Machines are already performing tasks once thought impossible, and soon they will handle much of the world’s production.

But if human labor is gradually replaced, where will state funding come from? The answer remains the same: **taxation.** The difference is that the tax burden will shift—from human labor to automated productivity.

If fewer people are working in conventional jobs, then who funds the government? The answer is not to eliminate taxes—but to reconfigure them. In this new reality, **taxes and automation** must work together. Taxation will increasingly target automated systems and corporate productivity rather than just labor income.

***

## Robots Produce, Humans Live

This new model is not a threat—it is a historical opportunity. For the first time, humanity can produce in abundance without exhausting its workforce. Robots will work, algorithms will operate, and the wealth generated will be immense.

Meanwhile, humans will be **traveling, creating, studying, resting, and taking care of their health, families, and the planet.** But to make this freedom viable, we must ensure a fair taxation system on automated production.

***

## The Central Message Still Holds True

Even in this futuristic scenario, the foundational truth remains:

**"The State produces nothing on its own. Everything it manages comes from the work—and now also the machines—paid for through society’s taxes. Nothing the State offers is free. As an old saying goes: 'The State will never give you anything it hasn’t already taken from you.'"**

In other words, the technological miracle can only become a societal benefit **if supported by organized taxation, fair redistribution, and efficient public management.**

***

## Challenges and Opportunities of a New Fiscal Model

The new era will demand a new fiscal architecture:

- **Taxation on Automation:** Highly automated companies must contribute proportionally more, as they rely less on human labor while continuing to generate massive wealth.

- **Universal Basic Income:** Part of the tax revenue generated by machines can finance a basic income program, ensuring dignity for all citizens regardless of their participation in traditional labor markets.

- **Free Lifelong Education:** With more free time, people will seek knowledge. Public investment in education will be crucial to prepare conscious and creative citizens.

***

## The Future Is Collective, Built on Intelligence and Justice

There is no miracle. Not yesterday, not today, not tomorrow.

If we desire a prosperous future where robots work and humans live, we must ensure that the wealth generated by technology is returned to the people—through fair taxes, managed with integrity and a vision for the future.

The wealth created by machines should serve humanity—not replace it. The foundation remains the same: **production, contribution, redistribution.**

Robots don’t vote, don’t fall ill, and don’t dream—but they can help us build a world where we, as humans, can pursue those dreams with dignity and freedom.
