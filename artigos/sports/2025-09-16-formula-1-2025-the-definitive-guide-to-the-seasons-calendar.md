## Season Guide: Calendar, Driver Changes, and New Regulations

The 2025 Formula 1 season, marking the sport’s 75th anniversary, is a pivotal chapter in motorsport history. Launched on March 16, 2025, with the Australian Grand Prix in Melbourne, this record-equalling 24-race campaign spans five continents and introduces significant driver changes, regulatory updates, and a restructured calendar to accommodate global events like Ramadan. As the season unfolds, fans are witnessing the final year of the current power unit and ground-effect car regulations before a major overhaul in 2026. This article dives into the confirmed facts—calendar details, driver line-ups, and rule changes—based on official announcements from the FIA and Formula 1, offering a comprehensive guide to the 2025 season.

### The 2025 Calendar: A Global Journey

The 2025 season, approved by the FIA World Motor Sport Council, comprises 24 Grands Prix, matching the record set in 2024. The calendar, announced on April 12, 2024, prioritizes geographical flow to enhance sustainability and reduce freight logistics, with a three-week summer break in August. Ramadan’s occurrence in March prompted a reshuffle, moving Bahrain and Saudi Arabia to April and restoring Australia as the season opener for the first time since 2019. Pre-season testing took place at the Bahrain International Circuit from February 26-28, 2025, setting the stage for the campaign.

Here is the full 2025 race schedule, including Sprint weekends:

- **March 14-16**: Australian Grand Prix, Melbourne

- **March 21-23**: Chinese Grand Prix, Shanghai (Sprint)

- **April 4-6**: Japanese Grand Prix, Suzuka

- **April 11-13**: Bahrain Grand Prix, Sakhir

- **April 18-20**: Saudi Arabian Grand Prix, Jeddah

- **May 2-4**: Miami Grand Prix, Miami (Sprint)

- **May 16-18**: Emilia-Romagna Grand Prix, Imola

- **May 23-25**: Monaco Grand Prix, Monte Carlo

- **June 6-8**: Spanish Grand Prix, Barcelona

- **June 13-15**: Canadian Grand Prix, Montreal

- **June 27-29**: Austrian Grand Prix, Spielberg

- **July 4-6**: British Grand Prix, Silverstone

- **July 25-27**: Hungarian Grand Prix, Budapest

- **August 1-3**: Belgian Grand Prix, Spa-Francorchamps (Sprint)

- **August 29-31**: Dutch Grand Prix, Zandvoort

- **September 5-7**: Italian Grand Prix, Monza

- **September 19-21**: Azerbaijan Grand Prix, Baku

- **October 3-5**: Singapore Grand Prix, Marina Bay

- **October 17-19**: United States Grand Prix, Austin (Sprint)

- **October 24-26**: Mexican Grand Prix, Mexico City

- **November 7-9**: São Paulo Grand Prix, Interlagos (Sprint)

- **November 20-22**: Las Vegas Grand Prix, Las Vegas

- **November 28-30**: Qatar Grand Prix, Lusail (Sprint)

- **December 5-7**: Abu Dhabi Grand Prix, Yas Marina

The six Sprint races, held in China, Miami, Belgium, Austin, Brazil, and Qatar, continue the 100km Saturday race format introduced in 2021. The season’s structure includes double-headers (like Australia-China and Qatar-Abu Dhabi) and a triple-header (Emilia-Romagna, Monaco, Spain) to fit the dense schedule within the year.

### Driver Changes: A New Era on the Grid

The 2025 season features one of the most dramatic driver market shake-ups in recent memory, headlined by Lewis Hamilton’s move to Ferrari. After 12 years and six world championships with Mercedes, the seven-time champion replaced Carlos Sainz Jr., who joined Williams on a multi-year deal. This shift ended Hamilton’s record for consecutive seasons with one constructor and marks his first campaign without a Mercedes power unit.

Other notable changes include:

- **Mercedes**: Promoted 18-year-old Andrea Kimi Antonelli from Formula 2 to replace Hamilton, pairing him with George Russell.

- **Red Bull**: Replaced Sergio Pérez with Liam Lawson, who joins Max Verstappen after a strong 2024 as a reserve.

- **Haas**: Fielded an all-new line-up with Ollie Bearman, a Ferrari junior who raced in 2024, and Esteban Ocon, who left Alpine. Nico Hülkenberg moved to Sauber (now Kick Sauber, transitioning to Audi in 2026).

- **Sauber**: Signed Hülkenberg and F2 champion Gabriel Bortoleto for a fresh pairing.

- **Alpine**: Promoted Jack Doohan, with Franco Colapinto as a reserve after a mid-2024 stint at Williams.

- **Racing Bulls**: Elevated Isack Hadjar alongside Yuki Tsunoda, replacing Daniel Ricciardo.

Only McLaren (Lando Norris and Oscar Piastri) and Aston Martin (Fernando Alonso and Lance Stroll) retained their 2024 line-ups, ensuring continuity. The influx of rookies—Antonelli, Bearman, Bortoleto, Doohan, and Hadjar—signals a youth movement, with teams mandated to field drivers with fewer than two Grand Prix starts in four free practice sessions (twice per car) to nurture talent.

### Regulatory Updates: Safety, Weight, and Testing

The 2025 season is the final year of the power unit configuration introduced in 2014 and the ground-effect cars launched in 2022, with major changes slated for 2026 (including the removal of the MGU-H and DRS). For 2025, the FIA introduced several tweaks to enhance safety, fairness, and competition:

- **Weight Increase**: The minimum driver weight, including seat and gear, rose from 80 kg to 82 kg, raising the car’s minimum weight (without fuel) from 798 kg to 800 kg. This benefits taller or heavier drivers, leveling the playing field.

- **Driver Cooling System**: Following the 2023 Qatar Grand Prix’s extreme heat, a cooling vest system (with 48m of piping) is optional for races above 30.5°C, adding up to 5 kg to the car’s weight if used. It becomes mandatory in 2026.

- **Pit Lane Start Procedure**: Cars starting from the pit lane must join the formation lap in prescribed order (unless delayed) before returning to the pit exit, streamlining race starts.

- **Testing Restrictions**: Testing of Previous Cars (TPC) is capped at 20 days per team, with championship drivers limited to 1,000 km over four days. Testing is banned within 60 days of a race at the same circuit or on significantly modified tracks.

- **Flexi-Wing Crackdown**: Rear wings face stricter flex tests from the Australian Grand Prix, limiting mainplane flex to 6mm and upper flap to 7mm. Front wing tests tighten from the Spanish Grand Prix, reducing maximum flex from 15mm to 10mm.

- **Fastest Lap Point Removed**: The bonus point for the fastest lap, introduced in 2019, was scrapped after controversies, such as Daniel Ricciardo’s strategic pit stop in Singapore 2024.

- **Gearbox Penalties Eliminated**: Grid penalties for exceeding gearbox limits were removed, giving teams more flexibility.

- **Qualifying Contingency**: If qualifying or Sprint qualifying is canceled (e.g., due to weather), the grid will be set by the Drivers’ Championship order or stewards’ discretion for the season opener.

These changes aim to enhance safety, reduce controversial strategies, and prepare teams for the 2026 overhaul, which includes 100% sustainable fuels and active aerodynamics.

### Teams and Technical Context

All 10 teams—Red Bull, McLaren, Ferrari, Mercedes, Aston Martin, Alpine, Williams, Racing Bulls, Haas, and Sauber—compete with Pirelli tires and two mandatory drivers per team. Renault’s Alpine will exit as an engine supplier after 2025, with Ferrari powering Haas and Sauber, and Red Bull Powertrains gearing up for 2026 with Ford. The season also marks the last use of the Drag Reduction System (DRS) before its replacement by active aerodynamics in 2026.

A landmark event, F1 75 Live, held on February 18, 2025, at The O2 Arena in London, saw all teams unveil their 2025 liveries in a collective celebration of the sport’s history. Broadcast on Sky Sports, ESPN, and F1’s social media, it drew millions, setting a festive tone.

### The Broader Picture

The 2025 season reflects Formula 1’s global reach and commitment to sustainability. Efforts like Aggreko’s low-carbon power solutions for European races and optimized freight logistics underscore environmental goals. The calendar’s regionalized flow—Asia in spring, Europe in summer, Americas in autumn, and Middle East to close—reduces carbon emissions while delivering high-octane racing to fans worldwide.

With the season now at its Azerbaijan Grand Prix (September 19-21), the stage is set for intense battles across iconic circuits like Singapore, Austin, and Interlagos. The driver changes, particularly Hamilton’s Ferrari move and the rookie influx, promise fresh rivalries, while regulatory tweaks ensure fairness and safety. As the final season before a technical revolution, 2025 is a bridge between eras, blending tradition with innovation.

Whether you’re a lifelong fan or new to the sport, the 2025 Formula 1 season offers a spectacle of speed, strategy, and human ambition. From Melbourne’s sunlit straights to Abu Dhabi’s twilight finish, every race is a chapter in F1’s storied legacy.
