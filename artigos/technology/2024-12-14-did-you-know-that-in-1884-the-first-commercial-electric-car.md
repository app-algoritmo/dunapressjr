At the end of the 19th and the beginning of the 20th century, electric cars promised to revolutionize mobility. In fact, by 1900, a third of all vehicles on the road were electric. In cities like New York and London, where electric motors provided quiet and emission-free transportation, electric cars were a popular choice, especially among the upper classes. However, this technology began to disappear quickly around 1920 due to the rise of internal combustion engines.

The mass production introduced by Henry Ford, combined with the gasoline engine's ability to cover long distances and the availability of cheap oil, pushed electric vehicles into the background. However, the initial impact of electric cars left a significant mark.

## Visionary Innovators

In 1898, Ferdinand Porsche, founder of the renowned sports car brand, developed the **Porsche P1**, an electric vehicle that marked the beginning of an era. Shortly after, Porsche also designed the world’s first hybrid vehicle, which used an internal combustion engine combined with electricity, foreshadowing modern technologies.

Another pioneer, Mercedes-Benz, introduced the **Mercedes Mixte** in 1906, an electric car that was even adopted as a taxi in several cities. This model also stood out in racing competitions, becoming a race car in 1907.

## Electric Technology in Heavy Industry

The photograph accompanying this article takes us to the United Kingdom in 1917, where a group of electric trucks operated efficiently in the industrial era. These vehicles, equipped with plug-in charging systems, were used in urban settings and were notable for their functionality and sustainability.

## The Return of an Old Idea

Today, we are witnessing the true reincarnation of this century-old technology. Advances in lithium batteries, more efficient electric motors, and greater environmental awareness have fueled a new wave of electric vehicles dominating the market. Automotive giants like Tesla and new hybrid and electric offerings from historic brands like Porsche and Mercedes-Benz have proven that electric mobility is not only viable but essential for the future of transportation.
