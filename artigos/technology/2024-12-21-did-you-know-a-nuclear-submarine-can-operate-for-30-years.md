Nuclear energy has enabled technological advancements that once seemed taken straight out of a science fiction novel. One of the most impressive examples is the operation of nuclear submarines, which can function for three decades with a minimal amount of fuel: just 5 to 6 kilograms of highly enriched uranium (HEU).

## How Is This Possible?

The uranium used in these submarines' reactors is enriched to levels exceeding 90% uranium-235, an isotope that allows for a solid and efficient chain reaction. During the nuclear fission process, uranium-235 atoms split, releasing enormous amounts of energy. To put this into perspective, a single kilogram of uranium-235 produces around 24 million kilowatt-hours of energy. This energy capacity is what enables nuclear submarines to achieve unprecedented autonomy, eliminating the need for refueling like conventional submarines.

## Advantages of Nuclear Submarines

- **Extended Autonomy:** Nuclear submarines can carry out missions for long periods without concerns about fuel supply. This allows them to stay underwater for significantly longer durations than diesel-electric submarines.

- **Energy Efficiency:** Thanks to the energy density of enriched uranium, nuclear reactors generate enough power not only to propel the submarine but also to supply its electrical systems and weaponry.

- **Reduced Logistical Risks:** By not depending on refueling stations, nuclear submarines are less vulnerable in conflict situations, enhancing their strategic capabilities.

## A Step Toward the Future

Advances in nuclear technology not only benefit the military field but also have implications for the development of clean and sustainable energy in other areas. However, these achievements also bring ethical and environmental challenges that must be addressed to ensure a balance between progress and sustainability.

Nuclear submarines are a testament to human ingenuity, showing how a small amount of material can sustain massive operations for decades. They exemplify how science and technology continue to challenge the limits of what we thought was possible.
