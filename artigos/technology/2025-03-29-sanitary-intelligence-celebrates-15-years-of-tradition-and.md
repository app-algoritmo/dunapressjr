**This year, Sanitary Intelligence, an international reference in sanitary regulation, celebrates 15 years of tradition and commitment to innovation in the sector.** To mark this historic milestone, the institution launched a new podcast hosted by Professor Dr. Natália Bellan, a renowned scientist in the field of medical device, pharmaceutical, and health technology regulation.

The podcast, titled **“Regulation and Innovation: Global Dialogues,”** aims to broadly and deeply address all categories of regulated products and services across different parts of the world. The initiative not only seeks to disseminate knowledge about health regulation, but also to foster essential discussions on the challenges and technological advances that impact the industry and global public health.

***

## **First Episode: Analysis of the IMDRF Meeting in Japan

The first live episode of the podcast featured an in-depth analysis of the recent **International Medical Device Regulators Forum (IMDRF)** meeting held in Japan. The event brought together experts and regulators from various countries to discuss critical issues related to regulatory harmonization and medical device safety.

During the episode, Professor Natália Bellan highlighted the key topics addressed in the meeting, including:

- **Trends and updates in global health regulation;**

- **Challenges to regulatory harmonization between different countries;**

- **The impact of emerging technologies, such as Artificial Intelligence, on the healthcare sector.**

The broadcast generated significant engagement from the academic community and regulatory industry professionals, establishing the podcast as an essential platform for high-level information and discussion within the sector.

***

## **A Milestone for Global Health Regulation

With 15 years of history, Sanitary Intelligence continues to expand its impact and contribute to the development of more efficient and accessible regulatory frameworks. The launch of the podcast reaffirms its commitment to knowledge dissemination and the promotion of fundamental discussions for the future of health regulation.
