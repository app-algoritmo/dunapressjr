## BRICS vs OECD Tech Innovations 2025

In the rapidly evolving landscape of global technology, 2025 has emerged as a pivotal year for international alliances to showcase their commitments to innovation. BRICS vs OECD tech innovations 2025 highlight contrasting yet complementary approaches to harnessing science, technology, and collaboration for economic growth and societal challenges.

The BRICS group, comprising Brazil, Russia, India, China, South Africa, and its expanded members like Egypt, Ethiopia, Iran, Saudi Arabia, and the UAE, is focusing on multilateral projects that emphasize digital sovereignty and cross-border research. Meanwhile, the OECD, representing mostly developed economies such as the United States, European nations, Japan, and Australia, is prioritizing policy reforms to mobilize innovation for transformative change. This comparison reveals how these blocs are navigating geopolitical shifts, climate imperatives, and the rise of artificial intelligence, all while addressing the need for inclusive development.

The year 2025 has seen BRICS nations ramp up their efforts through coordinated calls and summits, aiming to build resilient tech ecosystems independent of Western dominance. On the other hand, OECD countries are rethinking their science and technology policies to balance openness with security concerns. By examining these developments, we can understand the broader implications for global tech leadership and cooperation. BRICS vs OECD tech innovations 2025 not only reflect current priorities but also set the stage for future geopolitical dynamics in innovation.

### BRICS Tech Innovations 2025: Collaborative Drives and Competitions

BRICS countries have made significant strides in 2025, launching initiatives that foster innovation through joint funding and competitions. One standout development is the 1st coordinated call for BRICS innovation projects 2025, which encourages multilateral research in high-tech areas. This bottom-up approach allows BRICS nations to pool resources for projects that drive technological maturity and market impact, with applications open until early November 2025. This call is part of the broader BRICS STI Framework Programme, emphasizing cooperation in fields like advanced manufacturing and green technologies.

Building on this, the BRICS Action Plan for Innovation 2025-2030 outlines a strategic roadmap for the group, which now represents nearly half of the world's population. The plan focuses on deepening scientific ties to accelerate economic and social development, including shared projects in digital infrastructure and AI. For instance, at the Digital BRICS Forum in May 2025, the Joint Research Report on BRICS Strength in the Digital Economy 2025 was released, highlighting the bloc's growing prowess in digital technologies and its potential to rival established powers.

Competitions have also played a key role in spotlighting BRICS vs OECD tech innovations 2025. The BRICS Technological Innovation Competition, held as part of the 14th InnovaCities event, featured categories such as Digital Medical Assistance, Advanced Manufacturing, Green Energy, Artificial Intelligence, Virtual Simulation, Digital Agriculture, and Technologies of the Future. This event, scheduled for September 2025 in Foz do Iguaçu, Brazil, aims to showcase inventions that address urban challenges and sustainable development, drawing participants from across the BRICS nations. Similarly, the BRICS Urban Innovation Award 2025 recognizes solutions that accelerate Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), particularly in urban settings.

The expansion of BRICS in 2024 has further amplified these efforts in 2025. New members like the UAE are leveraging the platform for projects in innovation and connectivity, including independent fiber-optic networks, satellite systems, and cloud infrastructure. AI collaboration is another highlight, with joint working groups exploring applications in healthcare and climate resilience. The BRICS Global Innovation Drive, launched recently, boosts cross-border research and technology transfer, aiming to strengthen economic ties among Brazil, Russia, India, China, and South Africa, along with the newcomers.

The 17th BRICS Summit in Rio de Janeiro in July 2025 was a landmark event, resulting in 126 commitments across global governance, finance, health, artificial intelligence, and climate change. Leaders agreed on joint approaches to technology, including AI governance and sustainable tech initiatives. Discussions emphasized science and technology cooperation in AI, climate change, and healthcare, reinforcing the bloc's push for multipolarity. For example, automated factories, biotechnology, small modular nuclear reactors, drones, sodium-ion batteries, and AI processing were identified as priority areas for collaboration. This summit also highlighted practical measures for economic and technological autonomy, shifting from rhetoric to actionable projects.

On the ground, initiatives like the Innovation Bridge Portal's call for applications underscore grassroots involvement. Researchers, innovators, SMEs, and universities are invited to participate in multinational R&D projects, with deadlines extending into December 2025 in some cases. These efforts reflect BRICS' strategy to counterbalance OECD dominance by fostering innovation tailored to emerging markets' needs.

### OECD Tech Innovations 2025: Policy Reforms and Transformative Outlooks

Shifting focus to the OECD, 2025 has been marked by introspective reports and policy shifts aimed at adapting innovation systems to contemporary challenges. The OECD Science, Technology and Innovation Outlook 2025, released in October, explores how STI can support transformative change in economies and societies. It emphasizes mobilizing science and technology for green transitions, digitalization, and social inclusion, while calling for improved policy coherence and public-private partnerships.

A key theme in OECD tech innovations 2025 is the rethinking of science and tech policy to balance openness with economic security. The report highlights the need to maintain international collaboration that drives progress while addressing risks like technology leakage. This includes expanding benefits from STI investments and exploring technology convergence trends. Policies are evolving to include societal impact goals, moving beyond traditional growth metrics.

Government innovation trends identified by the OECD in early 2025 focus on personalized, co-created services that enhance public participation. These trends cover building more inclusive services and using them as platforms for civic engagement, reflecting a shift toward user-centric innovation. The organization's project on transforming STI systems underscores the need for evolution in a fast-changing world, ensuring relevance amid geopolitical tensions.

Artificial intelligence remains a cornerstone, with the OECD.AI Policy Observatory tracking over 900 national AI policies and initiatives. This platform promotes trustworthy AI aligned with OECD principles, influencing member countries' strategies. Complementing this, the UNCTAD Technology and Innovation Report 2025, while not exclusively OECD, aligns with their focus on inclusive AI for development, providing insights applicable to developed economies. Additionally, the 2025 AI Index Report from Stanford offers a comprehensive view of AI trends, which OECD nations often reference for policy-making.

The OECD Ministerial Council Meeting in June 2025, chaired by Costa Rica, further advanced discussions on innovation policies, emphasizing predictability for research and business. Business highlights from the outlook stress the importance of stable policies to foster innovation, aligning with broader efforts to reconfigure scientific cooperation in a changing geopolitical environment.

### Comparative Analysis of BRICS vs OECD Tech Innovations 2025

When comparing BRICS vs OECD tech innovations 2025, several differences emerge. BRICS initiatives are often project-oriented, with calls for proposals and competitions that directly fund collaborative R&D, such as the STI Framework Programme. This contrasts with OECD's emphasis on policy frameworks and outlooks that guide national strategies, like the STI Outlook 2025. BRICS prioritizes digital sovereignty and autonomy, evident in infrastructure projects and AI collaborations among emerging powers. OECD, meanwhile, focuses on openness and security, promoting international standards while protecting intellectual property.

Similarities exist in areas like AI and climate tech. Both groups address AI governance—BRICS through summit commitments, OECD via its AI observatory. Sustainability is another overlap, with BRICS' green energy competitions mirroring OECD's transformative policies for green transitions. However, BRICS' approach is more geared toward rapid catch-up and multipolarity, while OECD aims to maintain leadership through refined policies.

Geopolitically, BRICS vs OECD tech innovations 2025 underscore a divide: BRICS seeks to challenge the status quo with inclusive models for the Global South, as seen in its expansion and digital economy report. OECD, representing wealthier nations, focuses on evolving existing systems to tackle shared challenges like digitalization. This dynamic could lead to increased competition or potential bridges in areas like health tech and climate innovation.

Looking ahead, the outcomes of these initiatives will shape global tech landscapes. BRICS' hands-on projects may yield quick wins in emerging technologies, while OECD's policy-driven approach ensures long-term sustainability. As 2025 progresses, monitoring these developments will be crucial for understanding shifts in innovation power.

BRICS vs OECD tech innovations 2025 demonstrate diverse strategies for leveraging technology amid global uncertainties. From BRICS' collaborative competitions to OECD's forward-looking outlooks, these efforts highlight the importance of innovation in addressing pressing issues like climate change and digital divides.
