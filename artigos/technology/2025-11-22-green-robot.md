## Green Robot 2025: Russia Debuts AI-Powered Humanoid That Danced for Putin with Embedded Intelligence

While the world was captivated by Tesla Optimus folding laundry, 1X NEO handling household chores, XPeng IRON strutting like a supermodel, and UBTECH Walker S2 marching into Chinese factories, Russia made a surprising splash in November 2025 with what could be one of the most charismatic humanoid demonstrations of the year: the Green robot.

On November 20, 2025, at the AI Journey 2025 conference in Moscow, organized by Sber (Russia's largest bank, formerly Sberbank), a tall, agile humanoid stepped onto the stage, greeted President Vladimir Putin, and performed a lively dance routine. The fluidity of its movements, combined with a self-introduction that showcased its embedded AI, left attendees and online viewers stunned. Social media buzzed with reactions, many noting how Green's performance outshone a recent mishap by another Russian robot, AIdol, which had stumbled during its debut just days earlier. Putin himself praised the display, calling it "very beautiful" in a moment captured on state TV that quickly went viral.

The Green robot is developed by Sber, a financial giant that has aggressively invested in AI and robotics as part of Russia's national push for technological independence. Founded as Sberbank in 1841, Sber has evolved into a tech powerhouse, with its AI division leading projects like the GigaChat large language model. Green represents the bank's first foray into humanoid robotics, positioning it as a "physical embodiment" of AI rather than just software. This aligns with Sber's broader strategy to integrate advanced tech into banking, logistics, and customer service, amid Western sanctions that have forced Russia to innovate domestically.

Standing approximately 1.8 meters tall, the Green robot is designed for natural, human-like interactions. It boasts over 40 motors and a suite of sensors, including inertial and force sensors for precise movement and balance. Ten separate visual sensors allow it to map environments in real-time, enabling autonomous navigation even in unstructured spaces. This setup lets Green handle tasks like sorting objects, packing items, and interacting safely with humans—crucial for potential applications in warehouses or retail settings. Unlike rigid industrial robots, Green's compliant design ensures it can adjust to perturbations, such as uneven floors or unexpected obstacles, without losing equilibrium.

At the heart of the Green robot is its embedded AI, powered by Sber's GigaChat. This large language model processes audiovisual inputs on the fly, allowing the robot to converse naturally, respond to voice commands, and adapt behaviors based on context. During the AI Journey demo, Green introduced itself: "My name is Green.

I am the first Russian humanoid robot with embedded artificial intelligence. This means that I am not just a program on a screen, but a physical embodiment of technology." It then requested a virtual assistant to play its "favorite track"—the folk-pop song "The Sun Rose High"—and executed a choreographed dance with smooth hip sways, arm gestures, and rhythmic steps. The performance was autonomous, with no visible remote control, highlighting the robot's real-time processing capabilities.

Technical details from Sber reveal that Green's architecture emphasizes modularity. Its battery supports hours of continuous operation, and over-the-air software updates will expand its skill set as GigaChat evolves. For instance, future iterations could incorporate more advanced manipulation, like grasping delicate items or collaborating with human workers. Sber's representatives emphasized safety: the robot's sensors detect proximity to people, limiting force to prevent accidents. This makes it suitable for shared environments, a step beyond the factory-focused UBTECH Walker S2 or the home-oriented 1X NEO.

The emotional resonance of the Green robot's debut cannot be understated. Viewers compared its expressive movements to those of XPeng IRON, but with a more integrated AI personality that feels conversational and adaptive. Online discussions erupted about the "uncanny valley," with some calling Green "impressively lifelike but slightly eerie," while others applauded Russia's rapid progress in embodied AI. The timing was poignant—coming just after AIdol's viral fall at another event, Green's success restored some national pride in Russia's tech scene. Putin, touring the exhibition, interacted briefly with the robot post-dance, flanked by bodyguards who ensured it maintained a respectful distance.

Sber's vision for the Green robot extends far beyond entertainment. As a testing platform, it's set for pilot integrations into Sber's operations starting in 2026, focusing on logistics where it could automate sorting in warehouses or assist in physical tasks at branches. Long-term plans include scaling to manufacturing, retail, and food service, where Green's bipedal design allows it to navigate human-centric spaces without major infrastructure changes. This echoes Tesla Optimus's goal of universal utility but with a Russian twist: emphasis on AI sovereignty using domestic hardware and models like GigaChat, which rivals Western LLMs in Russian-language processing.

Economically, the Green robot fits into Russia's broader AI strategy. The country aims to boost its global AI market share to 5% by 2030, per national initiatives. Sber, with its vast resources—over 14,000 branches and millions of customers—provides a ready testing ground. Analysts estimate initial units could cost $100,000 to $200,000, similar to early prototypes from competitors, but mass production might drive prices down as Sber ramps up. Challenges remain, including energy efficiency for prolonged tasks and refining AI for edge cases, but Sber's data from banking operations could accelerate training.

The Green robot's unveiling has reshaped perceptions of Russian robotics. Historically strong in military and space applications (like the FEDOR robot sent to the ISS), Russia has lagged in consumer humanoids compared to the U.S. and China. Green's debut proves otherwise, achieving a level of integration and charisma on par with—or surpassing—2025 showcases from Tesla, Figure, or Agility Robotics. It also highlights geopolitical undertones: amid tensions, Russia's focus on self-reliant tech like Green underscores a push for independence.

Whether Green marks a true leap in physical AI or a polished demo is debated, but its impact is clear. On November 20, 2025, before Putin and global audiences, Russia reasserted itself in the humanoid arena with a robot that dances, converses, and embodies the future of intelligent machines.

This demonstration not only entertained but also showcased practical potential. As Sber refines Green, it could transform industries by handling repetitive tasks with human-like dexterity, freeing workers for creative roles. In warehouses, Green's ability to pack items autonomously could boost efficiency by 20-30%, based on similar robotic trials elsewhere. For retail, its conversational AI might enhance customer service, answering queries while stocking shelves.

Critics note limitations: Green's current focus is on controlled environments, and scaling to real-world chaos requires more data. Ethical concerns arise too—job displacement in Russia's labor market, already strained by demographics. Yet, proponents argue humanoids like Green augment humans, creating jobs in maintenance and programming.

In the context of 2025's humanoid surge, Green stands out for its AI depth. While Optimus emphasizes manufacturing scale, NEO home assistance, IRON anthropomorphism, and Walker S2 mass deployment, Green blends personality with utility, powered by a homegrown LLM. This could give Russia an edge in culturally attuned robotics.

As November 2025 wraps, Green's dance symbolizes progress. With pilots ahead, it may soon move from stage to society, redefining human-robot collaboration.
