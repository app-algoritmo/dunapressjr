## Norway National Day May 17: Vibrant Traditions

A Day of Joy and Pride in Norway. Tomorrow, May 17, 2025, marks Norway National Day, a vibrant celebration of the country’s independence and cultural heritage, also known as Constitution Day or "Syttende Mai" (Seventeenth of May). This annual event commemorates the signing of Norway’s Constitution on May 17, 1814, a pivotal moment in the nation’s history. Unlike many national days that focus on military displays, Norway’s celebration is a joyful, community-driven affair, with a special emphasis on children, parades, and traditional festivities.

From Oslo’s grand parades to Bergen’s unique Buekorps drummers, the day is filled with music, food, and national pride. In this article, we’ll explore 10 insights into the traditions of Norway National Day May 17, offering a glimpse into its history and how you can join the celebration. Ready to experience Norway’s biggest party? Let’s dive in!

## Tip 1: The Historical Significance of May 17

Norway National Day May 17 celebrates the signing of the Norwegian Constitution in 1814 at Eidsvoll, marking the country’s push for independence.

Background: The constitution, inspired by the U.S. Declaration of Independence and the French Revolution, declared Norway an independent kingdom after centuries under Danish rule, though it entered a union with Sweden until 1905. The first public celebration began in 1833 with a speech by poet Henrik Wergeland, and it became more formal in 1870 with the first children’s parade in Christiania (now Oslo).

Practical tip: Visit Eidsvoll Manor virtually via [Eidsvoll 1814](https://www.eidsvoll1814.no) to learn about this historic event.

Benefit: Understanding the day’s origins deepens your appreciation of Norway’s cultural pride.

## Tip 2: Children’s Parades as the Heart of the Celebration

A hallmark of Norway National Day May 17 is the children’s parades, which are the centerpiece of festivities across the country.

Tradition: Unlike many national days with military displays, Norway focuses on children, symbolizing the nation’s future. In Oslo, over 100,000 people join the largest parade, with 120 schools marching past the Royal Palace, where the royal family waves from the balcony. Bergen features two parades, including the unique Buekorps drummers, a tradition dating back to the 19th century.

Practical tip: Watch the Oslo parade live on national television or join smaller parades in towns like Tromsø or Stavanger.

Benefit: Showcases Norway’s inclusive, family-friendly celebration of national identity.

## Tip 3: The Iconic Bunad – Norway’s Traditional Costume

Wearing the bunad on Norway National Day May 17 is a cherished tradition, reflecting regional heritage and cultural pride.

Details: The bunad, a traditional folk costume, varies by region, with colors and styles indicating ancestral origins, such as Trøndelag or Hardanger. Up to 30% of Norwegians wear bunads on this day, creating a spectacular visual display during parades.

Practical tip: Learn about bunad variations at [Norsk Folkemuseum](https://www.norskfolkemuseum.no) in Oslo, which showcases Norway’s cultural attire.

Benefit: Highlights the diversity and craftsmanship of Norwegian cultural heritage.

## Tip 4: Festive Foods and Indulgences

Norway National Day May 17 is a feast of traditional foods, with a special focus on treats for children.

Cuisine: The day often starts with a hearty breakfast buffet of smoked salmon, scrambled eggs, and champagne, followed by hot dogs, ice cream, and waffles sold by street vendors. Traditional dishes like rømmegrøt (sour cream porridge) with cured meats or Kvæfjordkake (Norway’s national cake) are also popular.

Practical tip: Try making a traditional lefserull with smoked salmon, as shared on [Visit Norway](https://www.visitnorway.com).

Benefit: Brings people together through shared culinary traditions, adding to the festive spirit.

## Tip 5: The Russ Celebration – A Youth Tradition

High school graduates, known as "russ," add a lively element to Norway National Day May 17 celebrations.

Tradition: Since 1905, graduating students (russ) celebrate their final year with colorful overalls (red, blue, or other colors based on their study focus), decorated vans, and parties. On May 17, they join parades, handing out russekort (calling cards) to children as souvenirs.

Practical tip: Ask a russ for their russekort as a keepsake while watching the parades.

Benefit: Adds a youthful, energetic vibe to the day’s festivities.

## Tip 6: Music and the National Anthem

Music plays a central role on Norway National Day May 17, with marching bands and the national anthem filling the air.

Details: Bands lead parades, playing traditional songs like the national anthem, "Ja, vi elsker dette landet," written by Bjørnstjerne Bjørnson, alongside other favorites like "Norway in Red, White, and Blue." In Bergen, locals also sing their city anthem, "Nystemten."

Practical tip: Listen to the national anthem and other traditional songs on [NRK](https://www.nrk.no).

Benefit: Creates a festive atmosphere and reinforces national pride through music.

## Tip 7: Flags and National Symbols Everywhere

Norway National Day May 17 is a sea of red, white, and blue, with the Norwegian flag as the central symbol.

Tradition: Flags adorn homes, streets, and even cakes, while people wear red, white, and blue ribbons. During parades, children wave small flags, and the flag is raised in ceremonies across the country.

Practical tip: Join locals by wearing a ribbon in Norway’s colors or waving a flag during the celebrations.

Benefit: Symbolizes unity and national pride, creating a visually stunning celebration.

## Tip 8: Community Activities and Festivities

Beyond parades, Norway National Day May 17 features a range of community activities and events for all ages.

Events: In Oslo, the day includes memorial services, speeches, and a salute at noon, while Bergen hosts dinghy sailing and fireworks at Festplassen. Across Norway, schools organize games, concerts, and family gatherings, often with ice cream and hot dogs aplenty.

Practical tip: Check local programs on [Visit Oslo](https://www.visitoslo.com) or municipal websites for event schedules.

Benefit: Ensures everyone can participate in the day’s joyous atmosphere.

## Tip 9: Global Celebrations of Syttende Mai

Norway National Day May 17 is celebrated not just in Norway but also by Norwegian communities worldwide.

Global Reach: In the U.S., places like Brooklyn’s Bay Ridge and Petersburg, Alaska ("Little Norway"), host parades and festivities. In New Zealand, Fiordland and Norsewood celebrate with folk dancing and Norwegian songs. In the UK, Southwark Park in London hosts a major event with bunad displays and activities.

Practical tip: Join a celebration near you, such as the one in Southwark Park, detailed on [17 Mai London](https://17mai.london).

Benefit: Connects Norwegians globally, fostering a sense of shared heritage.

## Tip 10: Tips for Visitors Joining the Celebration

Visitors are warmly welcomed to join Norway National Day May 17, with a few practical tips to enhance the experience.

Advice: Dress in formal attire or festive clothing (avoid jeans to blend in), plan for road closures during parades, and book restaurant tables in advance. In Oslo, public transport adjusts routes, so plan ahead. In Bergen, the Light Rail skips central stops until 3 PM, as noted by [Visit Bergen](https://en.visitbergen.com).

Practical tip: Greet locals with “Gratulerer med dagen” (Happy Birthday) to join the festive spirit.

Benefit: Ensures a smooth and enjoyable experience for travelers joining the celebrations.

## Join the Celebration of Syttende Mai

Norway National Day May 17 is a joyous celebration of independence, culture, and community, marked by children’s parades, traditional bunad costumes, festive foods, and a shared sense of national pride. Whether you’re in Oslo watching the royal family wave from the palace, in Bergen enjoying the Buekorps drummers, or joining global celebrations in places like London or New Zealand, this day offers a unique glimpse into Norwegian heritage.
