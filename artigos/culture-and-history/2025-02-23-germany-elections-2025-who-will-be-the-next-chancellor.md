The German federal elections take place today, February 23, 2025, and they promise to be one of the most competitive in recent history. The collapse of the coalition government led by current Chancellor Olaf Scholz, combined with economic challenges and heated debates over migration and security, has made this election a decisive turning point for Germany’s future.

With a parliament consisting of 630 seats, approximately 59 million voters will determine who will lead Europe’s largest economy in the coming years. Below are the main candidates and their proposals.

!imagem

***

## **Friedrich Merz (CDU/CSU) – The Conservative Leader Focused on Economic Growth

- **Party:** Christian Democratic Union (CDU) and Christian Social Union (CSU)

- **Profile:** With extensive experience in the private sector and politics, Friedrich Merz has returned to the political arena with proposals aimed at economic growth, security, and strengthening Germany’s sovereignty.

## Main Proposals:

- **Economy:** Reducing bureaucracy and taxes for businesses, boosting industrial production, and securing more affordable energy to ensure competitiveness.

- **Security and Immigration:** Strengthening borders and implementing stricter measures for deporting illegal immigrants.

- **Defense:** Reinforcing Germany’s military capabilities to ensure national security and reduce dependence on international allies.

Merz positions himself as the ideal candidate to restore Germany’s economic strength and protect national interests in an uncertain global landscape.

## **Olaf Scholz (SPD) – The Incumbent Chancellor Seeking Re-election

- **Party:** Social Democratic Party (SPD)

- **Profile:** After years in office, Scholz is struggling to overcome political fatigue and convince voters that his economic and social strategies can lead Germany into a new period of stability.

## Main Proposals:

- **Economy:** Increased government intervention and higher taxes on large corporations.

- **Social Policy:** Expansion of welfare programs and promotion of multiculturalism.

- **Energy and Environment:** A full commitment to renewable energy, despite Germany’s current energy supply challenges.

Scholz faces an uphill battle due to economic stagnation and internal tensions within his own coalition, which ultimately led to the dissolution of the previous government.

## **Alice Weidel (AfD) – The Candidate Challenging the Establishment

- **Party:** Alternative for Germany (AfD)

- **Profile:** An economist and leader of a party that has seen significant growth in recent years, Alice Weidel promises a more secure and economically independent Germany.

## Main Proposals:

- **Economy:** A return to more efficient energy policies and incentives for the productive sector.

- **Immigration and Security:** Stricter border controls and policies aimed at preserving Germany’s cultural identity.

- **Foreign Policy:** Less involvement in international conflicts and a focus on Germany’s sovereignty.

The rise of the AfD reflects growing dissatisfaction among Germans with traditional party policies, particularly in areas such as security and the economy.

## **Robert Habeck (Greens) – The Pragmatic Environmentalist

- **Party:** Alliance 90/The Greens

- **Profile:** Habeck took over party leadership after Annalena Baerbock and aims to expand the Greens’ influence in government, despite economic challenges stemming from the energy transition.

## Main Proposals:

- **Energy:** Ending fossil fuel use and fully investing in renewables.

- **Social Policy:** Advocating for multicultural and environmental initiatives.

- **Economy:** Taxing large corporations to fund social and ecological projects.

The party faces difficulties due to rising energy costs and resistance from industrial sectors struggling with the closure of traditional energy sources.

## **Electoral Scenario and Possible Outcomes

The latest polls indicate Friedrich Merz (CDU/CSU) as the frontrunner, with approximately **29.5% of the vote**, followed by Alice Weidel (AfD) with **20.9%**. Olaf Scholz (SPD) and Robert Habeck (Greens) trail behind with **16% and 13%**, respectively.

Given the fragmented political landscape, the next government will likely result from coalition negotiations. Merz has already ruled out an alliance with the AfD, making a “grand coalition” between CDU/CSU, SPD, and possibly the Greens a potential outcome. Meanwhile, Scholz, if he manages a last-minute surge, may attempt to reassemble a coalition similar to the one he led in recent years.

Beyond choosing a leader, this election will determine whether Germany continues with the progressive policies of recent years or shifts toward a more conservative approach focused on economic recovery and sovereignty.

Vote counting will be closely watched, as the results could reshape not only Germany’s future but also the balance of power in Europe.

The 2025 German elections represent a crucial moment for the country. Voters will have to decide between Scholz’s continuity, Merz’s conservative revival, Weidel’s alternative vision, or the Greens’ environmental agenda.

Regardless of the outcome, one thing is certain: the next government will face significant challenges in handling immigration, the economy, energy, and security to ensure Germany’s prosperity in the years ahead.
