In an era marked by rapid technological advancements and growing social awareness, the concept of meritocracy seems to have lost its place in modern discourse. Once celebrated as a cornerstone of progress and fairness, it is now criticized and dismissed by many. Yet, the decline of meritocracy coincides with a rise in societal issues such as institutional inefficiency, corruption, and a lack of accountability. Could the rejection of merit as a guiding principle be one of the reasons for these alarming trends?

In the 21st century, one of the oldest and most fundamental concepts for the advancement of societies—meritocracy—has been systematically attacked and sidelined. In many debates, particularly in political and academic spheres, the term has fallen "out of fashion" and, in some cases, is even rejected as an ideal. Coincidentally, we are living in an era where many atrocities have come to light, such as institutionalized corruption, incompetence in key leadership positions, and the weakening of fundamental ethical values.

This correlation is not merely accidental. The abandonment of meritocracy as an essential value is directly contributing to the creation of weak and unhealthy societies, where privilege and personal favors replace effort and competence.

## **Meritocracy and Human Development

Meritocracy plays a critical role in strengthening individuals' character and personality. It is through personal effort that people build resilience, autonomy, and the ability to face adversity. When this system is replaced by easy privileges—such as parents who give everything to their children or jobs guaranteed through nepotism and favoritism—it creates a generation of adults who are emotionally fragile, lack personality, and are unprepared for life’s challenges.

When these individuals occupy positions for which they are unqualified, they generate more mistakes than results, perpetuating a cycle of mediocrity. The absence of challenges during personal development leads to adults incapable of addressing real-world problems, which weakens not only the individual but society as a whole.

## **The Decline of Meritocracy in the 21st Century

In today’s context, meritocracy has been criticized as an "unfair" system, with arguments favoring its replacement by policies based on group privileges or quotas that disregard individual skills and competencies. While there is merit in addressing historical inequalities, the complete abandonment of personal merit is creating serious consequences.

In this scenario, we witness an increase in atrocities: rampant corruption, incompetence in key roles, and a general sense of societal stagnation. When leaders, professionals, and managers reach their positions not through merit but through connections or privilege, the negative impact is clear: institutions decay, the economy suffers, and public trust erodes.

## **Parents’ Crucial Role in Teaching Merit

This problem often starts at home. Many parents, in an attempt to "protect" their children, avoid imposing challenges or responsibilities. While this approach may seem well-intentioned, it deprives children of essential experiences needed to develop character.

By guaranteeing everything easily—from toys to jobs—parents create unmotivated individuals incapable of creating their own opportunities. A generation raised without effort and merit is more likely to face identity crises, low self-esteem, and difficulties in finding purpose in life.

## **The Societal Impact: Weak Adults, Unhealthy Societies

The abandonment of meritocracy in the 21st century is leading to a sick society. When unqualified individuals occupy strategic positions, the impacts range from deteriorating public services to a loss of economic competitiveness. Furthermore, the weakening of merit discourages genuine talent, often driving capable individuals to seek opportunities elsewhere, contributing to brain drain and societal stagnation.

## **An Urgent Solution

Societies must urgently revisit the importance of meritocracy as a central value. This does not mean ignoring historical inequalities or denying the need for inclusion, but ensuring that effort, competence, and ethics are the primary criteria for distributing opportunities.

Likewise, parents should raise their children with an emphasis on effort and merit, encouraging them to face challenges and learn from failures. These lessons are crucial for forming resilient and capable adults who can contribute positively to society.

The 21st century marks a period of significant technological and social advances, but it also reveals the consequences of abandoning meritocracy. At the same time that atrocities such as corruption, inefficiency, and mediocrity become more evident, rejecting merit as an essential value contributes to the deterioration of societal structures.

If we want to build a strong and healthy society, we must restore meritocracy as a central principle. Valuing effort, competence, and ethics not only promotes collective progress but also strengthens individuals' character and restores social justice. After all, a society that does not reward merit is doomed to reap the bitter fruits of mediocrity and decline.
