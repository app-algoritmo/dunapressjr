## Saladin: The Muslim Hero Who Reconquered Jerusalem (Based on Arab Sources)

Saladin (Salah ad-Din Yusuf ibn Ayyub, 1137–1193) remains one of the most revered figures in Islamic history. A Kurdish military leader who rose to rule Egypt, Syria, and Yemen, he is best known for defeating the Crusaders at the **Battle of Hattin (1187)** and liberating Jerusalem after 88 years of Frankish rule.

This article draws on **Arab chronicles, medieval biographies, and modern research** from Middle Eastern scholars to present a comprehensive account of his life, strategies, and enduring influence.

***

## **Early Life and Rise to Power

### **1. Kurdish Origins and Service Under Nur ad-Din

- Born in **Tikrit (modern Iraq)**, Saladin began his career under Nur ad-Din Zengi, the Sunni ruler of Syria.

- **Primary Source:** Ibn al-Athir’s *"The Complete History"* (13th century) notes his early military training and loyalty to Nur ad-Din’s anti-Crusade campaigns.

- **Access:** [Qatar Digital Library – Ibn al-Athir Manuscripts](https://www.qdl.qa)

### **2. Conquest of Egypt (1169)

- Sent to Egypt to aid the Fatimid Caliphate, Saladin eventually **overthrew the Shi’a regime**, restoring Sunni rule under Nur ad-Din’s authority.

- **Arab Historian Perspective:**

*"Saladin’s consolidation of Egypt was not just a military victory but a political masterstroke,"* writes Dr. Mahmoud Ibrahim (Cairo University). *"He balanced Kurdish troops, Arab elites, and Fatimid bureaucrats."*

***

## **The Unification of Syria and Jihad Against the Crusaders

### **1. Death of Nur ad-Din and Saladin’s Expansion (1174–1186)

- After Nur ad-Din’s death, Saladin seized Damascus and Aleppo, uniting Syria and Egypt under the **Ayyubid Dynasty**.

- **Primary Source:** **Baha ad-Din ibn Shaddad**, Saladin’s personal advisor, documented his campaigns in *"The Rare and Excellent History of Saladin"*.

## Key Quote:

- > *"He never sought wealth for himself, only the unity of Muslims."*

### **2. The Battle of Hattin (1187) and Liberation of Jerusalem

- Saladin’s forces **crushed the Crusader army** at Hattin, capturing King Guy of Lusignan.

- On **October 2, 1187**, he entered Jerusalem, permitting Christians to leave peacefully—a contrast to the Crusaders’ 1099 massacre.

- **Modern Research:**

A 2023 study by the **University of Jordan** analyzed Saladin’s siege tactics using maps from **Al-Idrisi’s 12th-century geography texts**.

- **Link:** [University of Jordan – Medieval Warfare Studies](https://www.ju.edu.jo)

***

## **Saladin’s Leadership and Legacy

### **1. Chivalry and Diplomacy

- Despite his victories, Saladin **respected Richard the Lionheart** during the Third Crusade (1191–1192), even sending him gifts when Richard fell ill.

- **Arab Chronicler Account:**

**Imad ad-Din al-Isfahani**, an eyewitness, wrote:

- > *"He treated prisoners with mercy, earning even his enemies’ admiration."*

### **2. Death and Succession (1193)

- Saladin died in Damascus, leaving a **divided empire** but an **indelible legend**.

- **Mausoleum:** His tomb remains a pilgrimage site in the **Umayyad Mosque complex**.

### **3. Modern Reverence in the Arab World

- **Egyptian historian Dr. Farag Foda** (1946–1992) argued:

*"Saladin’s model of unity against foreign invasion still resonates in Arab political discourse."*

- **Syrian TV Series *"Saladin the Victorious"** (2020) revived his legacy for new generations.

***

Saladin’s life exemplifies **military genius, political acumen, and moral leadership**. His recapture of Jerusalem remains a defining moment in Islamic history, while his **tolerance** set a standard for medieval warfare.
