In Norway, there is an old tradition and superstition involving brooms during Christmas week, especially on Christmas Eve. This practice dates back to ancient times when it was believed that witches and evil spirits were more active during this season.

## **The Tradition and Superstition

According to the superstition, on Christmas Eve, witches and other evil spirits come out to fly and cause mischief. Since brooms were seen as the primary "means of transport" for witches, people feared that they would invade homes to steal brooms for their nocturnal flights.

To prevent this, it became customary to **hide brooms on Christmas Eve**. Many Norwegian families store their brooms in safe places, such as closets or basements, to protect them from these malevolent spirits. Some people even hide other cleaning tools, like mops and dustpans, as part of the same ritual.

## **Other Aspects of the Tradition

In addition to hiding brooms, in some parts of Norway, it was believed that leaving brooms unprotected could attract bad luck or negative energy into the house. Thus, this custom also had a symbolic role, representing the protection and spiritual cleansing of the home.

In earlier times, some families would also perform small protective rituals on Christmas Eve, such as prayers or the use of religious symbols, to ward off any evil presence.

## **How Is This Tradition Viewed Today?

Although the superstition is no longer taken seriously by most modern Norwegians, it is still common to find families who continue the habit of hiding brooms as a way to preserve the tradition and celebrate the Christmas spirit in a unique and fun way. For many, it serves as a cultural connection to the past and a way to keep alive the stories and legends passed down through generations.

This curious superstition adds a touch of mystery and magic to Christmas celebrations in Norway, showing how folklore and traditions can enrich modern festivities.
